**Ceto-Mukhaḥ**
Meaning: "whose mouth is consciousness"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *ceto-mukhaḥ* denotes having consciousness as the doorway to the cognition of dream and waking.

## Definition

The Sanskrit compound *ceto-mukhaḥ* (चेतोमुखः) is composed of two constituent words: *cetas* (चेतस्), meaning mind, intellect, cognition, or awareness, and *mukha* (मुख), meaning mouth, face, entrance, or doorway. Taken literally, the expression translates as "having consciousness as one's mouth" or "whose mouth is understanding". In general philosophical lexicography, *cetomukha* is defined as the "gateway to cognition" and serves as a technical term for the state of deep sleep (*suṣupti*).

In its specific contextual setting within Upaniṣadic philosophy, *ceto-mukhaḥ* functions as a primary epithet of *prājña*—the third quarter (*tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ*) of the Self (*Ātman*) residing in deep sleep. Śaṅkarācārya, in his commentary on the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, establishes a two-fold contextual definition of the term:
1. It signifies that deep sleep (*suṣupti*) acts as the doorway (*mukha*) leading to the specific cognitions (*cetas* or *bodha*) experienced in the subsequent states of dreaming (*svapna*) and waking (*jāgarita*).
2. Alternatively, it indicates that the perceiving entity (*cetaḥ*), characterized by empirical awareness, serves as the entrance or doorway through which dream and waking experiences are entered.

Thus, the contextual definition moves beyond the literal physical image of a "mouth," identifying *ceto-mukhaḥ* as the threshold of manifest awareness.

## Etymology

Grammatically, *ceto-mukhaḥ* (चेतोमुखः) is a compound word (*samāsa*) belonging to the class of *Bahuvrīhi* (possessive or exocentric compounds). It modifies the masculine noun *prājñaḥ* in the nominative singular masculine form.

The members of the compound are:
- *cetas* (चेतस्): A neuter nominal stem derived from the verbal root √*cit* (चित्), meaning to perceive, observe, or know.
- *mukha* (मुख): A neuter nominal stem meaning mouth, entrance, front, or doorway.

During compound formation, internal sandhi rules operate on the juncture of the two stems (*cetas* + *mukha*). Before a voiced consonant such as the labial nasal *m-*, the final *-as* of the neuter stem *cetas* undergoes euphonic change to *-o-*, producing the compound stem *ceto-mukha*.

The traditional analytical expansion (*vigraha-vākya*) of this *Bahuvrīhi* compound is expressed as:
- *cetaḥ mukhaṃ yasya saḥ* ("He whose mouth or doorway is consciousness/cognition").

Śaṅkarācārya formulates the grammatical resolution in his commentary as *cetanā-lakṣaṇaṃ ceto mukham asya iti ceto-mukhaḥ*, designating that entity whose entrance or mouth is characterized by consciousness.

## Ceto-mukhaḥ in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

The expression *ceto-mukhaḥ* occurs in Mantra 5 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, a text belonging to the *Atharva Veda*. The mantra describes the third quarter (*tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ*) of *Ātman*, known as *prājña*, operating in the sphere of deep sleep (*suṣuptasthāna*).

Grammatically, *ceto-mukhaḥ* functions as an adjectival modifier in the nominative singular masculine, aligned with a series of parallel epithets describing *prājña*: *ekībhūtaḥ* (unified/become one), *prajñāna-ghanaḥ* (a mass of undifferentiated consciousness), *ānanda-mayaḥ* (full of bliss), and *ānanda-bhuk* (the enjoyer of bliss).

The surrounding text of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* sets up a four-fold framework (*catur-pāda*) mapping the states of consciousness onto the sacred syllable *AUM*:
- Mantra 3 describes *vaiśvānara*, the first quarter corresponding to the waking state (*jāgarita-sthāna*), which is outward-cognizing (*bahiṣ-prajña*) and experiences gross objects (*sthūla-bhuk*).
- Mantra 4 describes *taijasa*, the second quarter corresponding to the dream state (*svapna-sthāna*), which is inward-cognizing (*antaḥ-prajña*) and experiences subtle objects (*pravivikta-bhuk*).
- Mantra 5 introduces *prājña* in deep sleep (*suṣupti*), where dualistic projections are re-absorbed into an undifferentiated seed state (*ekībhūta*). Here, *ceto-mukhaḥ* articulates the causal gateway aspect of *prājña*: deep sleep is the latent precursor and doorway through which conscious experience emerges into dream and waking, and into which those experiences recede.
- Mantra 6 designates *prājña* as the Lord of all (*sarveśvara*), the all-knowing (*sarvajña*), the inner controller (*antar-yāmī*), and the origin and dissolution of all beings (*yoniḥ sarvasya*).

Textual and Exegetical Distinctions:
- **Upaniṣad Wording vs. Gauḍapāda's Kārikā**: While the Upaniṣad text directly designates *prājña* as *ceto-mukhaḥ*, Gauḍapāda in his *Kārikā* (I.1–I.15) analyzes the functional limits of *prājña*. Gauḍapāda notes that *prājña* is bound by cause (*kāraṇa-baddha*) because it is characterized by the non-apprehension of reality (*tattva-agrahaṇa*), whereas *turīya* (the Fourth) is free from both cause (non-apprehension) and effect (misapprehension).
- **Śaṅkara's Commentary**: Śaṅkara integrates *ceto-mukhaḥ* into Advaita metaphysics, explaining that although *prājña* in deep sleep experiences no object manifold, consciousness (*cetas*) remains intact as the underlying doorway (*mukha*) for empirical manifestations upon waking.

## Primary Texts and Commentaries

The primary textual locator for *ceto-mukhaḥ* is Mantra 5 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*:

> **Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, Mantra 5**:
> *yatra supto na kaṃcana kāmaṃ kāmayate na kaṃcana svapnaṃ paśyati tat suṣuptam / suṣuptasthāna ekībhūtaḥ prajñānaghana evānandamayo hyānandabhuk cetomukhaḥ prājñas tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ // 5 //*

Key commentaries and treatises explicating the expression include:
- **Śaṅkarācārya's *Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya*** (on Mantra 5): Expounds *ceto-mukhaḥ* both as the doorway to waking and dream cognitions (*jāgrat-svapna-bodha-prati-dvāra-bhūtatvāt*) and as the state whose entrance is characterized by consciousness (*cetana-lakṣaṇaṃ ceto mukham asya*).
- **Ānandagiri's *Ṭīkā*** on Śaṅkara's commentary: Clarifies Śaṅkara's gloss by explaining how the unified causal state (*kāraṇa-rūpa*) of deep sleep functions as the indispensable antecedent to empirical experience.
- **Sadānanda's *Vedāntasāra*** (§ 38, § 115): Synthesizes *Māṇḍūkya* terminology, identifying *prājña* in the individual micro-level and *īśvara* at the macro-level as associated with causal ignorance (*ajñāna*) and acting as the illuminator of the unmanifest state.

## Interpretations and Disputes

Differences in translation, grammatical analysis, and philosophical interpretation of *ceto-mukhaḥ* are documented across classical and modern sources:

1. **Literal Lexicographical Renderings**: Early European Sanskritists and dictionaries (such as Macdonell) translated the compound literally based on its components as "whose mouth is understanding" or "having mind as mouth".
2. **Philosophical Dictionary Renderings**: John Grimes (*A Concise Dictionary of Indian Philosophy*) renders *Cetomukha* as "gateway to cognition," emphasizing its technical usage as a name for deep sleep (*suṣupti*).
3. **Śaṅkarācārya's Dual Exegesis**: Śaṅkara records two complementary interpretations in his commentary:
   - *Deep sleep as doorway*: *Suṣupti* is *ceto-mukhaḥ* because it serves as the doorway (*mukha*) through which consciousness passes into waking and dream cognitions (*cetas*).
   - *Consciousness as entrance*: Empirical awareness (*cetas*) acts as the entryway through which the unmanifest consciousness of deep sleep reconnects with external objects.
4. **Distinction Between Prājña and Turīya**: A central interpretive issue in Advaita Vedānta is distinguishing *prājña* (which is *ceto-mukhaḥ*) from *turīya*. While *prājña* is *ceto-mukhaḥ* and *prajñāna-ghanaḥ* within the causal realm of deep sleep (*suṣupti*), it remains enveloped by causal ignorance (*bīja-nidrā*). By contrast, *turīya* (the Fourth) is non-dual Pure Consciousness, transcending all causal doorways and free from both non-apprehension and misapprehension.
