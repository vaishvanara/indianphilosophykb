**Ahiṃsā**

Meaning: “non-injury; non-violence (from the verb root hims = “to injure” + the prefix a = “not”)”
Definition: It is the first and most important of the abstentions (Yama) of the Yoga System. It is the cardinal virtue upon which all others depend. It is the law of compassion in body, mind, and spirit.

Reference:
Grimes, John (1996). A Concise Dictionary of Indian Philosophy: Sanskrit Terms Defined in English. Albany: State University of New York Press

---

Ahiṃsā is the foremost and most important yama, universally translated as non-violence, non-injury, or non-harming. Rooted in the Sanskrit prefix a- (non) and hiṃsā (to injure or kill), it dictates that a yogi should not cause pain, suffering, or harm to any living creature—in thought, word, or deed. Far beyond physical non-violence, true ahiṃsā demands a radical transformation of inner consciousness, eliminating malice, anger, harsh speech, and judgmental thoughts toward others and oneself. In the yoga tradition, ahiṃsā is grounded in the metaphysical realization of the unity of all life; harming another is ultimately harming the universal Self (Ātman) that resides within all beings. Patañjali notes that when a person is deeply established in ahiṃsā, hostility ceases in their presence, meaning even natural enemies (like predators and prey) live in peace around them. It is the active practice of universal love, compassion, and radical reverence for life, transforming the practitioner into a sanctuary of peace in a violent world.
