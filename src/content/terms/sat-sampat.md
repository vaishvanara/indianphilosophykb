# Ṣaṭ-Sampat (षट्सम्पत्)

## The Six Virtues of Mental Discipline in Advaita Vedānta

In the non-dual tradition of **Advaita Vedānta**, spiritual liberation (*Mokṣa*) is not achieved through mere ritual performance or speculative intellect, but through direct Self-knowledge (*Jñāna*). However, raw intellectual capacity alone is insufficient to assimilate non-dual truth; the mind must first undergo a rigorous process of ethical and psychological conditioning.

To formalize this preparation, Adi Shankara outlined the **Sādhana-Catuṣṭaya**—the "Fourfold Qualifications of an Eligible Seeker" (*Adhikārī*). The third requirement within this framework is **Ṣaṭ-sampat** (Sanskrit: षट्सम्पत्), literally translating to the **"Sixfold Treasures"** or **"Six Mental Virtues."**

Composed of *Ṣaṭ* (six) and *Sampat* (wealth, assets, or virtues), Ṣaṭ-sampat provides a complete methodology for emotional regulation, attentional stability, and cognitive resilience.

---

## The Six Fold Treasures

The six virtues function as an integrated psychological sequence. Each discipline builds upon the foundation established by the preceding ones, systematically shifting the mind from external distraction to internal tranquility.

### 1. Śama (शम) — Inner Tranquility / Mental Control

* **Definition:** The deliberate quelling of emotional reactivity, baseline anxiety, and compulsive thought patterns at the level of the mind (*Manas*).
* **Cognitive Function:** *Śama* is top-down emotional regulation. Instead of reacting reflexively to sensory triggers or internal desires (*Kāma*), the practitioner cultivates a calm, neutral internal state. It is the mastery of the mind from within.

### 2. Dama (दम) — Sensory Restraint

* **Definition:** The control and mastery over the ten sensory and motor organs (*Indriyas*—five organs of perception and five organs of action).
* **Cognitive Function:** While *Śama* works internally on thought-impulses, *Dama* operates at the sensory gateway. It prevents the physical senses from impulsively pursuing external objects. *Śama* and *Dama* function together as internal brake systems: *Śama* quiets the driver (the mind), while *Dama* reins in the horses (the senses).

### 3. Uparati (उपरति) — Withdrawal / Cessation of Duties

* **Definition:** The natural turning away from worldly entanglements, excessive social obligations, and mechanical sensory seeking.
* **Cognitive Function:** *Uparati* is the state of cognitive quietude where the mind no longer hungers for constant external stimulation. It includes **Svadharma-anuṣṭhānam**—strict adherence to one's authentic duties without taking on extraneous, stress-inducing commitments that scatter mental energy.

### 4. Titikṣā (तितिक्षा) — Fortitude / Forbearance

* **Definition:** The capacity to endure physical discomforts, emotional friction, and dualities of nature (heat and cold, pleasure and pain, praise and blame) without seeking immediate escape or venting grief.
* **Cognitive Function:** *Titikṣā* is psychological stress resilience. It is not stoic suppression or passive suffering, but an unshakeable poise that recognizes physical and emotional discomforts as transient, superficial phenomena that do not alter core awareness.

### 5. Śraddhā (श्रद्धा) — Informed Faith / Intellectual Trust

* **Definition:** Unwavering trust and commitment to the teachings of the scriptures (*Śāstra*) and the words of the teacher (*Guru*), validated through one's own reasoning and experience.
* **Cognitive Function:** Unlike blind belief or dogma, *Śraddhā* in Vedānta is an open, receptive, and active hypothesis. It provides the initial cognitive trust required to deeply contemplate subtle metaphysical truths before direct realization occurs.

### 6. Samādhāna (समाधान) — Deep Mental Integration / Single-Pointed Focus

* **Definition:** The continuous, unwavering focus of the intellect (*Buddhi*) on the ultimate reality (*Brahman* or *Atman*).
* **Cognitive Function:** *Samādhāna* is the culmination of the previous five virtues. With the mind quieted (*Śama*), senses restrained (*Dama*), worldly noise reduced (*Uparati*), friction endured (*Titikṣā*), and direction clarified (*ŚRaddhā*), attentional bandwidth is fully consolidated on Self-inquiry (*Ātma-vicāra*).

## Ṣaṭ-sampat in the Context of Sādhana-Catuṣṭaya

To see how *Ṣaṭ-sampat* fits into the complete Vedāntic preparatory framework, consider its position among the four qualifications:

| Qualification | Sanskrit Term | Core Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Discrimination** | *Viveka* | Discerning between the Eternal (*Nitya*) and the Transient (*Anitya*). |
| **2. Dispassion** | *Vairāgya* | Renunciation of desire for temporary pleasures in this world and the next. |
| **3. Six Virtues** | **Ṣaṭ-sampat** | **Systematic mental discipline, emotional regulation, and attentional control.** |
| **4. Longing for Freedom** | *Mumukṣutva* | An intense, burning desire for total spiritual liberation. |

## Summary

The **Ṣaṭ-sampat** model highlights a fundamental principle of classical Indian psychology: higher philosophical inquiry requires a refined, stable mental vessel. By systematically cultivating tranquility (*Śama*), sensory discipline (*Dama*), quietude (*Uparati*), endurance (*Titikṣā*), cognitive trust (*Śraddhā*), and unified focus (*Samādhāna*), a seeker transforms the mind from a turbulent distraction into a precise instrument for non-dual realization.
