Meaning: "relative pronoun ('who', 'which', 'what'); first semivowel of Sanskrit; (in prosody) a bacchic foot; (lexical) a goer, wind, or joining."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verses 9, 10, 11, 12)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In Sanskrit, *ya* (य) functions primarily as the relative pronoun, translated as "who," "that," "which," or "what". In standard sentence construction, it introduces a relative clause and is paired with a corresponding demonstrative correlative pronoun in the main clause, most frequently *tad* or less commonly *idam*, *etad*, *adas*, *īdṛś*, or *tādṛśa*. 

When doubled (e.g., *ya ya*) or combined with indefinite pronouns and particles (such as *kaś cit*, *kaś ca*, *kaś cana*, or *api*), its meaning is generalized to "whoever," "whichever," "whatever," or "any soever". In addition, the neuter singular form *yad* is used idiomatically as a conjunction or adverbial marker meaning "as for," "as regards," "that is to say," or "because". 

Lexicographically and phonetically, *ya* also designates the first semivowel of the Sanskrit alphabet, corresponding to the vowels *i* and *ī*. Specialized traditional lexicons list additional nominal definitions for *ya*, including a mover or goer, wind, joining, restraint, fame, light, or a carriage.

---

## Etymology

*Ya* is classified as a relative pronoun and serves as the actual pronominal stem used in declension. Unlike standard Sanskrit nouns, personal and relative pronouns do not possess a single uninflected crude base applicable across all case inflections. The masculine nominative singular is *yaḥ* (*yas*), the feminine nominative singular is *yā*, and the neuter nominative and accusative singular is *yat* or *yad*. Comparative philology notes that the Sanskrit relative pronoun *yaḥ* / *yad* corresponds to the Greek relative pronoun ὅς, ἥ, with Sanskrit *y* corresponding to the spiritus asper in Greek.
