**Yatra** 
Meaning: "what"
From the [Mandukya Upanishad](mandukyopanisat.md) (Verse 5)

Translation by Swami Sharvananda
Sanskrit: यत्र 
Transliteration: Yat  
Meaning: "where"

Reference:
Sharvananda, Swami (1920). Mundaka and Mandukya Upanishads: With Sanskrit Text; Paraphrase with Word-For-Word Literal Translation, English, Rendering and Comments. Mylapore, Madras: Sri Ramakrishna Math

--------------------
According to the search results, the Sanskrit word यत्र (yátra) means "where" or "in which place".
Key points about the meaning of यत्र:

- It is an adverb in Sanskrit, derived from the root य- (ya-) and the suffix -त्र (-tra).
- यत्र literally translates to "where" or "in which place".
- It can also mean "on which occasion" or "in which case".

The word is cognate with Avestan 𐬫𐬀𐬚𐬭𐬀 (yaθra), and is inherited from Proto-Indo-Iranian *Hyátra and Proto-Indo-European *Hyótro.

In Hindi, यत्र can be translated as "जिस जगह" or "जहाँ", both meaning "where".

So in summary, यत्र is a Sanskrit adverb that denotes a place, occasion or case, and is most accurately translated as "where" or "in which place". It is a common word used in Sanskrit and derived from earlier Proto-Indo-Iranian and Proto-Indo-European roots.
