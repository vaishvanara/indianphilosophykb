Meaning: "thus", "so", "in this manner"; an indeclinable particle marking quotations, direct speech, text conclusions, or word references.

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verses 1, 8)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In classical and Vedic Sanskrit, *iti* (इति) is an indeclinable particle (*avyaya* / *nipāta*) that literally signifies "thus", "so", "in this manner", or "in this wise". It serves primarily as a demonstrative adverb of manner that points to a preceding or following statement, thought, phrase, or condition.

The term fulfills several key functions across Sanskrit literature and philosophy:

1. **Quotation and direct speech marker**: *iti* functions as the standard Sanskrit equivalent of quotation marks or inverted commas. Placed typically at the end of a direct citation (*oratio recta*), speech, or internal reflection, it signifies that the preceding words represent the exact expressions spoken, written, or thought (e.g., *iti uktvā*, "having so said"; *iti kṛtvā*, "having so considered").
2. **Textual and formal conclusion**: Positioned at the close of a book, chapter, section, or sacred recitation, *iti* signals its completion or formal conclusion.
3. **Linguistic and philosophical metalanguage**: In Sanskrit grammar (such as Paninian rules) and philosophical systems (including Mīmāṃsā and Vedānta), appending *iti* to a word indicates that the expression refers to its own phonetic form (*svarūpa*) or its conceptual referent rather than functioning as an active syntactic component. It is also used to aggregate a list of enumerated items under a unified concept or category.
4. **Compound formations**: *iti* enters into several standard nominal compounds, including:
   - *Itihāsa* (*iti-ha-āsa*, "so indeed it was"): traditional history, legend, or epic narrative (specifically designating works such as the *Rāmāyaṇa* and *Mahābhārata*).
   - *Itikartavyatā* (*iti-kartavya-tā*, "thus-it-must-be-done-ness"): proper obligation, standard procedure, or duty.
   - *Ityādi* (*iti* + *ādi*, "beginning thus"): "and so forth", "et cetera".

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *iti* is classified as an indeclinable particle (*nipāta* or *avyaya*) and an adverb (*avyayībhāva* / *gati* element). Etymologically, it is derived from the pronominal stem or base *i* (a demonstrative base indicating proximity) combined with the adverbial suffix *-ti*, forming a demonstrative adverb of manner meaning "in this manner" or "thus". 

Because it is an indeclinable, *iti* is uninflected and does not undergo declension for case, number, or gender. Grammarians distinguish this indeclinable particle from the homonymous nominal base *iti* (a feminine noun derived from the verbal root *i*, "to go", meaning "going" or "approach"). In derivative compounds like *itihāsa*, the term is formed by combining the particle *iti* with the emphatic particle *ha* and the past tense verb *āsa* ("was").
