**Tad** 
Meaning: "he, she, it, that, this"
From the [Mandukya Upanishad](mandukyopanisat.md) (Verse 5)

Reference:
Monier-Williams, Monier (1899). A Sanskrit-English Dictionary: Etymologically and Philologically Arranged with Special Reference to Cognate Indo-European languages. Oxford: Clarendon Press 

-----------------------
तद् (Tad) is a Sanskrit word that is often translated to "that" or "it". However, its meaning is more complex and nuanced than a simple translation.

तद् can be used as a pronoun to refer to a person, place, thing, or idea that is previously mentioned or implied. It can also be used to refer to a general or abstract concept, such as "that which is" or "that which exists".

In Sanskrit grammar, तद् is known as a "pronoun of reference" or "pronoun of antecedence", because it refers back to a previously mentioned word or concept. It is often used to connect ideas or to indicate a relationship between different concepts.

In philosophical and spiritual contexts, तद् is often used to refer to the ultimate reality, the Self, or the Absolute. For example, the Upanishads often use तद् to refer to the ultimate reality that underlies all existence.

In a more literal sense, तद् can be used to refer to a specific thing or object, such as "that book" or "that tree". However, its meaning is often more abstract and nuanced than a simple literal translation.
