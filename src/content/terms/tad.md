Meaning: "that, he, she, it, this, those; therefore, thereupon, in that case, then".

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In standard Sanskrit lexicography and grammar, *tad* (तद्) functions primarily as the demonstrative and third-person pronoun meaning "that," "he," "she," "it," or "they/those". As a demonstrative pronoun, it specifically points to an entity or person that is absent, distant, or previously mentioned. It serves universally as the correlative to the relative pronoun *yad* ("who," "which," "whatever") in complex sentence structures. In its neuter form or when used adverbially, *tad* carries several common particle and connective meanings, including "there," "thither," "then," "in that case" (as a correlative to *yadi* or *ced*), "therefore" or "consequently" (as a correlative to *yad*, *yataḥ*, or *yena*), and "now" as a transitional narrative marker. When repeated (*tad tad* or *tat tat*), it expresses distributive or diverse meanings such as "this and that," "each," "various," or "respective".

In Indian philosophical literature, particularly within Vedānta, *tat* ("that") acquires a profound extended meaning as the designation for the ultimate, unmanifest Supreme Reality or *Brahman*. This usage is centrally instantiated in the sacred Vedantic formula *tat tvam asi* ("That thou art"), wherein *tat* denotes the invisible, undifferentiated Absolute (*Brahman*) as identical with the individual self (*tvam*). Furthermore, in Sanskrit philosophy of language and grammar, thinkers note that because the pronoun *tad* can refer to any object in the world depending on the speaker's intention (*vivakṣā*), it possesses a unified general denotation delimited by contextual application rather than an infinite multiplicity of distinct meanings.

## Etymology

Grammatically, *tad* belongs to the class of pronominal stems (*sarvanāman* / *sarvādi*). Unlike standard nominals derived from verbal roots (*dhātu*), Sanskrit personal and demonstrative pronouns do not possess a crude verbal root derivation in traditional grammatical analysis; their primary derivation from a verbal root remains unestablished, functioning instead directly as primitive pronominal bases. 

The neuter nominative and accusative singular form *tad* (or *tat*) functions as the uninflected base used in compound formations. The inflected stem for oblique cases is *ta-*, while the nominative singular exhibits suppletive masculine and feminine forms: *sas* / *sa* (सः / स) for the masculine and *sā* (सा) for the feminine. In sentence combination, the nominative singular masculine *sa* drops its final *-s* before all consonants.

The base *tad-* / *ta-* forms the constituent basis for numerous technical compound terms and secondary derivatives (*taddhita*):

* *Tatpuruṣa* (तत्पुरुष): "his man" (*tasya puruṣaḥ*), serving as the classic exemplar and technical designation for determinative noun compounds.
* *Taddhita* (तद्धित): "good for that/him" (*tat-hita*), the technical term for secondary nominal suffixes and words derived through them.
* *Tattva* (तत्त्व): "that-ness," denoting essential truth, reality, or fundamental principle.
* *Tādātmya* (तादात्म्य): "identity of nature" or "essential identity," formed from *tad-ātman*.
* *Tādrūpya* (ताद्रूप्य): "identity of form" or "truth".
* *Tādṛś* (तादृश्) / Tādṛśa (तादृश): "such" or "of such a kind," formed with the suffix *-dṛś* / *-dṛśa*.
* *Tāvat* (तावत्): "so much" or "so many".

Additionally, adverbial derivatives formed from the base *ta-* include *tatas* ("thence", "thereupon", formed with the ablative suffix *-tas*), *tatra* ("there", "in that place", formed with *-tra*), *tadā* ("then", "at that time"), *tarhi* ("at that time", "then"), and the adverbial ablative singular *tasmāt* ("therefore", "on that account").
