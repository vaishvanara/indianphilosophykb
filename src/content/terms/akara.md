Meaning: "the letter or sound 'A'; the first constituent mora (*mātrā*) of the sacred syllable AUM, symbolizing the waking state (*jāgrat*) and the gross realm."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 8, Verse 9)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

*Akāra* (Sanskrit: अकार) denotes the first letter, vowel, or phoneme "A" in the Sanskrit alphabet. In ordinary linguistic and lexical usage, the suffix *-kāra* is appended to a speech sound or letter to designate its phonetic form, giving *akāra* the literal meaning "the letter or sound 'a'." 

Beyond its primary phonological definition, *akāra* possesses a foundational philosophical significance in Vedantic literature, particularly within the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* and related texts. In this extended context, *akāra* designates the first of the three constituent sound-measures (*mātrā*) that compose the sacred monosyllable *AUM* (or *OM*). Symbolically, it corresponds to the waking state (*jāgrat*), the waking self (*Vaiśvānara* or *Viśva*), and the gross physical realm (*Virāṭ*), serving as the initial phonetic and metaphysical anchor of manifested existence.

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *akāra* is a Sanskrit masculine noun formed by combining the short vowel *a* (अ) with the grammatical suffix *-kāra* (कार). The suffix *-kāra* is derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *kṛ* (कृ), meaning "to make," "to do," or "to produce." 

In Sanskrit grammatical tradition, *-kāra* is attached to phonemes to mean "the sound or letter produced," functioning as a designation for specific speech sounds without altering their basic phonetic value. Thus, *akāra* literally signifies "the utterance of 'a'" or "the letter 'a'." As a phoneme, *a* is traditionally classified as the primary, uncreated sound from which all articulate speech arises.

## Akāra in Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verses 8–9) and its accompanying commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā* and Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*), *akāra* plays a central role in equating the four quarters (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*) with the four constituent parts of the sacred syllable *AUM*.

### Metaphysical and symbolic identification

The Upaniṣad identifies *akāra*, the first letter of *AUM*, with *Vaiśvānara* (or *Viśva*), the first quarter of the Self. *Vaiśvānara* is the consciousness operating in the waking state (*jāgrat-sthāna*), conscious of external objects (*bahiṣ-prajña*), endowed with seven limbs and nineteen mouths, and experiencing the gross physical universe (*sthūlabhug*).

### Grounds of equivalence (*Sāmānya*)

The *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* establishes the correspondence between *akāra* and *Vaiśvānara* on two explicit shared features:

1. ***Āpti* (all-pervasiveness or obtaining):** Just as the sound *a* pervades all speech (being the fundamental vowel inherent in and underlying every articulate sound) so too *Vaiśvānara* pervades the entire waking cosmos.
2. ***Ādimattva* (priority or precedence):** Just as *akāra* is the initial letter of the alphabet and the first sound-measure of *AUM*, *Vaiśvānara* is the first quarter of the Self through which empirical experience begins.

### Meditative role and spiritual fruit

The Upaniṣad states that he who knows and meditates on *akāra* as *Vaiśvānara* "obtains all desires and becomes first among the great." In the Advaitic contemplative process (*laya-cintana* or *Om-upāsanā*), detailed further in texts such as the *Rāma Gītā*, the aspirant uses *akāra* to collect and resolve the gross physical universe (*Virāṭ*) and individual waker consciousness (*Viśva*). *Akāra* is then merged into *ukāra* (the dream state / *Taijasa* / *Hiraṇyagarbha*), *ukāra* into *makāra* (deep sleep / *Prājña* / *Īśvara*), and finally into the soundless fourth state (*amātrā* / *Turīya*), realizing the non-dual Absolute.

### Parallel scriptural attestations 

The primordial status of *akāra* is reiterated in the *Bhagavad Gītā* (10.33), where Kṛṣṇa declares, "Of letters, I am the letter A" (*akṣarāṇām akāro'smi*), affirming its position as the foundational, self-luminous sound of creation.
