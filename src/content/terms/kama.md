Meaning: "desire" or object of desire, whose total absence defines the state of deep sleep (*suṣupti*) and *Prājña*.

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5, 9)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

Lexically, *kāma* (Sanskrit: काम) denotes desire, wish, longing, affection, or the desired object itself. In standard Sanskrit literature and Indian philosophy, it refers broadly to psychological desire or inclination, as well as to sensual pleasure and love. In the traditional fourfold scheme of human goals (*puruṣārtha*), *kāma* represents the hedonistic or aesthetic value of life alongside *dharma* (duty/ethics), *artha* (wealth/material prosperity), and *mokṣa* (liberation).

In the specific textual setting of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 5), *kāma* appears in the accusative singular form *kāmam* within the foundational definition of the third quarter (*tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ*) of *Ātman*, known as *Prājña* presiding over deep sleep (*suṣuptasthāna*):

> *yatra supto na kaṃcana kāmaṃ kāmayate na kaṃcana svapnaṃ paśyati tat suṣuptam...*
> ("That is deep sleep wherein the sleeper desires no desire and sees no dream...")

Contextually, *kāma* functions in both an internal sense (the mental impulse or volition of longing) and an objective sense (the external or mental objects of desire, *kāmyamāna-viṣaya*). In the waking (*jāgarita*) and dream (*svapna*) states, consciousness is active and divided through subject-object duality, driven by desires for gross and subtle objects. By contrast, in deep sleep (*suṣupti*), all dualistic mental projections and desires cease and are re-absorbed into a unified, non-different mass of consciousness (*prajñāna-ghana*). Thus, the complete absence of *kāma* serves as the defining characteristic of deep sleep, distinguishing the causal seed state (*Prājña*) from empirical agitation.

## Etymology

Grammatically, *kāma* is a masculine nominal stem derived from the primary Sanskrit verbal root √*kam* (कम्), belonging to the first and tenth conjugational classes, meaning "to desire," "to wish," "to love," or "to be enamored."

The word formation involves:

- The verbal root √*kam* (कम्) + the primary nominalizing suffix *-a* (*Ghañ-pratyaya* or *a-pratyaya*).
- Under the influence of the *Ghañ* suffix, internal vowel gradation (*vṛddhi*) takes place, lengthening the medial short vowel *-a-* of the root √*kam* to *-ā-*, yielding the nominal base *kāma-* ("desire" or "object of desire").

In verse 5 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *kāma* occurs in the accusative singular masculine form *kāmam* (Sanskrit: कामम्), functioning as the cognate accusative direct object of the verb *kāmayate* (the third person singular present middle form of the derivative/causative stem *kāmaya-* derived from √*kam*). The phrasing *kaṃcana kāmaṃ kāmayate* literally translates as "desires any desire," expressing complete freedom from desire in deep sleep.
