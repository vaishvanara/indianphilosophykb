Meaning: "on account of pervasiveness or obtaining; the ablative form of *āpti* used in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad to explain the symbol *akāra* ('A')."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 9)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In Sanskrit grammar and lexicography, *apteraḥ* (occurring in original text as *āpteḥ*, or in sandhi as *āpter-*) is the ablative singular case form of the feminine noun *āpti* (Sanskrit: आप्ति), derived from the root *āp* ("to reach," "to obtain," "to pervade"). Lexically, *āpti* denotes attainment, pervasiveness, reaching, or complete covering. The inflected ablative form translates literally as "from pervasiveness," "on account of obtaining," or "due to all-pervading nature."

In the ninth verse of the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, the term appears within the phrase *prathamā mātrā 'pter ādimattvād vā* ("the first sound-measure of AUM, on account of pervasiveness or priority"). Here, it serves as the first of two explicit grounds of correspondence (*sāmānya*) between *akāra* (the first constituent letter 'A' of *AUM*) and *Vaiśvānara* (the Self in the waking state, *jāgrat*).

In Śaṅkarācārya's commentary (*bhāṣya*), *āpti* ("pervasiveness" or "obtaining") is interpreted in a dual sense:

1. **Pervasiveness (*vyāpti*):** Just as the sound *a* pervades all articulate speech (as declared in Vedic scripture, *akāro vai sarvā vāk*), so *Vaiśvānara* pervades the entire physical universe.
2. **Obtaining / encompassing (*āptatva*):** Just as the sound *a* encompasses or precedes all subsequent letters in the alphabet, *Vaiśvānara* encompasses all empirical experience.

Furthermore, the Upaniṣad establishes an exegesis connecting the cause *āpti* ("pervasiveness") with the spiritual fruit (*phala*) gained through meditation: he who contemplates *akāra* as *Vaiśvānara* on the ground of *āpti* "obtains (*āpnoti*) indeed all desires."

## Etymology

Etymologically, *apteraḥ* is a grammatically inflected form parsed as follows:

1. **Base noun:** *Āpti* (आप्ति), a feminine abstract noun derived from the primary Sanskrit verbal root *āp* (आप्), meaning "to reach," "to obtain," "to gain," or "to pervade."
2. **Suffixation:** The noun *āpti* is formed by appending the primary nominal suffix *-ti* (ति) to the root *āp* (*āp* + *ti* = *āpti*), expressing the act, state, or quality of reaching or pervading.
3. **Case inflection:** In the ablative singular case, *āpti* inflects as *āpteḥ* (आप्तेः), functioning causally to mean "on account of pervasiveness."
4. **Sandhi modification:** In the text of the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad (verse 9), when *āpteḥ* is immediately followed by the initial vowel of *ādimattvād*, the final visarga (*-ḥ*) undergoes regular phonetic transformation into *-r-*, yielding the compound reading *mātrā'pter-ādimattvād* (मात्राऽप्तेरादिमत्त्वाद्), from which the variant transliteration *apteraḥ* / *āpteraḥ* is derived.
