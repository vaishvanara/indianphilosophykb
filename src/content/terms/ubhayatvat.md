Meaning: "on account of being the middle between the two," "on account of bothness," "because of being perceived in both states," or "owing to two-foldness."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 10)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

Literally, *ubhayatvāt* (Sanskrit: उभयत्वात्) signifies "on account of bothness," "because of two-foldness," or "owing to validity/presence in both cases". In ordinary Sanskrit lexical usage, it functions as a causal adverbial expression indicating that a given property or condition applies equally to two entities or states.

In Advaita Vedānta philosophy, specifically within Gauḍapāda’s *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā* (Book II, *Vaitathya Prakaraṇa*, Verse 5) and Śaṅkarācārya’s commentary on the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the term serves as a crucial logical reason (*hetu*) in demonstrating the unreal or illusory nature (*vaitathya*) of the waking state (*jāgrat-sthāna*). 

Gauḍapāda establishes that just as objects perceived in a dream are unreal due to being confined within the body, objects perceived in the waking state are likewise unreal. The word *ubhayatvāt* is invoked to assert that both waking and dream experiences share the identical structural feature of being cognized through subject-object differentiation (*bhedānām utpannatvāt*). Because this attribute of being perceived within dualistic mental cognition is common to both states, the wise declare the waking state to be equal (*samaṃ smṛtam*) to the dream state in its ultimate unreality.

---

## Etymology

The Sanskrit term *ubhayatvāt* is derived from the Sanskrit pronominal adjective *ubhaya* (उभय), meaning "both," "two-fold," or "belonging to both sets," which originates from the Vedic and Sanskrit dual stem *ubha* ("both"). 

Appending the abstract neuter nominal suffix *-tva* (-त्व) forms the abstract noun stem *ubhayatva* (उभयत्व), signifying "bothness," "two-foldness," or "the state of being in both." Inflected in the ablative singular case (*-āt*, -ात्) of a neuter *-a* stem noun, it serves as the ablative singular of a neuter abstract noun used adverbially in philosophical dialectics. Syntactically, it functions as an adverbial clause of cause, indicating "on account of," "because of," or "owing to."
