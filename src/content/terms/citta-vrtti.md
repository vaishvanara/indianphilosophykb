# Citta-Vṛtti

## Classical Philosophical Framework of Citta-Vritti in Patanjali’s Yoga Sutras

### 1. Textual Authority, Invocation, and Canonical Foundation

The canonical authority of Sage Patanjali as the foundational codifier of Classical Yoga is preserved through the traditional Sanskrit invocation (_Patañjali Śloka_). According to ancient Indian tradition, Patanjali was a purified sage who provided remedies for the threefold impurities of human existence: body, speech, and mind.

योगेन चित्तस्य पदेन वाचां । मलं शरीरस्य च वैद्यकेन ॥ योऽपाकरोत्तं प्रवरं मुनीनां । पतञ्जलिं प्राञ्जलिरानतोऽस्मि ॥ (_yogena cittasya padena vācāṃ | malaṃ śarīrasya ca vaidyakena || yo'pākarottaṃ pravaraṃ munīnāṃ | patañjaliṃ prāñjalirānato'smi ||_)

"I respectfully bow down with folded hands and offer my salutations to Sage Patanjali, the highest among the sages, who has presented the remedies for removing the impurities of the body through his treatise on Ayurveda, of language through his treatise on grammar (_Patañjala Mahābhāṣya_), and of the mind field (_Citta_) through his treatise on Yoga (_Yoga Sūtras_)."

Traditional heritage outlines Patanjali’s classical contributions to human cultivation across three distinct disciplines:

- **Purification of the Body (**_**Āyurveda**_**)**: Formulated medical knowledge to remove physical impurities and diseases, maintaining health and vital energy (_Āyus_).
- **Purification of Speech (**_**Grammar / Mahābhāṣya**_**)**: Established grammatical principles and linguistic purity to ensure correct words (_pada_) and precise expression.
- **Purification of the Mind (**_**Yoga Sūtras**_**)**: Systematized the science of Yoga to arrest mental fluctuations (_citta-vṛtti_) and maintain lasting spiritual serenity.

The opening of the text—_Sutra 1.1: Atha yogānuśāsanam_—establishes its sacred authority. The initial word _**Atha**_ signifies an auspicious, authoritative commencement (_adhikāra_), drawing immediate, focused attention to the profound subject. The term _**anuśāsana**_ denotes an exposition, discipline, or sacred code of living (_śāsana_). The prefix _**anu**_ confirms that Patanjali was not inventing Yoga anew; rather, he was collecting, systematizing, and continuing an ancient oral tradition of wisdom passed down through the sages across the Vedas and Upanishads.

### 2. Definition and Nature of Citta, Vritti, and Nirodha (Sutras 1.1–1.4)

In Samkhya-Yoga metaphysics, the internal psychological organ (_antaḥkaraṇa_) is termed the _**Citta**_. It comprises three interrelated functional aspects that sprout from the core self:

1. **Mind (**_**Manas**_**)**: The outer sensory processing organ and lower thinking faculty.
2. **Intelligence (**_**Buddhi**_**)**: The higher intellect, responsible for decision-making, reasoning, and spiritual discrimination.
3. **Consciousness / Ego (**_**Ahaṁkāra**_**)**: The self-identity mechanism or "I-maker."

The _Citta_ is governed and constituted by the three _Guṇas_ (qualities of primordial nature): _Sattva_ (purity, clarity, equilibrium), _Rajas_ (action, passion, agitation), and _Tamas_ (inertia, darkness, heavy obscurity).

The fundamental definition of Yoga is set forth in _Sutra 1.2_:

**योगश्चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः** (_Yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ_) _"Yoga is the restriction, control, or total cessation (nirodha) of the fluctuations, modifications, or thought-waves (vrittis) of consciousness."_

When the mental modifications remain unrestrained, the Seer (_Draṣṭṛ_ or Soul) fails to distinguish itself from the internal organ. It gets caught in _Vṛtti Sārūpyam_ (_Sutra 1.4: Vṛtti sārūpyam itaratra_), assimilating with and becoming trapped in the turbulent mental waves. Conversely, when all _vṛttis_ are silenced through total restraint (_nirodha_), absolute liberation is realized (_Sutra 1.3: Tadā draṣṭuḥ svarūpe 'vasthānam_), and the Seer rests undisturbed in its own absolute, true nature.

### 3. The Five Vrittis (Mental Modifications) & The Klishta/Aklishta Dichotomy

Patanjali classifies all mental fluctuations (_Vṛttis_) into five distinct categories (_Sutras 1.5–1.11_):

1. _**Pramāṇa**_ **(Valid Cognition)**: Correct knowledge derived through three valid means of proof—direct perception (_pratyakṣa_), logical inference (_anumāna_), and authoritative verbal testimony or scripture (_āgama_).
2. _**Viparyaya**_ **(Misperception / False Knowledge)**: Illusory knowledge of an existing object whose actual form is misapprehended (e.g., mistaking a rope for a snake or a jaundice-induced distortion where white objects appear yellow).
3. _**Vikalpa**_ **(Conceptualization / Verbal Delusion)**: Conceptualization based purely on verbal expressions that lack any corresponding objective substance or physical reality (_vastu-śūnya_), such as the verbal concept of "a rabbit's horn" (_śaśa-viṣāṇa_).
4. _**Nidrā**_ **(Sleep)**: A distinct mental modification grounded in the sense of emptiness or absence (_abhāva_) of active conscious modes, wherein consciousness rests in dreamless obscurity.
5. _**Smṛti**_ **(Memory)**: The retention and recollection of imprints (_saṁskāras_) derived from past experiences across the other four _vṛttis_ without alteration.

These five _vṛttis_ are governed by the fundamental dichotomy of being either _**Kliṣṭa**_ (painful, afflicted, or leading to karmic bondage) or _**Akliṣṭa**_ (non-painful, unafflicted, or leading toward spiritual freedom).

While a _Kliṣṭa_ modification reinforces egoistic attachments and perpetual mental turbulence, an _Akliṣṭa_ modification—specifically purified memory (_smṛti-pariśuddhi_)—can be strategically leveraged to transcend mental disturbances. As set forth in _Sutra 1.20_ (_śraddhā-vīrya-smṛti-samādhi-prajñā-pūrvaka itareṣām_), memory, when systematically purified of worldly desires, allows the practitioner to recall prior states of inner stillness and divine absorption. By calling forth these past impressions of tranquil equilibrium, the practitioner re-establishes faith (_śraddhā_) and spiritual energy (_vīrya_), utilizing purified memory as a springboard to neutralize intrusive thought-waves and cultivate unwavering, truth-bearing wisdom (_ṛtambharā prajñā_).

### 4. The Five Cittabhumi (Levels of the Mental Ground in Vyasa Bhashya)

In the _Vyāsa Bhāṣya_ (Vyasa’s foundational commentary on _Sutra 1.1_), it is established that _Samādhi_ (mental absorption) is a universal attribute (_sarvabhauma_) present across all human minds, but its expression depends entirely on the operational ground (_Cittabhūmi_) of the mind. Vyasa delineates five distinct _Cittabhūmis_:

1. _**Kṣipta**_ **(Scattered / Distracted)**: Overpowered by _Rajas_, the mind is extremely restless, constantly shifting from one object to another, and driven by worldly desires for pleasure or pain. Unfit for Yoga.
2. _**Mūḍha**_ **(Infatuated / Deluded)**: Overpowered by _Tamas_, the mind is sluggish, ignorant, sleepy, and inclined toward unrighteous actions under the influence of violent emotions. Unfit for Yoga.
3. _**Vikṣipta**_ **(Occasionally Steady)**: Influenced primarily by _Sattva_, yet intermittently disrupted by _Rajas_. The mind can temporarily fixate on pleasurable objects and withdraw from painful ones; however, because concentration remains subordinate to distraction, it is unfit for true Yoga.
4. _**Ekāgra**_ **(One-Pointed)**: Dominated by pure _Sattva_, the mind becomes completely introverted and capable of remaining fixed upon a single object. It reveals the true nature of reality, weakens the afflictions (_kleśas_), and serves as the foundation for _Saṁprajñāta Yoga_.
5. _**Niruddha**_ **(Fully Arrested / Restrained)**: Dominated by pure _Sattva_, all active mental modes, modifications, and subconscious dispositions are completely arrested, opening the doorway to _Asaṁprajñāta Yoga_.

### 5. The Kleshas (Afflictions) and Their Entanglement with Mental Modifications

When the Seer identifies with the unrestrained fluctuations of the _Citta_, consciousness becomes entangled in five fundamental afflictions (_Kleśas_):

1. _**Avidyā**_ **(Ignorance)**: The foundational root defect; mistaking the non-eternal for the eternal, the impure for the pure, pain for pleasure, and the non-Self for the Self.
2. _**Asmitā**_ **(Egoism / False Identity)**: The erroneous identification of the power of the Seer (_Draṣṭṛ_) with the instrument of seeing (_Buddhi_).
3. _**Rāga**_ **(Attachment)**: The emotional attraction and persistent craving resulting from past pleasurable experiences.
4. _**Dveṣa**_ **(Aversion)**: The emotional repulsion and hatred resulting from past painful experiences.
5. _**Abhiniveśa**_ **(Clinging to Life / Fear of Death)**: The instinctive, deep-seated fear of annihilation that persists naturally in all living beings.

These five afflictions form an integrated causal hierarchy progressing from intellectual distortions down to gross instinctive defects:

- **Intellectual Defects (**_**Caitasika Doṣa**_ **/ Root Level)**
    - _Avidyā_ (Fundamental Ignorance)
        - Generates _Asmitā_ (Egoistic Self-Notion / Intellect Distortion)
- **Emotional Reactions (**_**Mānasika**_ **/ Psychological Level)**
    - _Asmitā_ gives rise to reactive emotional duality:
        - _Rāga_ (Attachment to pleasure)
        - _Dveṣa_ (Aversion and hatred toward pain)
- **Instinctive Defects (**_**Kāyika**_ **/ Gross** _**Sthūla**_ **Level)**
    - _Rāga_ and _Dveṣa_ culminate in _Abhiniveśa_ (Instinctive clinging to life and fear of bodily dissolution)

Through systematic spiritual discipline (_Sādhana_), these gross instinctive defects are traced back to their subtle root causes and neutralized, stopping the _vṛttis_ at their origin.

### 6. The Dual Means of Restraint: Abhyasa (Practice) and Vairagya (Dispassion)

Patanjali declares in _Sutra 1.12_ that the mental modifications are arrested through the dual application of practice (_Abhyāsa_) and dispassion (_Vairāgya_).

- _**Abhyāsa**_ **(Practice)**: Defined in _Sutra 1.14_:

**स तु दीर्घकालनैरन्तर्यसत्कारासेवितो दृढभूमिः** (_Sa tu dīrghakāla nairantarya satkārāsevito dṛḍhabhūmiḥ_) _"Practice becomes firmly grounded (dṛḍhabhūmi) when it is cultivated continuously (nairantarya), over a long period of time (dīrghakāla), with earnest devotion and reverent dedication (satkāra)."_

- _**Vairāgya**_ **(Dispassion / Renunciation)**: Defined as the state of supreme mastery (_vaśīkāra_) wherein the practitioner maintains absolute control over the mind, remaining unattached to worldly objects, celestial attractions, or emotional ties (_rāga_). While _Abhyāsa_ represents positive spiritual effort, _Vairāgya_ represents negative detachment—relinquishing objective desire.

Practitioners (_Sādhakas_) are classified into four distinct categories based on their degree of enthusiasm and intensity of practice (_Sutras 1.21–1.22_):

- _**Mṛdu**_ **(Feeble / Mild Intensity)**: Seekers who exert low intensity of effort; their progress and attainment of liberation are slow and time-bound.
- _**Madhya**_ **(Average / Moderate Intensity)**: Seekers operating with medium enthusiasm and steady, moderate effort.
- _**Adhimātra**_ **(Keen / High Intensity)**: Seekers who dedicate many hours daily to rigorous discipline with deep commitment.
- _**Tīvrasaṁvegīn**_ **(Supremely Enthusiastic / Extremely Intense)**: Advanced seekers endowed with extraordinary, unyielding fervor and a fully restrained mind; for them, liberation (_kaivalya_) is imminent and timeless.

### 7. Progression of Mental Absorption: Samadhi and Ultimate Liberation

#### I. Cognitive Absorption (_Saṁprajñāta Samādhi_)

- **Operational Definition**: Lower absorption where consciousness remains focused on a subtle or gross object of meditation, systematically refining intellectual discrimination through four progressive stages:
    - _**Vitarka**_ **/** _**Savitarka**_ **(Deliberative Thought)**: Concentrated focus upon gross physical manifestations accompanied by analytical and auspicious thinking.
    - _**Vicāra**_ **/** _**Savicāra**_ **(Reflective Analysis)**: Transitioning from gross forms to subtle energetic principles (_tanmātras_) and non-deliberative reflection.
    - _**Ānanda**_ **/** _**Sananda**_ **(Spiritual Bliss)**: Transcending analytical thought to rest in an experience of unalloyed spiritual joy and illumination.
    - _**Asmitā**_ **/** _**Sasmita**_ **(Pure I-Am-ness)**: Relinquishing bliss to abide solely in pure, refined self-awareness, where egoism is transformed into pristine identity.

#### II. The Threshold of Silence (_Virāma-Pratyaya_) and Non-Cognitive Absorption (_Asaṁprajñāta Samādhi_)

- _**Virāma-Pratyaya**_ **(The Practice of Cessation)**:
    - Represents the systematic methodology and cause of total mental quietude, wherein all active _vṛttis_ are deliberately brought to a standstill.
    - Demands the complete relinquishment of all external supports, objectified concepts, _mantras_, breath regulation, or visual imagery.
    - Serves as the transitional pause where the active modifications of the internal organ (_antaḥkaraṇa-vṛtti-nirodha_) cease completely.
- _**Asaṁprajñāta Samādhi**_ **(Non-Cognitive Absorption)**:
    - The resulting state of profound absorption wherein objectified awareness entirely disappears.
    - Leaves no residual mental modifications, placing the practitioner in an unconditioned, bodiless state (_videha_) where only latent subconscious impressions (_saṁskāras_) remain.

#### III. Seedless Absorption (_Nirbīja Samādhi_) and Absolute Liberation (_Kaivalya_)

- _**Smṛti-Pariśuddhi**_ **(Purification of Memory)**:
    - The complete purification of memory wherein all objective imprints and empirical knowledge drop away like fully ripened fruit.
    - Consciousness shines forth purely without reflection or objective dependence.
- **Dissolution of Latent Impressions (**_**Saṁskāra-Śeṣa**_**)**:
    - The final dissolution of all latent seeds (_bīja_) of karma and delusion that could generate future rebirth or mental disturbance.
    - The internal organ loses its capacity to restrict or obscure true awareness.
- **Absolute Liberation (**_**Kaivalya**_**)**:
    - Unwavering, truth-bearing wisdom (_ṛtambharā prajñā_) flows perpetually.
    - The Seer (_Draṣṭṛ_) permanently transcends all five _kleśas_ and abides eternally in its true, unblemished, absolute state (_Svarūpa Avasthānam_).
