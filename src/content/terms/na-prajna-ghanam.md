Meaning: "not a mass of consciousness;" in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, negating the deep sleep state (*Prājña*) as a causal attribute of the non-dual Self (*Turīya*).

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 7)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

*Na prajñā-ghanaṃ* (न प्रज्ञाघनम्), which also appears in manuscript and commentary traditions as *na prajñānaghanaṃ* (न प्रज्ञानघनम्), is a negative Sanskrit phrasal expression meaning "not a mass of consciousness," "not massed sentiency," or "not an undifferentiated bulk of cognition."

Lexically, the phrase combines the negative particle *na* ("not") with the compound *prajñā-ghana* or *prajñāna-ghana*. The noun *prajñā* (or *prajñāna*) denotes consciousness, wisdom, or cognitive awareness, while *ghana* signifies a compact mass, solid lump, or dense collection.

Contextually within the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *na prajñā-ghanaṃ* forms an essential part of the apophatic *neti-neti* ("not this, not this") definition of *Turīya* (the Fourth) in verse 7. In verse 5, the state of deep dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*) or *Prājña* is defined as *prajñānaghana eva* ("verily a mass of unified consciousness"), wherein all waking and dream mental modifications (*vṛtti*) collapse into an undifferentiated, dark causal seed state (*kāraṇāvasthā*). In verse 7, *na prajñā-ghanaṃ* explicitly negates this deep-sleep state from *Turīya*, establishing that the ultimate non-dual Self is neither an empirical state of awareness nor a dormant mass of causal unconsciousness.

## Etymology

The expression *na prajñā-ghanaṃ* is a syntactic phrase comprising a negative indeclinable particle and an inflected nominal compound:

1. **Negative particle:** *Na* (न), an indeclinable grammatical particle expressing negation ("not").
2. **First member:** *Prajñā* (प्रज्ञा, feminine noun) or *prajñāna* (प्रज्ञान, neuter noun), derived from the verbal prefix *pra-* ("forth, forward") combined with the Sanskrit root *jñā* (ज्ञा, "to know"), denoting "consciousness, intelligence, or wisdom."
3. **Second member:** *Ghana* (घन), a masculine/neuter nominal derivative of the verbal root *han* (हन्, "to strike, compact, or slay"), meaning "solid mass, lump, or density." When appended as the final member of a compound (*uttarapada*), *-ghana* functions adjectivally to mean "consisting solely of" or "a solid mass of" (e.g., *prajñāna-ghana*).
4. **Compound classification (*samāsa*):** *Prajñā-ghana* is classified in traditional grammar (*vyākaraṇa*) as a *Karmadhāraya* (descriptive determinative compound) or a *Ṣaṣṭhī-tatpuruṣa* (genitive determinative compound).
5. **Compound expansion (*vigraha-vākya*):** *Prajñāyāḥ ghanaḥ* ("a mass of consciousness") or *prajñā-mātra-ghanaḥ* ("that which is a dense, solid mass consisting of nothing but consciousness").
6. **Inflection and syntax:** The compound takes the neuter nominative/accusative singular termination *-am* (*prajñā-ghanam* / *prajñānaghanam*), agreeing in gender, number, and case with *caturtham* (*Turīya*, the Fourth).

## Na prajñā-ghanaṃ in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

The expression occurs in verse 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, which presents the definitive text on *Turīya*:

> *nāntaḥprajñaṃ na bahiṣprajñaṁ nobhayataḥprajñaṃ na prajñānaghanaṁ na prajñaṁ nāprajñam | adṛṣṭam avyavahāryam agrāhyam alakṣaṇam acintyam avyapadeśyam ekātmapratyayasāraṁ prapañcopaśamaṁ śāntaṁ śivam advaitaṁ caturthaṁ manyante sa ātmā sa vijñeyaḥ ||* (Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, 7)
> Not consciousness inward, not consciousness outward, not consciousness of both, not a mass of consciousness, not consciousness, and not unconsciousness. The fourth (*Turīya*) is considered as invisible, unrelated, ungraspable, indefinable, inconceivable, ineffable, the Essence of the consciousness of Selfhood and Oneness, the negation of all relative existence, peaceful, auspicious, and non-dual; that is the Self; that is to be realized.

Grammatically, *na prajñā-ghanaṃ* functions as the fourth of six negative adjectival predicates modifying the unconditioned Self (*Ātman* / *Turīya*). The surrounding text systematically refutes the attributes of the three empirical quarters (*pāda*) outlined in verses 3–5:

- ***Nāntaḥprajñam*:** Not conscious of internal subjective objects (refuting *Taijasa*, the dream state, verse 4).
- ***Na bahiṣprajñam*:** Not conscious of external objective objects (refuting *Viśva* / *Vaiśvānara*, the waking state, verse 3).
- ***Nobhayataḥprajñam*:** Not conscious of both simultaneously (refuting intermediate twilight states between waking and dream).
- ***Na prajñā-ghanaṃ* / *na prajñānaghanaṃ*:** Not a mass of consciousness (refuting *Prājña*, the deep sleep state *suṣupti*, verse 5).
- ***Na prajñaṃ*:** Not simple or simultaneous cognition of all objects at once.
- ***Nāprajñam*:** Not insentient or unconscious matter (*jaḍa*).

Philosophically, Śaṅkarācārya explains in his *Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya* that *na prajñā-ghanaṃ* denies that *Turīya* is equivalent to the condition of deep sleep. In deep sleep (*suṣupti*), owing to the absence of specific mental projections, all experiences become unified into an undifferentiated mass (*prajñānaghana*); however, this state remains a causal condition (*kāraṇāvasthā*) characterized by non-apprehension of Truth (*tattvāpratibodha*) and the retention of the latent seed (*bīja*) of ignorance (*avidyā*). By stating *na prajñā-ghanaṃ*, the *Upaniṣad* clarifies that *Turīya* is free from both cause and effect (*kāraṇa-kārya-vinirmukta*), transcending the darkness of deep sleep as well as the active illusions of dream and waking.

Gauḍapāda, in his *Gauḍapādīya Kārikā* (1.11–1.15), underscores this distinction by noting that while *Viśva* and *Taijasa* are bound by both cause and effect (misapprehension and non-apprehension), *Prājña* (*prajñānaghana*) is bound by cause alone (*bīja-baddha*). *Turīya*, indicated by *na prajñā-ghanaṃ*, is bound by neither.

## Interpretations and disputes

### Orthographic and textual variants

Editions of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* and its commentaries record two orthographic forms for this negation: *na prajñā-ghanaṃ* (न प्रज्ञाघनम्) and *na prajñānaghanaṃ* (न प्रज्ञानघनम्). Both variants express the exact same philosophical meaning: the negation of the compound *prajñānaghana* introduced in verse 5.

### Causal deep sleep vs. absolute liberation

A principal area of exegetical discussion in Vedantic literature centers on whether deep sleep (*suṣupti* / *prajñānaghana*) constitutes liberation. Opponents and non-Vedantic schools occasionally mistake the bliss and non-duality of deep sleep for the ultimate goal. Advaita Vedantins emphasize that *prajñānaghana* in deep sleep is a temporary, veiled state of ignorance (*tamopradāna*), whereas *Turīya*, realized by negating *prajñā-ghana*, is permanent, unconditioned, self-luminous Pure Consciousness (*śuddha-caitanya*).

### Method of superimposition and rescission (*adhyāropa-apavāda*)

Commentators highlight the pedagogical structure of the *Upaniṣad*: verse 5 superimposes *prajñānaghana* as the third quarter (*pāda*) of the Self for the purpose of meditation (*upāsanā*), while verse 7 rescinds (*apavāda*) this attribute via *na prajñā-ghanaṃ* to reveal the unconditioned nature of *Turīya*.
