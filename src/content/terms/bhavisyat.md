Meaning: 'that which is to be' or 'the future,' denoting the future temporal dimension identified with the sacred syllable AUM.

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 1)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In Sanskrit, *bhaviṣyat* (भविष्यत्) functions as the neuter nominalized form of the future participle, literally signifying "that which is about to exist," "impending," or "the future". In the opening proclamation of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, it appears alongside *bhūtam* (the past) and *bhavat* (the present) to form the triadic division of empirical time (*trikāla*). While *bhūta* denotes that which has already come into being and *bhavat* denotes that which is currently existing, *bhaviṣyat* designates the entire domain of futurity: all events, states, and manifestations that are yet to come into existence.

Contextually, the *Upaniṣad* posits that *bhaviṣyat*, together with past and present time, is entirely non-different from the sacred syllable AUM (*Oṃkāra*). Thus, *bhaviṣyat* is defined not merely as a grammatical tense, but as one of the three temporal modalities that constitute the manifest universe, all of which are grounded in and identical with AUM.

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *bhaviṣyat* is derived from the primary Sanskrit verb root √*bhū* (भू), which signifies "to be," "to become," or "to come into existence". The word formation employs the future tense stem *bhaviṣya-* combined with the present/future active participle suffix *-at* (*śatṛ-pratyaya*). The affix *-sya-* (with the intermediate connecting vowel *i*, forming *-iṣya-*) marks futurity, while the participle termination *-at* inflects the verbal root into an active participle meaning "about to be" or "that which will become". 

When nominalized in the neuter gender (*bhaviṣyat*), it functions as a substantive denoting future time or futurity (*bhaviṣyat-kāla* or *bhaviṣyat-tva*). In classical Sanskrit grammatical treatises, the form is also used technically to designate the future tense (*bhaviṣyantī*).

## Bhaviṣyat in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

The term *bhaviṣyat* occurs in verse 1 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*: *bhūtaṁ bhavad bhaviṣyaditi sarvamoṅkāra eva* ("All that is past, present, and future is, indeed, AUM"). In this verse, *bhaviṣyat* functions as the third constituent of the temporal triad (*trikāla*), linked with *bhūtam* and *bhavat* through euphonic combination (*sandhi*) with the quotation particle *iti* (*bhaviṣyat* + *iti* = *bhaviṣyaditi*).

Within the structural framework of the *Upaniṣad*, the expression serves a vital pedagogical and metaphysical role. The text opens by declaring that the syllable AUM is all this universe (*idam sarvam*). It then unfolds this statement by categorizing all relative existence into the three divisions of time, namely the past (*bhūtam*), present (*bhavat*), and future (*bhaviṣyat*), and identifying each with AUM. Immediately following this triad, the text introduces a fourth dimension: *yaccānyat trikālātītaṁ tadapyoṅkāra eva* ("And whatever else there is beyond the threefold division of time—that also is truly AUM").

In Śaṅkara's commentary (*bhāṣya*) on verse 1, he explains that names and their corresponding objects are non-different, and all speech is a modification of AUM. Consequently, all temporal manifestations, including the unmanifested future seed-state (*bhaviṣyat*), are conditioned by time and are non-different from AUM. Furthermore, Śaṅkara notes that that which transcends the three divisions of time (*trikālātītam*), such as the unmanifested causal state (*avyākṛta*), is also AUM. 

Gauḍapāda's *Kārikā* systematically correlates the three temporal states and their corresponding cosmic and individual quarters (*viśva*, *taijasa*, *prājña*) with the sound elements *A*, *U*, and *M*, while establishing *Turīya* ("the Fourth") as the partless (*amātra*), non-dual Reality transcending all temporal conditions.

## Primary Text and Commentaries

The primary text establishing the usage of *bhaviṣyat* in this context is Mantra 1 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*:

> *hariḥ om | omityetadakṣaramidaṁ sarvaṁ tasyopavyākhyānam | bhūtaṁ bhavad bhaviṣyaditi sarvamoṅkāra eva | yaccānyat trikālātītaṁ tadapyoṅkāra eva || 1 ||*

Beyond the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, parallel formulations of the temporal triad occur across Vedantic literature:

1. ***Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* 3.8.3–4:** Gārgī questions Yājñavalkya regarding that which is "above the heavens, beneath the earth, embracing heaven and earth, past, present, and future" (*bhūtaṁ ca bhavac ca bhaviṣyac ca*), to which Yājñavalkya replies that it is woven in unmanifested ether (*ākāśa*).
2. ***Kaṭha Upaniṣad* 1.2.14 & 2.1.12–13:** The Self is described as "different from past and future" (*anyatra bhūtācca bhavyācca*) and as the "lord of the past and the future" (*īśāno bhūtabhavyasya*).
3. ***Bhagavad Gītā* 7.26:** Kṛṣṇa declares, "I know the beings that are past, present, and future (*vedāhaṁ samatītāni vartamānāni cārjuna | bhaviṣyāṇi ca bhūtāni*), but Me no one knows".
4. **Śaṅkara's *Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya* (Mantra 1 & Mantra 12):** Śaṅkara clarifies that AUM represents both the conditioned *Brahman* (*sopodhika-brahman*) bound by past, present, and future, and the unconditioned *Brahman* (*nirupadhika-brahman*) beyond the time triad.

## Interpretations and Disputes

In Vedantic exegesis, the interpretation of *bhaviṣyat* and its place within the time triad (*trikāla*) has been a subject of careful distinction between empirical (*vyāvahārika*) and ultimate (*pāramārthika*) standpoints.

1. **Temporal reality vs. superimposition**: Realist and dualist commentators interpret *bhaviṣyat* as denoting real, objective future time in a linear creationist process. In contrast, Advaita Vedantins following Gauḍapāda and Śaṅkara emphasize that time, comprising past, present, and future (*bhaviṣyat*), is a conceptual construction (*vikalpa*) or superimposition (*adhyāsa*) upon pure Consciousness. Gauḍapāda's *Kārikā* (especially in the *Vaitathya Prakaraṇa* and *Advaita Prakaraṇa*) demonstrates through reason that the three states of time are unreal like dream projections, because anything that is non-existent in the beginning and at the end (such as past and future) is also non-existent in the present.
2. **The status of the unmanifested future**: Commentators discuss whether *bhaviṣyat* includes the unmanifested causal state (*avyākṛta* / *prājña*). Śaṅkara explicitly addresses this in his *Māṇḍūkya-bhāṣya* on verse 1, stating that while past, present, and future encompass all manifested effects (*kārya*), that which is unconditioned by time yet known through its effects (*avyākṛta*) is subsumed under *trikālātītam* ("beyond the three times"), yet both aspects are non-different from AUM.
3. **Modern scholarly perspectives**: Modern scholars such as Swami Nikhilananda and Karl H. Potter note that the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* uniquely uses the analysis of the three temporal states (*bhūtam*, *bhavat*, *bhaviṣyat*) alongside the three states of consciousness (*jāgrat*, *svapna*, *suṣupti*) to establish that ultimate Reality (*Turīya*) is not merely a temporal future state to be attained (*tat tvam mṛto bhaviṣyasi*), but the ever-present, non-dual substratum of all time.
