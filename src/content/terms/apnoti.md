Meaning: "obtains, reaches, pervades, or acquires; 3rd-person singular present verb of root *āp*, denoting the attainment of all desires in *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 9)."

From the [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 9)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In ordinary and lexical Sanskrit usage, *āpnoti* (Sanskrit: आप्नोति) is the third-person singular present indicative active verb form of the primary root *āp* (आप्), meaning "he/she/it obtains," "reaches," "pervades," "attains," "gains," or "acquires." In classical Sanskrit grammar, it expresses the act of reaching, encompassing, or successfully securing an object or state.

In the philosophical context of Upaniṣadic literature, specifically in verse 9 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *āpnoti* serves as a key verbal predicate expressing both a metaphysical property of pervasiveness and the experiential result or spiritual fruit (*phala-śruti*) realized through symbolic meditation (*upāsanā*).

In verse 9, *āpnoti* occurs within the scriptural exposition of the first sound-measure (*mātrā*) of *AUM*, namely *akāra* (the letter 'A'): *prathamā mātrā 'pter ādimattvād vā 'pnoti ha vai sarvān kāmāṁś cādir bhavati ya evaṁ veda* ("The first sound-measure is *akāra*, on account of pervasiveness or priority; he who knows this obtains indeed all desires and becomes the first among the great").

In Śaṅkarācārya's commentary (*bhāṣya*), the verb *āpnoti* is linked causally to the abstract noun *āpti* ("pervasiveness" or "obtaining"):

1. **Cosmic and linguistic pervasiveness (*vyāpti*):** Just as the sound *akāra* pervades (*āpnoti*) all articulate speech (as the fundamental vowel inherent in every sound), and just as *Vaiśvānara* pervades the entire physical universe during the waking state (*jāgrat-sthāna*), the letter 'A' represents universal pervasiveness.
2. **Attainment of desires (*phala-śruti*):** On the basis of this correspondence, the seeker who contemplates *akāra* as *Vaiśvānara* obtains (*āpnoti*) all legitimate desires (*sarvān kāmān*), entering into harmony with the all-pervading waker-consciousness.

## Etymology

Etymologically, *āpnoti* is parsed according to classical Sanskrit verbal morphology:

1. **Verbal root:** Derived from the primary verbal root *āp* (आप्), belonging to the fifth conjugation class (*svādi-gaṇa*), meaning "to reach," "to obtain," "to pervade," or "to encompass."
2. **Class marker and stem formation:** In the present indicative system (*laṭ-lakāra*), roots of the fifth conjugation take the class affix *-nu-* (*śnu-pratyaya*), which undergoes *guṇa* strengthening to *-no-* in singular active forms, yielding the present stem *āpno-* (*āp* + *no* = *āpno-*).
3. **Personal termination:** Appending the third-person singular primary active ending *-ti* (*tip-pratyaya*) to the stem yields *āpnoti* (*āpno* + *ti* = *āpnoti*), functioning as a third-person singular active verb ("obtains" / "pervades").
4. **Phonetic sandhi:** In *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 9), the initial long vowel *ā-* of *āpnoti* combines with the preceding conjunction *vā* ("or") via vowel sandhi (*vā* + *āpnoti* = *vā'pnoti*), appearing in transliterated editions as *vā 'pnoti*.
