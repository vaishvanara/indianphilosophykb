**Yama**
Meaning: Abstentions

Yama constitutes the first limb of Patañjali’s eight-limbed yoga (Aṣṭāṅga Yoga), serving as the foundational ethical and moral code for a spiritual practitioner. Translated as "abstentions," "restraints," or "moral vows," the yamas regulate how an individual interacts with the external world, society, and other living beings. Patañjali outlines five universal yamas: ahiṃsā (non-violence), satya (truthfulness), asteya (non-stealing), brahmacarya (continence), and aparigraha (non-possessiveness). Crucially, Patañjali describes them as mahāvrata (the great vows), meaning they are absolute, universal, and unconditioned by time, place, class, or circumstance. They are not merely social conventions or arbitrary religious rules, but essential psychological purifiers. Without mastering the yamas, the mind remains turbulent, caught in guilt, conflict, and reactivity, making deeper meditative concentration (samādhi) impossible. They clear away negative karmic footprints and align human behavior with universal cosmic law (dharma), creating the stable psychological groundwork necessary for higher spiritual evolution.

Yama refers to the five ethical restraints or abstentions in yoga:

- अहिंसा (Ahiṃsā) - Non-violence, abstention from harming any living being
- सत्य (Satya) - Truthfulness, abstention from lying
- अस्तेय (Asteya) - Non-stealing, abstention from taking what is not given
- ब्रह्मचर्य (Brahmacārya) - Continence, abstention from sexual misconduct
- अपरिग्रह (Aparigraha) - Non-possessiveness, abstention from greed and hoarding
