**Satya**
Meaning: “truth (from the verb root as = “to be”)”
Definition: The world of the highest truth or being. 

Reference:
Grimes, John (1996). A Concise Dictionary of Indian Philosophy: Sanskrit Terms Defined in English. Albany: State University of New York Press

---

Satya is the second yama, translating to truthfulness, sincerity, and alignment with reality. It requires that a practitioner’s thoughts, words, and actions correspond truthfully to facts and the ultimate reality of existence. Practicing satya means speaking the truth without distortion, exaggeration, or deceit, yet it is always governed and tempered by the higher vow of ahiṃsā (non-harming)—meaning truth should never be spoken brutally if it causes unwarranted destruction or malicious harm. In deeper spiritual terms, satya goes beyond literal honesty; it is the alignment of one's entire life with ultimate metaphysical Truth (Brahman). When a person lives and speaks in absolute truth, their words acquire deep potency and authority. Patañjali states that one established in satya achieves karma-phala-āśrayatva, meaning their words and intentions carry the power to manifest reality, because their consciousness mirrors the unalterable truth of the universe.
