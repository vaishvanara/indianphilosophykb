Meaning: "that which has been," "the past"; also "being," "existing entity," or "gross element" (from root *bhū*).

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 1)](mandukyopanisat.md) 

---

## Definition

In Sanskrit literature and Indian philosophy, *bhūta* (Sanskrit: भूत) functions both as a participle and as a noun with several distinct shades of meaning depending on context. Literally denoting "that which has come into being" or "existed," its primary senses are categorized as follows:

1. **Temporal meaning ("the past")**: In the verse 1 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *bhūtaṃ* (भूतं) occurs as the first term in the temporal triad *bhūtaṃ bhavad bhaviṣyaditi* ("all that is past, present, and future"). Here, *bhūtaṃ* specifically designates past time or everything that has already transpired within the empirical stream of time, all of which is declared to be non-different from the sacred syllable *AUM* (*Oṁkāra*).
2. **Ontological meaning ("created being" or "entity")**: In general usage, *bhūta* refers to any living creature, entity, or created thing, encompassing divine, human, animal, and plant life.
3. **Cosmological meaning ("gross element")**: In Vedantic and Sāṅkhya cosmology, *bhūta* (or *mahābhūta*) denotes a gross physical element. The tradition enumerates five such physical principles: space/ether (*ākāśa*), air (*vāyu*), fire (*tejas*), water (*ap*), and earth (*pṛthivī*).
4. **Substantive meaning ("fact" or "reality")**: The term is additionally used to signify an actual occurrence, a fact, or reality.

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *bhūtaṃ* is the nominative and accusative singular neuter form of the past passive participle stem *bhūta*. It is derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *bhū* (भू), which belongs to the 1st conjugational class (*bhva:di-gaṇa*) and means "to be," "to become," "to exist," or "to arise".

The participial stem *bhū-ta* is formed by appending the primary past participial suffix *-ta* (*kta*) directly to the root *bhū* without internal vowel change. When inflected in the neuter gender as *bhūtaṃ*, the participle is substantivized to mean literally "that which has become" or "past time".
