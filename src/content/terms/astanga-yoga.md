# Aṣṭāṅga-Yoga

## The Eight-Fold Path of Classical Yoga Psychology

In contemporary global culture, "Yoga" is often equated primarily with physical postures (*āsanas*) and flexibility exercises. However, in classical Indian philosophy—specifically within the foundational framework laid down by Patañjali in the **Yogasūtras**—physical posture represents only one structural component of a complete cognitive and psychological discipline.

This system is known as **Aṣṭāṅga-Yoga** (Sanskrit: अष्टाङ्गयोग), derived from *aṣṭa* (eight) and *aṅga* (limb, component, or member). Far from being a linear sequence of steps to be completed sequentially and discarded, the eight limbs function as interdependent, mutually reinforcing dimensions of a unified methodology designed to systematically quiet the fluctuations of the mind (*citta-vṛtti-nirodha*) and realize pure consciousness (*Puruṣa*).

---

## Detailed Anatomy of the Eight Limbs

### The External Limbs (Bahiraṅga)

The first five limbs establish the moral, biological, and physiological preconditions required to stabilize the nervous system and clear cognitive noise.

#### 1. Yama (Ethical Restraints / Social Discipline)

The *Yamas* regulate the practitioner's relationship with the external world and other living beings, preventing the psychological agitation generated by unethical or reactive social conduct.

* **Ahiṃsā:** Non-harming in thought, speech, and action.
* **Satya:** Truthfulness aligned with non-harming.
* **Asteya:** Non-stealing and freedom from covert covetousness.
* **Brahmacarya:** Conservation and redirection of vital sensory/sexual energy.
* **Aparigraha:** Non-possessiveness and psychological freedom from greed.

#### 2. Niyama (Self-Discipline / Personal Observances)

While *Yamas* are prospective restraints, *Niyamas* are positive internal habits that build self-regulation and emotional resilience.

* **Śauca:** Purity and cleanliness of body, environment, and mental content.
* **Saṃtoṣa:** Contentment; unconditional acceptance of the present reality.
* **Tapas:** Disciplined austerity; the capacity to endure physical/mental friction to burn away conditioning.
* **Svādhyāya:** Self-study, reflection, and study of authoritative psychological/philosophical texts.
* **Īśvarapraṇidhāna:** Surrender of egoic control to a higher ordering principle or supreme awareness.

#### 3. Āsana (Posture)

In classical Raja Yoga, posture is defined simply as *Sthira-sukham āsanam*—that which is **steady and comfortable**. The primary goal of *āsana* is not physical gymnastics, but establishing a bodily state where physical restlessness is eliminated, allowing the practitioner to sit motionless for extended periods without visceral discomfort or cognitive distraction.

#### 4. Prāṇāyāma (Control of Vital Energy & Breath)

*Prāṇāyāma* involves the deliberate regulation, extension, and retention of the breath. Because respirational rhythm directly maps to autonomic nervous system activity, controlling the respiratory cycle regulates mental state, reduces heart rate variability fluctuations, and quiets brainstem arousal centers.

#### 5. Pratyāhāra (Sensory Withdrawal)

*Pratyāhāra* serves as the critical bridge between external bodily discipline and internal mental training. It is the conscious decoupling of the internal processing channels (*Manas*) from external sensory inputs (*Indriyas*). Rather than suppressing sensory organs, *Pratyāhāra* draws attention inward, much like a tortoise retracting its limbs into its shell.

---

### The Internal Limbs (Antaraṅga)

Once sensory input is internalized, the final three limbs form a continuous continuum of deep psychological concentration, collectively referred to by Patañjali as **Saṃyama** (the triple internal constraint).

```
   [Dhāraṇā] -------------> [Dhyāna] -------------> [Samādhi]
   FOCUSED INTENT           SUSTAINED FLOW          TOTAL ABSORPTION
   (Intermittent focus      (Unbroken stream        (Subject-object duality
    on an object)            of awareness)           dissolves into pure being)

```

#### 6. Dhāraṇā (Concentration)

*Dhāraṇā* is the binding of attention to a single focal point—such as the breath, an internal visualization, an inner sound (*Anāhata*), or a specific concept. At this stage, focus is deliberate and requires active cognitive effort; interruptions and mental wandering still occur periodically.

#### 7. Dhyāna (Meditation / Sustained Absorption)

When *Dhāraṇā* becomes effortless and unbroken, it transforms into *Dhyāna*. In *Dhyāna*, there is a continuous, undisturbed flow of cognition toward the chosen object, without distraction. If *Dhāraṇā* is like individual drops of water falling onto a surface, *Dhyāna* is like oil poured smoothly from a vessel.

#### 8. Samādhi (Integration / Cognitive Absorption)

The apex of the system, *Samādhi*, occurs when the practitioner's subjective sense of ego (*Ahaṃkāra*) dissolves, leaving only the essential illumination of the object of contemplation.

* **Samprajñāta Samādhi:** Absorption accompanied by subtle cognitive activity (reasoning, reflection, bliss, and I-sense).
* **Asamprajñāta / Nirbīja Samādhi:** Seedless absorption beyond cognitive constructs, where latent subconscious conditioning (*saṃskāras*) is neutralized, culminating in total spiritual liberation (*Kaivalya*).

## Aṣṭāṅga-Yoga in Cognitive Perspective

| Limb | Cognitive / Neuro-Behavioral Function |
| --- | --- |
| **Yama & Niyama** | Regulates threat-detection pathways (amygdala modulation); reduces social/moral anxiety and cognitive dissonance. |
| **Āsana & Prāṇāyāma** | Downregulates sympathetic arousal; enhances vagal tone and interoceptive awareness. |
| **Pratyāhāra** | Filters sensory gating; reduces default mode network (DMN) reactivity to external stimuli. |
| **Dhāraṇā** | Trains top-down executive attention and prefrontal cortex engagement. |
| **Dhyāna** | Sustains attentional allocation; suppresses task-irrelevant intrusive thoughts. |
| **Samādhi** | Deconstructs self-referential cognitive networks; alters state of subjective consciousness. |

---

## Summary

Patañjali’s **Aṣṭāṅga-Yoga** is a sophisticated, highly structured system of cognitive engineering. By systematically addressing social conduct, personal hygiene, bodily posture, autonomic nervous regulation, sensory filtering, and attentional focus, Aṣṭāṅga-Yoga provides a complete blueprint for moving from psychological distraction to total cognitive liberation and self-realization.
