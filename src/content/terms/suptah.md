Meaning: "asleep, sleeping; a sleeping person, the sleeper; (philosophically) one characterized by the absence of the knowledge of Reality."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5) ](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In its primary lexical sense, *supta* functions as an adjective or past participle meaning "fallen asleep," "sleeping," "inactive," or "dormant," and substantively in the masculine (*suptaḥ*) as "a sleeping person," "one asleep," or "the sleeper" (its neuter form, *suptam*, denotes "sleep").

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (mantra 5), the word occurs in the nominative singular (*supto*, modified by sandhi) within the opening clause: *yatra supto na kañcana kāmaṁ kāmayate na kañcana svapnaṁ paśyati tat suṣuptam* ("That is the state of deep sleep wherein the sleeper does not desire any objects nor does he see any dream"). 

Within this context, the term bears two distinct layers of meaning:

1. **Literal / Empirical Meaning**: It denotes the individual experiencer who has passed beyond the waking state (*jāgrat*) and the dream state (*svapna*) into deep, dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*). Here, the sleeper experiences neither the gross objects and desires of waking life nor the subtle mental projections of dreams. Deep sleep is the sphere of *Prājña*, the third quarter (*pāda*) of the Self, characterized by unified, undifferentiated consciousness (*ekībhūta*, *prajñānaghana*) and the experience of effortless bliss (*ānandabhuk*).

2. **Extended / Philosophical Meaning**: In Advaita commentary (specifically that of Śaṅkara), *supta* receives an extended technical interpretation denoting "one characterized by the absence of the knowledge of Reality" (*tattva-ajñāna*). In this figurative sense, the perceiver across all three empirical states (*Viśva* in waking, *Taijasa* in dreaming, and *Prājña* in deep sleep) is fundamentally "asleep" to the non-dual Truth (*Brahman* or *Turīya*). While waking and dreaming involve both the non-apprehension of reality and the misapprehension of it (taking appearances for what they are not), deep sleep is marked solely by non-apprehension. Thus, the Upaniṣad uses the restrictive phrase "wherein the sleeper does not desire any objects nor does he see any dream" to distinguish the deep sleeper from the waking and dreaming sleeper.

## Etymology

* **Root**: Derived from the Sanskrit verbal root √*svap* (Class II *svapiti*, Class I *svapa*), meaning "to sleep" or "to fall asleep."
* **Derivation**: Formed by appending the past passive participle suffix *-ta* (*kta*) directly to the root √*svap*. Under standard grammatical rules of internal sandhi, the root vowel and semi-vowel undergo *samprasāraṇa* (vocalic reduction of *va* to *u*), yielding the base *sup-tá*.
* **Part of Speech & Grammatical Form**: Verbal adjective / past passive participle used substantively in the masculine nominative singular (*suptaḥ*).
* **Phonetic Environment in Text**: In *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* 5, the visarga of *suptaḥ* is replaced with *o* before the voiced consonant *na* (*suptaḥ* + *na* → *supto na*).
* **Status**: The etymology and morphological derivation from √*svap* + *-ta* are fully established by Sanskrit lexicographers and grammarians.
