**Utkarṣati**
Meaning: "increases"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 10)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

"To exalt, elevate, or cause to increase; to attain superior or elevated knowledge."

---

## Definition

In Classical Sanskrit lexicography, *utkarṣati* (उत्कर्षति) literally means "draws up," "pulls upward," or "elevates" (MacDonell, 1892, p. 48). From this primary physical sense of upward traction, the verb acquires extended and figurative meanings denoting elevation in rank or status, enhancement, exaltation, and the expansion or intensification of knowledge (MacDonell, 1892, p. 48; Śaṅkarācārya, trans. 1936, p. 77).

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (Mantra 10), *utkarṣati* occurs in the exposition of the second quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*) and its symbolic identification with the second phonetic element (*mātrā*) of the sacred syllable *OM* (*AUM*), namely the vowel *U* (*ukāra*). The Upaniṣad correlates *Taijasa*—the manifestation of the Self in the dream state, which cognizes subtle, internal mental impressions—with the sound *U* on two explicit grounds: *utkarṣāt* ("on account of superiority or exaltation") and *ubhayatvāt* ("on account of being intermediate") (Śaṅkarācārya, trans. 1936, p. 77). 

In his commentary (*bhāṣya*), Śrī Śaṅkarācārya explains that the sound *U* is regarded as "superior" (*utkṛṣṭa*) because it succeeds *A* in the utterance of *AUM*, while *Taijasa* is spiritually superior to the waking self (*Viśva*) because *Taijasa* experiences inward states of mind (*manaḥ-spandita*) and subtle impressions, bringing the seeker closer to non-dual reality than external sensory perception (Śaṅkarācārya, trans. 1936, p. 77). The Upaniṣad then articulates the meditative result (*phala*) of realizing this identity: *utkarṣati ha vai jñānasantatim* ("he elevates or expands the continuity of knowledge") (Śaṅkarācārya, trans. 1936, p. 77). Śaṅkarācārya glosses *utkarṣati* here as *vardhayati* ("causes to grow or increase"), indicating that the contemplative’s capacity and continuity of spiritual knowledge undergo continuous elevation and expansion, resulting in superior wisdom, universal respect, and an unbroken lineage of Vedāntic knowers (Śaṅkarācārya, trans. 1936, p. 77).

In broader Advaita Vedānta literature, the nominal derivative *utkarṣa* is similarly employed to denote superiority, elevation, or the contemplative practice of superimposing a higher principle onto a meditative symbol (*utkarṣa-buddhi*) (Sivananda, 1949, p. 480).

## Etymology

The term *utkarṣati* (उत्कर्षति) is a third-person singular present active indicative verb (*prathama-puruṣa ekavacana*, Parasmaipada) derived from the verbal root *kṛṣ* (कृष्, Class 1 / 6, meaning "to draw," "to pull," "to drag," or "to plow") combined with the directional prefix *ud-* (उत्, signifying "up," "upward," "out," or "exaltation") (MacDonell, 1892, p. 48). 

According to Sanskrit sandhi rules, the final dental consonant of the prefix *ud-* assimilates to the unvoiced plosive of the root, yielding *ut-* before *k-*. In the Class 1 present system formation, the radical vowel *ṛ* undergoes *guṇa* strengthening to *ar*, producing the present verbal stem *utkarṣa-*. The primary active third-person singular suffix *-ti* is appended to this stem (*ut-* + *kṛṣ* + *a* + *ti* = *utkarṣati*) (MacDonell, 1892, p. 48; Monier-Williams, 1864, p. 72). 

Standard nominal cognates formed from the same prefix-root combination include the abstract noun *utkarṣa* (उत्कर्ष, "elevation, increase, exaltation, distinction, excellence") and the past passive participle *utkṛṣṭa* (उत्कृष्ट, "drawn up, elevated, superior, exalted") (MacDonell, 1892, pp. 48–49).
