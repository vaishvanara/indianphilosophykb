---

## Definition

In Sanskrit, *eva* (एव) is an indeclinable particle and adverb used primarily to emphasize, restrict, or intensify the meaning of the preceding word. Its primary lexical meanings include "only," "just," "exactly," "precisely," "alone," "merely," "in fact," and "without limitation". 

In classical Sanskrit grammar and philosophical texts, *eva* functions as a restrictive particle (*niyama*) that operates in three distinct semantic contexts:

1. **Qualificand restriction:** When attached to a noun or subject, it restricts the attribute exclusively to that subject and negates it elsewhere (e.g., "Arjuna alone is a bowman").
2. **Adjectival restriction:** When attached to an adjective or qualifier, it emphasizes or isolates that specific attribute (e.g., "the conch is white alone").
3. **Verbal restriction:** When attached to a verb, it indicates the necessary existence or occurrence of the action, asserting its presence and negating its non-association (e.g., "the blue lotus does exist").

In Vedic literature, *eva* (frequently written as *evā*) additionally functions as a demonstrative adverb meaning "so" or "just so" (a usage later replaced by *evam*) or as an adjective meaning "speedy" or "swift," and as a noun referring to a "course," "way," "habit," or "custom".

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *eva* is classified as an indeclinable (*avyaya*) particle or adverb. Traditional lexicographers and grammarians derive the indeclinable particle *eva* from the verbal root *i* ("to go") with the *uṇ* suffix (*Uṇādisūtra* i, 152), or trace it to the pronominal base *e-*. The homonymous Vedic nominal and adjectival form *éva* ("swift," "course") is likewise connected to the verbal root *i* ("to go").
