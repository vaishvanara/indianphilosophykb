## Definition

In ordinary and grammatical Sanskrit usage, *bhavati* (Sanskrit: भवति) is the third-person singular present indicative active verb form of the root *bhū* (भू), meaning "he/she/it is," "becomes," "comes to be," "takes place," or "exists."

In Upaniṣadic literature, specifically within the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verses 9, 10, and 11), *bhavati* functions as a verbal predicate expressing the spiritual transformation, ontological identification, or experiential result (*phala*) attained by an aspirant who realizes the symbolic equivalence between the sound-measures (*mātrā*) of the sacred monosyllable *AUM* (*OM*) and the quarters (*pāda*) of Consciousness (*Ātman*). In these contexts, *bhavati* transitions from a simple copula ("is") to an existential or causative designation of spiritual becoming ("becomes" or "comes to be established as").

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *bhavati* is parsed according to classical Sanskrit verbal morphology:

1. **Verbal root:** Derived from the primary verbal root *bhū* (भू), classified under the first conjugation class (*bhvādi-gaṇa*), meaning "to be," "to become," or "to exist."
2. **Stem formation:** In the present system (*laṭ-lakāra*), the root vowel *ū* undergoes *guṇa* strengthening to *o*, which transforms into *av* before the thematic vowel *-a-*, producing the present verbal stem *bhava-* (*bhū* → *bho* + *a* = *bhava-*).
3. **Personal ending:** Appending the third-person singular primary active ending *-ti* (*tip-pratyaya*) to the stem yields *bhavati* (*bhava* + *ti* = *bhavati*), functioning as a third-person singular present tense verb ("becomes" / "is").

## Bhavati in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* and its classical commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā* and Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*), *bhavati* occurs repeatedly in mantras 9, 10, and 11 to declare the spiritual efficacy and meditative results (*phala-śruti*) accruing to a seeker who meditates on *AUM* through its constituent sound-measures (*mātrā*):

### 1. Verse 9 (meditation on akāra / 'A')

Regarding the first sound-measure *akāra* ('A'), which corresponds to *Vaiśvānara* (the Self in the waking state, *jāgrat*), the Upaniṣad states: *āpnoti ha vai sarvān kāmāṁś cādir bhavati ya evaṁ veda* Meaning: "He who knows this obtains indeed all desires and becomes (*bhavati*) the first among the great". Here, *bhavati* denotes the elevation of the meditator to a preeminent, foundational status (*ādi*), mirroring the priority of *akāra* among phonemes.

### 2. Verse 10 (meditation on ukāra / 'U')

Regarding the second sound-measure *ukāra* ('U'), corresponding to *Taijasa* (the Self in the dream state, *svapna*), the text states: *utkarṣati ha vai jñāna-santatiṁ samānaś ca bhavati nāsyābrahmavit kule bhavati ya evaṁ veda*. Meaning: "He who knows this exalts the continuum of knowledge, becomes (*bhavati*) equal (mindful of harmony), and in his family no one ignorant of Brahman is born / becomes (*bhavati*)". In this mantra, *bhavati* appears twice: first to signify the aspirant's attainment of equanimity (*samānaḥ bhavati*), and second to affirm that no unillumined offspring comes to be (*na bhavati*) in such a knower's lineage.

### 3. Verse 11 (meditation on makāra / 'M')

Regarding the third sound-measure *makāra* ('M'), corresponding to *Prājña* (the Self in deep sleep, *suṣupti*), the text declares: *mīte ha vā idaṁ sarvam apītiś ca bhavati ya evaṁ veda*. Meaning: "He who knows this measures all this and becomes (*bhavati*) the place of merger". Here, *bhavati* denotes the realization of the causal substratum wherein all phenomenal distinctions are resolved.

### Doctrinal and Exegetical Role

In Advaita Vedānta commentary, Śaṅkarācārya explains that the usage of *bhavati* in these verses reflects the progressive stages of contemplative absorption (*upāsanā*). While the empirical results expressed with *bhavati* in verses 9–11 reward lower or qualitative meditations (*saguṇa-upāsanā*), they serve as pedagogical steps leading the seeker to the soundless fourth state (*amātrā* / *Turīya*), where all process of "becoming" (*bhavana*) ceases in the realization of the eternal, non-dual Self (*Ātman*).
