Meaning: "not consciousness" or "not conscious," denoting that *Turīya* is not a state of simultaneous or omniscient cognition.

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 7)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

The expression *na prajñaṃ* (न प्रज्ञं) is a Sanskrit negative phrasal construction composed of the negative particle *na* (न, "not") and the nominative/accusative singular neuter adjective or noun *prajñam* (प्रज्ञम्, "conscious," "knowing," or "cognition"). Literally, it translates to "not conscious" or "not cognitive."

In its specific textual context in Mantra 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *na prajñaṃ* functions as a pivotal apophatic negation (*niṣedha*). It specifies that *Turīya* (तुरीय, the transcendent "Fourth" aspect of Reality/Self) is not a state of active, all-encompassing, or omniscient consciousness. Contextually, *na prajñaṃ* is paired with *nāprajñam* (नाप्रज्ञम्, "nor unconscious"): while *na prajñaṃ* negates the notion that *Turīya* is an empirical or objective state of knowing, *nāprajñam* clarifies that *Turīya* is neither insentient (*jaḍa*) nor a mere void (*śūnya*). Thus, idiomatic and contextual analysis demonstrates that *na prajñaṃ* denies objective or relational cognition without negating Pure Consciousness itself.

## Etymology

The phrase *na prajñaṃ* (न प्रज्ञं) consists of two members:

1. *na* (न): The uninflected negative particle in Sanskrit denoting negation ("not" or "no").
2. *prajña* (प्रज्ञ): A nominal stem formed by combining the verbal prefix *pra-* (प्र, signifying "forth," "intensity," or "distinction") with the verbal root *jñā* (ज्ञा, "to know" or "to cognize"), followed by the primary nominal suffix *-a*, yielding the adjective or noun *prajña* ("intelligent," "knowing," or "conscious").

In Mantra 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the word takes the neuter nominative/accusative singular inflection *prajñam* (प्रज्ञम्) to agree grammatically with the unexpressed neuter subject (*Brahma* or the neuter aspect of *Turīyam*). In phrasal sandhi with the preceding and following words in the verse (*na prajñāna-ghanaṁ na prajñaṁ nāprajñam*), the negative particle *na* precedes *prajñam* without compounding into a single word, followed by vowel sandhi between *na* and *aprajñam* (*na* + *aprajñam* \\(\rightarrow\\) *nāprajñam*). Grammatically, if analyzed as a negative compound (*nañ-tatpuruṣa* or *nañ-bahuvrīhi* in derivative nominal forms), the prefix *a-* or particle *na* signifies the absence or negation of specific, objectified knowing (*prajñāna*).

## *Na prajñaṃ* in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

The expression *na prajñaṃ* occurs in Mantra 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, which defines *Turīya*—the non-dual Supreme Reality—through a series of rigorous negative predicates (*neti neti*):

> *nāntaḥ-prajñaṁ na bahiṣ-prajñaṁ nobhayataḥ-prajñaṁ na prajñāna-ghanaṁ na prajñaṁ nāprajñam |*  
> *adṛṣṭam avyavahāryam agrāhyam alakṣaṇam acintyam avyapadeśyam ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṁ prapañcopaśamaṁ śāntaṁ śivam advaitaṁ caturthaṁ manyante sa ātmā sa vijñeyaḥ ||*

Grammatically, *na prajñaṃ* acts as one of six distinct cognitive negations in the opening line of the mantra. The *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* structures human experience and the Self (*Ātman*) into four quarters (*pāda*):
1. *Vaiśvānara* / *Viśva* (the waking state), characterized as *bahiṣ-prajña* (conscious of the external world).
2. *Taijasa* (the dream state), characterized as *antaḥ-prajña* (conscious of the internal mental world).
3. *Prājña* (the deep sleep state), characterized as *prajñāna-ghana* (a unified mass of undifferentiated consciousness/bliss).
4. *Turīya* (the Fourth), the unconditioned, non-dual substratum of all states.

Within this surrounding structure, *na prajñaṃ* denies that *Turīya* is a state of simultaneous omniscient cognition or simple active knowing. The Upaniṣad systematically negates:
- Internal cognition (*nāntaḥ-prajñam*)
- External cognition (*na bahiṣ-prajñam*)
- Intermediate/simultaneous dual cognition (*nobhayataḥ-prajñam*)
- Undifferentiated mass of sleep-cognition (*na prajñāna-ghanam*)
- All-knowing/simultaneous cognition (*na prajñaṃ*)
- Unconsciousness or insentiency (*nāprajñam*)

### Commentarial Interpretations

In Śaṅkarācārya's commentary (*Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya*), Śaṅkara explicitly clarifies that *na prajñaṃ* means *Turīya* is not a state of simultaneous perception of all objects (*na yugapad eva sarvaviṣaya-jñātṛ*). Śaṅkara notes that while *Prājña* (deep sleep) represents the causal seed state (*bīja-nidrā*) where duality is unperceived due to ignorance (*avidyā*), *Turīya* is the ever-illumined, non-dual Reality (*sarva-dṛk*) free from both cause (non-apprehension) and effect (misapprehension).

Gauḍapāda, in his *Māṇḍūkya-kārikā* (Book I, *Āgama Prakaraṇa*), further elaborates that *Prājña* knows neither itself nor truth/untruth, whereas *Turīya* is eternally aware and non-dual. Gauḍapāda stresses that negating these cognitive modes does not destroy Reality; rather, when superimposed duality (*prapañca*) is sublated through knowledge, *Turīya* shines as the self-established Self.

---

### Advaita Vedānta School Perspective

In the Advaita Vedānta tradition of Śaṅkara and Gauḍapāda, *na prajñaṃ* is essential for preventing the reification of *Turīya* into a relational God (*Īśvara*) or an active mental state. It establishes that *Turīya* is not an object to be known by an instrument of knowledge (*pramāṇa*), but Pure, unconditioned Consciousness (*Caitanya*) in which the subject-object distinction is completely dissolved.
