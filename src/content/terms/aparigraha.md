**Aparigraha**
Translation: “non-possession; non-grasping; non-desiring; not greedy”
Definition: It is to accept only that which is absolutely necessary, in thought, word, and deed.

Reference:
Grimes, John (1996). A Concise Dictionary of Indian Philosophy: Sanskrit Terms Defined in English. Albany: State University of New York Press

---

Aparigraha is the fifth yama, translating to non-possessiveness, non-greed, non-attachment, or non-hoarding. It instructs the practitioner not to accumulate material possessions, wealth, or sensory objects beyond what is genuinely necessary for sustaining life and fulfilling one's dharmic duties. Aparigraha guards against the trap of consumerism and the psychological illusion that accumulation brings security and happiness. Hoarding stems from fear, insecurity, and a scarcity mindset—the anxious belief that there will not be enough in the future. By letting go of excess and practicing voluntary simplicity, the practitioner learns to trust the natural flow of life. Furthermore, aparigraha applies to non-attachment to ideas, opinions, relationships, and memories, preventing the mind from clinging to the past. Patañjali highlights a profound mystical benefit of this practice: when one is entirely free from greed and hoarding, they gain deep insight into the mechanics of existence, including understanding past and future cycles, as the mind is no longer clogged by material weights.
