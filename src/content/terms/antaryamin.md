Meaning: "inner controller, indwelling guide, or immanent ruler; in *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 6), an epithet of *Prājña*/*Īśvara* as the inner controller of all."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 6)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In ordinary and lexical Sanskrit usage, *antaryāmin* (Sanskrit: अन्तर्यामिन्, nominative singular: *antaryāmī*) is a masculine noun and adjectival compound literally translating as "inner controller," "indwelling ruler," "inner guide," or "the witness dwelling within every living being." It combines *antar* ("within," "inner") with *yāmin* ("guide," "ruler," "controller").

In Upanishadic literature and classical Vedantic philosophy, most prominently in verse 6 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *antaryāmin* functions as a technical designation for the immanent aspect of God (*Īśvara*) or the causal Self (*Prājña*). In this context, *antaryāmin* describes the omniscient, divine Consciousness that indwells all creatures and cosmic phenomena, directing and sustaining all physical, sensory, and mental operations from within without being affected by their changing modifications.

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *antaryāmin* is a Sanskrit agent compound parsed according to classical nominal morphology:

1. **First member:** *Antar* (अन्तर्), an indeclinable adverbial particle or preposition meaning "within," "inside," "inner," or "inwardly."
2. **Second member:** *-yāmin* (यामिन्), an agentive nominal suffix derived from the primary Sanskrit verbal root *yam* (यम्), meaning "to guide," "to curb," "to restrain," "to control," or "to rule."
3. **Grammatical function:** *Antaryāmin* functions grammatically as a masculine agent noun or adjective (*antaryāmī* in nominative singular).
4. **Phonetic combination:** In compound formation, the components *antar* and *yāmin* join directly as *antaryāmin* ("one who rules or controls from within").

## Antaryāmin in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 6) and its commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā*, Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*, Śrī Sadananda's *Vedāntasāra*, Śrī Vidyāraṇya Svāmī's *Pañcadaśī*, and Śaṅkarācārya's *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* 1.2.18–20), *antaryāmin* defines the divine, causal nature of Consciousness.

### Textual context (verse 6)

Verse 6 describes the third quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), corresponding to deep sleep (*suṣupti*) on the individual level (*Prājña*) and the unmanifest causal state on the cosmic level (*Īśvara*): 

> "He is the Lord of all (*eṣa sarveśvaraḥ*); He is the knower of all (*eṣa sarvajñaḥ*); He is the inner controller (*eṣa antaryāmī*); He is the source of all (*eṣa yoniḥ sarvasya*), for from Him all beings originate and in Him they finally disappear."

### Doctrinal meaning and cognitive architecture

1. **Immanent cause and ruler (*kāraṇa-svarūpa*):** Advaita Vedānta explains that *antaryāmin* represents Pure Consciousness associated with the collective causal sheath (*kāraṇa-śarīra*) or cosmic illusion (*māyā*). As *antaryāmin*, *Īśvara* / *Prājña* is both the material and efficient cause (*nimittopādāna-kāraṇa*) of the universe. It pervades all minds, senses, and elements like a subtle thread (*sūtra*), impelling their functions while remaining their unseen witness (*sākṣī*).
2. **Vedic foundations (*Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* III.7):** The *Māṇḍūkya* formulation draws directly upon Yājñavalkya's exposition of the *Antaryāmin* in the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* (3.7.3–23). Yājñavalkya declares that the Inner Controller inhabits the earth, water, fire, sky, mind, and intellect, controlling each from within while remaining unknown to them: "He who inhabits the intellect, yet is within the intellect, whom the intellect does not know, whose body the intellect is, and who controls the intellect from within—He is your Self, the Inner Controller, the Immortal."
3. **Distinction from Transcendent Liberation (*Turīya*):** Advaita Vedānta clarifies that while *antaryāmin* is the supreme Lord and controller of the manifest cosmos within empirical reality (*vyavahāra*), this controllership (*antaryāmitva*) is still an attribute conditioned by causal nescience (*māyā* / *upādhi*). In *Turīya* (the "Fourth", verse 7), all concepts of ruling, creation, and causal relation subside into unconditioned, partless, non-dual Pure Consciousness (*Nirguṇa Brahman*).
