See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 4)](mandukyopanisat.md)
 
---

## Definition

Lexically, *antaḥ* (Sanskrit: अन्तः), a euphonic variant of *antar* or *antas*, functions primarily as an indeclinable adverb, preposition, or prefix signifying "within," "inside," "inwards," "in the middle," "interior," "amidst," or "between". When used as an adjectival compound prefix, it designates an inner, internal, or intermediate location or relationship, contrasting directly with external (*bāhya* or *bahir*) phenomena. In a secondary lexical sense connected to the nominal stem *anta*, the term also denotes "end," "limit," "boundary," "border," "conclusion," or "extremity".

In classical Indian philosophy, *antaḥ* is most prominent as a prefix in compound terms defining internal faculties and subjective states:

1. **Internal cognitive apparatus (*antaḥkaraṇa*)**: Literally the "inner instrument" or "internal organ," *antaḥkaraṇa* designates the internal psychological organ as distinguished from the external sense organs (*bāhyendriya* / *bāhyakarana*). Across philosophical systems, it functions as the seat of cognitive processing, receiving and arranging sensory data. In Advaita Vedānta, Sāṃkhya, and Yoga, it is analyzed into distinct functional aspects: the intellect (*buddhi*), ego (*ahaṅkāra*), reflective mind (*manas*), and memory/subconscious consciousness (*citta*).
2. **Inward consciousness and subjective states**: Preceding terms of cognition or experience, *antaḥ* specifies an internal orientation. For instance, *antaḥprajñā* ("inwardly cognitive") denotes the subjective consciousness characteristic of the dream state (*taijasa*) where experience is directed toward internal mental projections rather than external objects. Similarly, *antaḥsukha* denotes internal happiness, and *antaryāmin* refers to the inner guide or indwelling ruler.

---

## Etymology

In classical Sanskrit grammar, *antaḥ* is classified as an indeclinable (*avyaya*), functioning as an adverbial or prepositional particle (*upasarga* / *nipāta*) indicating interiority or position within. 

Phonetically, *antaḥ* is the *visarga*-final variant of the base *antar* / *antas*, produced in sentence or compound formation according to euphonic combination (*sandhi*) rules before voiceless consonants or in pauses. 

In grammatical terminology, *antaḥstha* (literally "standing in between") designates the semivowels (*y*, *r*, *l*, *v*), so called because they occupy an intermediate position between vowels and consonants or stops and sibilants. Furthermore, in Paninian rules, operations that apply internally within a word during its formation stage are termed *antaḥkārya* or *antaraṅga*, as opposed to external operations (*bahiraṅga*).
