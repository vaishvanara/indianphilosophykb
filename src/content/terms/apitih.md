Meaning: "merger, reabsorption, or place of dissolution; nominative form *apītiḥ* used in *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 11) to describe becoming the place of dissolution."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 11)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In ordinary Sanskrit grammar and lexicography, *apītiḥ* (Sanskrit: अपीतिः, nominative singular of the feminine noun *apīti*) signifies "merger," "reabsorption," "dissolution," "disappearance," or "entry into a material or causal source." Derived from the verbal combination *api-i* ("to go into" or "to merge"), the term denotes both the process of an effect dissolving back into its cause and the locus or place where that dissolution occurs.

In the second clause of verse 11 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the word occurs as *apītiś* (modified via palatal sibilant sandhi before *ca*) within the statement: *mīte ha vā idaṁ sarvam apītiś ca bhavati ya evaṁ veda* ("He who knows this measures all this and becomes the place of merger/dissolution"). In this scriptural usage, *apītiḥ* functions as a predicate nominative noun designating the causal state or locus of reabsorption attained by a meditator who realizes the symbolic identity between *makāra* (the third constituent letter 'M' of *AUM*) and *Prājña* (the Self in deep sleep, *suṣupti*).

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *apītiḥ* is parsed according to classical Sanskrit nominal morphology:

1. **Verbal components:** Formed from the primary verbal root *i* (इ), meaning "to go," "to move," or "to enter," prefixed by the preverb *api-* (अपि-), which indicates interiority, entry, or union ("into," "unto," "internal to"). The combined verbal stem *apī-* (*api* + *i*) means "to merge into" or "to be reabsorbed."
2. **Nominal suffixation:** The primary feminine action suffix *-ti* (*ktin-pratyaya*) is appended to *apī-*, yielding the abstract noun stem *apīti* (*api* + *i* + *ti* = *apīti*), meaning "the act, state, or locus of merging/dissolution."
3. **Grammatical form:** In the nominative singular case (*prathamā vibhakti*), the *i*-stem noun inflects as *apītiḥ* (अपीतिः, "merger" or "the place of merger").
4. **Phonetic sandhi:** In the second clause of *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* verse 11, when the nominative singular visarga (*-ḥ*) of *apītiḥ* is followed by the voiceless palatal conjunction *ca* ("and"), it undergoes regular visarga-sandhi transformation into the palatal sibilant *-ś-*, producing the combined textual reading *apītiś ca*.

## Apītiḥ in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 11) and its classical commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā*, Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*, and related Advaita Vedānta texts), *apītiḥ* plays a central role in articulating the experiential result (*phala-śruti*) of meditating on the third sound-measure (*mātrā*) of *AUM*.

### Clause structure and exegetical transition

Verse 11 contains a twofold structural formulation:

- **First clause (ground of correspondence):** Uses the ablative case *apīteḥ* ("on account of merger") to explain why *makāra* ('M') corresponds to *Prājña*: just as the preceding sounds 'A' (*akāra*) and 'U' (*ukāra*) merge into 'M' upon pronunciation, so gross waking (*Viśva*) and subtle dream (*Taijasa*) experiences merge into *Prājña* during deep sleep (*suṣupti*).
- **Second clause (result of knowledge):** Transitions to the nominative case *apītiḥ* (*apītiś ca bhavati*) to state the spiritual fruit realized by the knower: "He who knows this measures all this and becomes the place of merger/dissolution."

### Doctrinal and experiential significance

1. **At-one-ment with the causal substratum:** In Advaita Vedānta, *Prājña* (on the individual level) and *Īśvara* (on the cosmic level) represent the causal state (*kāraṇa-svarūpa* or *kāraṇa-śarīra*) in which all differentiated names and forms (*nāmarūpa*) subside into unmanifest seed-ignorance (*bīja-nidrā* / *māyā*). By meditating on *makāra* as *Prājña*, the aspirant transcends superficial identification with the waking and dream bodies, becoming established as the causal locus (*apītiḥ*) in which all universe-manifestations dissolve.
2. **Gateway to non-dual realization:** Becoming the "place of merger" (*apītiḥ*) marks the highest stage of qualitative meditation (*saguṇa-upāsanā*). By resolving all objective multiplicity into the causal state of *Prājña*, the seeker is prepared to cross over from the sound-measures (*mātrā*) of *AUM* into the measureless, soundless silence (*amātrā*) of *Turīya* (the "Fourth"), realizing the absolute, non-dual Self (*Nirguṇa Brahman*).
