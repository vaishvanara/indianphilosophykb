Meaning: "not consciousness of both" or "not conscious of both internal and external objects;" in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, negating twilight or intermediate cognition between waking and dream.

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 7)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

*Nobhayataḥprajñaṃ* (नोभयतःप्रज्ञम् / नोभयातःप्रज्ञं)—formed via sandhi from *na* (न) and *ubhayataḥprajñam* (उभयतःप्रज्ञम्)—is a negative Sanskrit phrasal expression meaning "not conscious of both sides," "not possessed of dual-directional cognition," or "not aware of internal and external worlds simultaneously."

Lexically, the compound combines the indeclinable particle *na* ("not"), the adverb *ubhayataḥ* ("from or on both sides," "in both directions"), and the nominal stem *prajñā* ("consciousness," "cognition," or "awareness"). 

Contextually within the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (Mantra 7), *nobhayataḥprajñaṃ* forms an integral clause in the apophatic neti-neti ("not this, not this") definition of *Turīya* (the non-dual Fourth). While waking consciousness (*bahiṣprajña*) is directed outward toward physical objects and dream consciousness (*antaḥprajña*) is directed inward toward mental impressions, *nobhayataḥprajñaṃ* explicitly negates any intermediate, hybrid, or twilight state of consciousness (*sandhyāvasthā* / *antarālavastā*) between waking and dream—such as a state in which an individual is half-aware of external perceptions and half-aware of internal fantasies simultaneously.

---

## Etymology

The expression *nobhayataḥprajñaṃ* (नोभयतःप्रज्ञम्) is a syntactic phrase comprising a negative indeclinable particle and an inflected nominal compound:

1. **Negative Particle**: *Na* (न), an indeclinable grammatical particle denoting negation ("not").
2. **First Member / Adverbial Base**: *Ubhayatas* (उभयतस् / उभयतः), an indeclinable adverb formed from the pronominal adjective stem *ubhaya* (उभय, "both") by adding the directional/ablative adverbial suffix *-tas* (*tasil* in Pāṇinian grammar), meaning "on both sides," "from both directions," or "toward both realms."
3. **Second Member**: *Prajñā* (प्रज्ञा, feminine noun), derived from the verbal prefix *pra-* ("forth, forward") and the root *jñā* (ज्ञा, "to know"), signifying "consciousness, intelligence, or cognition."
4. **Sandhi Union**: The combination of the final short *-a* of *na* with the initial *u-* of *ubhayataḥprajñam* undergoes vocalic guṇa combination (*a* + *u* → *o*), yielding *nobhayataḥprajñam* (नोभयतःप्रज्ञम्).
5. **Compound Classification (*Samāsa*)**: *Ubhayataḥ-prajña* is classified in Sanskrit grammar (*vyākaraṇa*) as a *Bahuvrīhi* (possessive exocentric compound).
6. **Compound Expansion (*Vigraha-vākya*)**: *ubhayataḥ (ubhayor antaḥ-bāhya-viṣayayoḥ) prajñā yasya tat nobhayataḥprajñam* ("that entity whose consciousness is not directed to both internal and external objects simultaneously").
7. **Inflection & Syntax**: The compound takes the neuter nominative/accusative singular termination *-am* (*nobhayataḥprajñam*), agreeing in gender, number, and case with *caturtham* (*Turīya*, the Fourth).

## Nobhayataḥ-prajñaṃ in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

The expression occurs in Mantra 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, which presents the definitive text on the nature of *Turīya*:

> *nāntaḥprajñaṁ na bahiṣprajñaṁ nobhayataḥprajñaṁ na prajñānaghanaṁ na prajñaṁ nāprajñam | adṛṣṭam avyavahāryam agrāhyam alakṣaṇam acintyam avyapadeśyam ekātmapratyayasāraṁ prapañcopaśamaṁ śāntaṁ śivam advaitaṁ caturthaṁ manyante sa ātmā sa vijñeyaḥ ||* (Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, 7)

Grammatically, *nobhayataḥprajñaṃ* functions as the third of six negative adjectival predicates modifying the unconditioned Self (*Ātman* / *Turīya*). The surrounding text establishes a systematic negation of all empirical modalities of experience described in earlier mantras:
- *nāntaḥprajñam*: Not conscious of the internal subjective world (negating *Taijasa*, the dream state, Mantra 4).
- *na bahiṣprajñam*: Not conscious of the external objective world (negating *Viśva* / *Vaiśvānara*, the waking state, Mantra 3).
- *nobhayataḥprajñam*: Not conscious of both simultaneously (negating intermediate twilight states between waking and dream).
- *na prajñānaghanam*: Not a solid mass of unified consciousness (negating *Prājña*, the causal deep-sleep state, Mantra 5).
- *na prajñaṃ*: Not simple or simultaneous cognition of the entire universe at once.
- *nāprajñam*: Not insentient or unconscious matter (*jaḍa*).

Philosophically, Śaṅkarācārya explains in his *Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya* that *nobhayataḥprajñaṃ* denies that *Turīya* can be identified with any intermediate state (*antarālavastā*) between waking and dream—such as a daydreaming condition in which one half-perceives external objects and half-perceives internal mental imagery. By excluding this twilight state alongside waking, dream, and deep sleep, the Upaniṣad establishes that *Turīya* is not a psychological state among states, but the non-dual, relationless substratum (*niranvaya-adhiṣṭhāna*) underlying all experience.

Gauḍapāda, in his *Gauḍapādīya Kārikā* (Agama Prakaraṇa), underscores that the negation of these distinct cognitive orientations demonstrates that duality (*dvaita*) has no real existence (*ajātavāda*). When the illusory attributions of waking, dream, intermediate states, and deep sleep are removed through apophatic negation, the non-dual Self (*Turīya*) alone shines as the sole Reality.

## Interpretations and Disputes

1. **Translation and Exegetical Nuances**:
   Translators and commentators render *nobhayataḥprajñaṃ* with slight stylistic variations. Swami Nikhilananda translates it as "nor that which is conscious of both," while traditional glosses specify "not conscious of what is between the two." Śaṅkarācārya explicitly clarifies that the term negates an intermediate state (*antarālavastā*) between the waking state (*jāgaritasthāna*) and the dream state (*svapnasthāna*).

2. **Prevention of Psychological Misconceptions**:
   Advaita Vedāntic scholars emphasize the pedagogical necessity of including *nobhayataḥprajñaṃ* in Mantra 7. Without this specific negation, an inquirer might erroneously suppose that *Turīya* represents a hybrid, altered, or hypnotic state of consciousness produced by blending internal and external perceptions. The negation clarifies that *Turīya* transcends all empirical, psychological, and transitional modes of cognition.
