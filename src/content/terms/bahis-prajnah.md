Meaning: "outwardly conscious or aware of external objects; an epithet of *Vaiśvānara* in the waking state (*jāgrat*) in Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, Verse 3."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 3)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In lexical and ordinary Sanskrit usage, *bahiṣ-prajñaḥ* (Sanskrit: बहिष्प्रज्ञः) is an adjectival compound literally meaning "outwardly conscious," "having consciousness directed outward," or "cognizant of external objects." It combines the indeclinable particle *bahiḥ* ("out," "outside," "externally") with *prajñā* / *prajña* ("consciousness," "cognition," "awareness").

In Upaniṣadic literature and classical Vedāntic philosophy, most prominently in verse 3 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *bahiṣ-prajñaḥ* serves as a key technical term designating the cognitive mode of *Vaiśvānara* (or *Viśva*), the first quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*). It characterizes awareness oriented toward external physical objects (*sthūla-viṣaya*) during the waking state (*jāgrat-sthāna*), distinguishing it from dream consciousness (*antaḥ-prajñaḥ*, "inwardly conscious") and deep sleep (*prājña*, "unified mass of consciousness").

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *bahiṣ-prajñaḥ* is a Sanskrit possessive compound (*bahuvrīhi samāsa*) parsed as follows:

1. **First member:** *Bahiḥ* (बहिः), an indeclinable adverbial particle signifying "outside," "outwardly," or "externally."
2. **Second member:** *Prajñā* (प्रज्ञा) or *prajña* (प्रज्ञ), a noun derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *jñā* (ज्ञा, "to know" or "to cognize") prefixed by *pra-* (प्र-, "forth," "forward"), meaning "cognition," "consciousness," or "intelligence."
3. **Phonetic sandhi:** In compound formation, when the final visarga (*-ḥ*) of *bahiḥ* precedes the voiceless labial stop *p-* in *prajñā*, it undergoes visarga-sandhi transformation into the retroflex sibilant *-ṣ-*, resulting in *bahiṣ-prajña*.
4. **Grammatical form:** As a masculine nominative singular adjective (*bahiṣ-prajñaḥ*), it functions as a modifier agreeing with *prathamaḥ pādaḥ* ("the first quarter") or *Vaiśvānaraḥ* ("the universal waker").

## Bahiṣ-prajñaḥ in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 3) and its classical commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā* and Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*), *bahiṣ-prajñaḥ* plays an essential role in mapping the four quarters (*pāda*) of Consciousness.

### Textual Definition (Mantra 3)  

Verse 3 introduces the first quarter of the Self: "The first quarter is *Vaiśvānara*, whose sphere of activity is the waking state (*jāgrat-sthāna*), who is conscious of external objects (*bahiṣ-prajñaḥ*), who has seven limbs and nineteen mouths, and who is the experiencer of gross objects (*sthūla-bhuk*)."

### Doctrinal Role and Cognitive Architecture  

1. **External orientation (*bāhya-viṣaya*):** Advaita commentary explains that while Consciousness in its essential nature is unconditioned and non-different in all states, in the waking state it appears to move outward through ignorance (*avidyā*). The mind (*antaḥkaraṇa*) projects itself through the sense organs onto physical entities, giving rise to the illusion that objects exist independently outside the perceiver.
2. **Instruments of cognition:** *Vaiśvānara* experiences gross external objects through nineteen "mouths" or instruments: the five organs of perception (*jñānendriya*: ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose), five organs of action (*karmendriya*: speech, hands, feet, organ of excretion, organ of generation), five vital airs (*prāṇa*, *apāna*, *vyāna*, *udāna*, *samāna*), and four internal mental faculties (*manas*, *buddhi*, *ahaṅkāra*, *citta*).
3. **Contrast with other states:** *Bahiṣ-prajñaḥ* contrasts directly with *antaḥ-prajñaḥ* ("inwardly conscious"), which characterizes *Taijasa* in the dream state (*svapna*) where awareness apprehends subtle mental impressions (*sūkṣma-viṣaya*), and with *Prājña* in deep sleep (*suṣupti*), where both outward and inward cognitions subside into an undifferentiated mass of consciousness (*prajñānaghana*).
4. **Negation in Turīya (verse 7):** In mantra 7, the Upaniṣad defines Ultimate Reality (*Turīya*, the "Fourth") through systematic apophatic negation (*neti neti*), declaring that *Turīya* is "neither inwardly conscious (*na antaḥ-prajñam*) nor outwardly conscious (*na bahiṣ-prajñam*), nor conscious of both simultaneously (*na ubhayataḥ-prajñam*)." By negating *bahiṣ-prajñaḥ*, the text asserts that the waking state's outward subject-object duality is an illusory superimposition upon Pure Consciousness.
