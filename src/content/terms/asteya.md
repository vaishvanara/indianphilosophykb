Asteya is the third yama, translating to non-stealing, non-covetousness, or absolute integrity. On a basic level, it prohibits taking anything that does not belong to one without explicit permission, including material objects, money, or property. However, in the deeper context of yoga, asteya extends far beyond physical theft. It forbids intellectual theft (plagiarism or taking credit for others' work), emotional theft (manipulating others for personal gain), and energetic theft (taking more than one gives from society or nature). Asteya is rooted in the psychological state of abundance versus scarcity; stealing arises from avidyā (ignorance) and a deep-seated feeling of lack—the delusion that one must grab from others to be complete. Practicing asteya cultivates profound trust in the universe, honesty, and contentment (saṃtoṣa). Patañjali notes that when a practitioner is firmly established in non-stealing, all manner of wealth and abundance naturally gravitate toward them, as they no longer project a frequency of scarcity or greed.
