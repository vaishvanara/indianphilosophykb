Meaning: "they consider," "they deem," or "they regard," specifically in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 7), where sages regard the non-dual Self (*Turīya*) as the Fourth.

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 7)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

*Manyante* (Sanskrit: मन्यन्ते) is a Sanskrit verbal form meaning "they think," "they believe," "they deem," "they consider," "they regard," or "they hold of opinion." 

Lexically, the word denotes the active mental process of holding a conviction, forming a judgment, or assigning a specific designation or status to an entity.

Contextually within the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 7), *manyante* occurs in the climactic statement defining the ultimate reality of the Self (*Ātman*):

> *nāntaḥprajñaṁ na bahiṣprajñaṁ nobhayataḥprajñaṁ na prajñānaghanaṁ na prajñaṁ nāprajñam | adṛṣṭam avyavahāryam agrāhyam alakṣaṇam acintyam avyapadeśyam ekātmapratyayasāraṁ prapañcopaśamaṁ śāntaṁ śivam advaitaṁ caturthaṁ manyante sa ātmā sa vijñeyaḥ ||* (Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, 7)
> Not consciousness inward, not consciousness outward, not consciousness of both, not a mass of consciousness, not consciousness, and not unconsciousness. The fourth (*Turīya*) is considered as invisible, unrelated, ungraspable, indefinable, inconceivable, ineffable, the Essence of the consciousness of Selfhood and Oneness, the negation of all relative existence, peaceful, auspicious, and non-dual; that is the Self; that is to be realized.

In this passage, *manyante* functions as the key predicate linking the apophatic negations of empirical attributes to the positive designation of *Turīya* ("the Fourth"). The text clarifies that enlightened seers regard this non-dual, unconditioned Reality, which is devoid of internal consciousness (*antaḥprajña*), external consciousness (*bahiṣprajña*), or massed sentiency (*prajñānaghana*), as the "Fourth" (*caturtham*) relative to the three relative states of waking (*Viśva*), dream (*Taijasa*), and deep sleep (*Prājña*). As Śaṅkarācārya explains in his commentary, *manyante* here conveys that *Turīya* is not an additional numerical state, but the very essence of the non-dual Self (*Ātman*) recognized upon the cessation of illusory phenomena (*prapañcopaśama*).

## Etymology

Grammatically, *manyante* is a finite verb form (*tiṅanta*) functioning as the 3rd person plural present indicative *Ātmanepada* (*laṭ, prathama-puruṣa bahuvacana*) of the Sanskrit verbal root:

1. **Root**: *Man* (मन्, class 4 *Ātmanepada*), meaning "to think, believe, imagine, deem, consider, regard, or perceive."
2. **Conjugational formation**: As a verb belonging to the fourth conjugational class (*divādi-gaṇa*), the root *man-* takes the conjugational affix *-ya-* (*śyan* in Paninian grammar), forming the present verbal stem *manya-* (*man* + *ya*).
3. **Personal termination**: To the stem *manya-*, the 3rd person plural *Ātmanepada* primary ending *-nte* (present tense) is attached (*manya* + *ante* = *manyante*, "they think / consider").

Related nominal and participial derivatives from the same root *man* in classical Sanskrit literature include *mata* (past passive participle, "thought, considered, approved"), *mati* (feminine noun, "thought, intellect, opinion"), *manas* (neuter noun, "mind"), *saṃmata* ("agreed upon, respected"), and *mīmāṃsā* (desiderative noun, "investigation, reflection, inquiry").
