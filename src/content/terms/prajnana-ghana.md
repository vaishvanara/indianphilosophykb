**Prajñāna-Ghana**
Meaning: "a mass of consciousness"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

In the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, prajñāna-ghana denotes a unified mass of consciousness characteristic of deep sleep (suṣupti), where manifold perception ceases.

## Definition

The Sanskrit expression *prajñāna-ghana* (प्रज्ञानघन) is a compound formed by two primary elements: *prajñāna* (प्रज्ञान) and *ghana* (घन). The word *prajñāna* signifies consciousness, cognisance, or full knowledge, while *ghana* denotes a solid mass, aggregate, or dense shape. Combined literally, the expression signifies a "solid mass of consciousness" or "compacted awareness".

Idiomatically and contextually, *prajñāna-ghana* describes a state of undifferentiated awareness in which all pluralistic mental modifications (*vṛtti*), sensory impressions, and dualistic distinctions of subject and object dissolve into a unified whole. In Vedāntic metaphysics, it characterizes the causal state of the Self (*Prājña*) during deep dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*). In this state, consciousness is not absent or destroyed; rather, the diverse cognitions of the waking (*jāgrat*) and dream (*svapna*) states are withdrawn and consolidated into a single, homogeneous matrix of consciousness.

## Etymology

Morphologically, *prajñāna-ghana* (प्रज्ञानघन) is analyzed into two nominal components:
1. *Prajñāna* (प्रज्ञान): Formed from the verbal root *jñā* (ज्ञा, "to know") with the prefix *pra-* (प्र-, "forth" or "profound"), yielding the neuter noun *prajñāna*, meaning higher cognisance or consciousness.
2. *Ghana* (घन): Derived from the root *han* (हन्, "to strike" or "to solidify"), functioning as a noun meaning a dense mass, shape, or compact aggregate (*saṅghāta* or *mūrta*).

In Sanskrit grammar (*vyākaraṇa*), the compound is classified as a *karmadhāraya-samāsa* (descriptive determinative compound) or a *tatpuruṣa-samāsa*. Its structural expansion (*vigraha-vākya*) is expressed either as *prajñānam eva ghanaḥ* ("consciousness itself as a solid mass") or as *prajñānasya ghanaḥ* ("a mass consisting of consciousness"). Classical commentaries emphasize the particle *eva* ("verily" or "alone") to stress that no non-conscious or material element enters into this state, indicating pure, undifferentiated consciousness excluding external or internal mental objects. A parallel scriptural usage occurs in the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* with the compound *saindhava-ghana* ("a mass of salt"), where a lump of salt is uniform in taste inside and out; similarly, *ghana* indicates complete homogeneity without internal differentiation.

## prajñāna-ghana in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

The compound *prajñāna-ghana* (प्रज्ञानघन) occurs in Verse 5 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*:

> *yatra supto na kañcana kāmaṁ kāmayate na kañcana svapnaṁ paśyati tat suṣuptam / ekībhūtaḥ prajñānaghana evānandamayo hyānandabhuk cetomukhaḥ prājñas tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ //*

("That is the state of deep sleep wherein the sleeper does not desire any objects nor does he see any dream. The third quarter (*pāda*) is the *Prājña* whose sphere is deep sleep, in whom all experiences become unified, who is verily a mass of consciousness (*prajñānaghana*), who is full of bliss, who experiences bliss, and who is the doorway to cognition.")

Grammatically, *prajñānaghanaḥ* functions as a nominative singular masculine predicate qualifying *Prājñaḥ*, the third quarter (*tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ*) of the Ātman.

Contextually, the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* analyzes the totality of human experience across four quarters or states: the waking state (*Viśva*), the dream state (*Taijasa*), the deep sleep state (*Prājña*), and the transcendental state (*Turīya*). In verse 5, the Upaniṣad explains that during deep sleep (*suṣuptasthāna*), the dualistic experiences of the waking and dream states—consisting of mental forms and sense perceptions—reach a state of non-differentiation (*ekībhūta*). Just as daytime objects become enveloped into a single mass of darkness at night, all distinct cognitions merge into a single, concentrated mass of consciousness (*prajñānaghana*).

Philosophically, *prajñāna-ghana* represents the causal state (*bīja*) of consciousness. The surrounding epithets in verse 5 clarify its nature:
- *Ekībhūta* ("become unified"): The multiplicity of waking and dreaming mental modifications (*vṛtti*) resolves into an undifferentiated state.
- *Ānandamaya* ("endowed with bliss"): Free from the miseries and strain caused by subject-object duality, the mind experiences an abundance of peace.
- *Ānandabhuk* ("enjoyer of bliss"): It experiences the natural, effortless quietude of non-duality.
- *Cetomukha* ("doorway to consciousness"): It serves as the gateway through which consciousness transitions back into dream and waking states.

**Distinction Between Textual Layers:**
1. **Upaniṣadic Text**: The Upaniṣad uses *prajñāna-ghana* to characterize *Prājña*, the third quarter of Ātman presiding over deep sleep.
2. **Gauḍapāda's Kārikā**: Gauḍapāda clarifies that while *Prājña* is free from mis-apprehension of reality (*anyathāgrahaṇa* or dream/waking illusion), it remains bound by non-apprehension of reality (*agrahaṇa* or causal darkness). Thus, *Prājña* is associated with causal sleep (*bījanidrā*), whereas the fourth quarter (*Turīya*) transcends both non-apprehension and mis-apprehension.
3. **Śaṅkara's Commentary**: Śaṅkara notes that the particle *eva* in *prajñānaghana eva* emphasizes that in deep sleep there is no non-conscious or material entity present, but only consciousness itself unified into a mass. Śaṅkara also points out that while *Prājña* is endowed with bliss (*ānandamaya*), it is not the ultimate, unconditioned Bliss (*Turīya*), because it remains encased in nescience (*avidyā*).

## Interpretations and Disputes

In classical Advaita Vedānta, *prajñāna-ghana* (and its variant *vijñāna-ghana*) has been interpreted across different levels of reality (*vyāvahārika* versus *pāramārthika*):

1. **Relative / Causal State vs. Absolute Reality**:
In Śaṅkara's commentary on the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *prajñānaghana* in verse 5 specifically describes *Prājña*—the individual or cosmic consciousness conditioned by the causal body (*kāraṇa-śarīra*) of nescience (*avidyā*) during deep sleep. Here, *prajñānaghana* is non-differentiation due to the abeyance of mental modes, but it is not yet ultimate liberation (*mokṣa*), because the seeds of ignorance persist.
Conversely, in Śaṅkara's *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* (verses 237 and 562) and the *Pañcadaśī* of Svāmi Vidyāraṇya, *prajñāna-ghana* or *vijñāna-ghana* is frequently used as an essential definition (*svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*) of Para-Brahman or the unconditioned Self, signifying a pure, homogeneous mass of Consciousness free from all *upādhis* (limiting adjuncts).

2. **Translation Variations**:
Scholarly translators render *prajñānaghana* based on context:
- Swami Nikhilananda translates it as "a mass of all consciousness unified" or "a mass of consciousness entire" in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, and as "solid mass of intelligence" when translating *vijñānaghana* in the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad*.
- Śrī Ramana Maharshi and Śrī Muruganar gloss *prajñāna-ghana* as "full consciousness" or "homogeneous mass of consciousness".
- Academic works (such as Karl H. Potter's *Encyclopedia of Indian Philosophies*) highlight that *ghana* in *prajñānaghana* signifies a compact, unbroken totality, analogous to *saṅghāta* (mass/aggregate) or *mūrta* (dense form), excluding internal division.

3. **Relation to Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad (*saindhava-ghana* / *vijñānaghana*)**:
Commentators compare *prajñāna-ghana* with the *Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* text (II.iv.12 / IV.v.13), where the Self is described as *saindhava-ghana-vat*—like a lump of salt that has neither inside nor outside, but is entirely a mass of taste. Śaṅkara uses this Vedic parallel to show that *ghana* signifies the total absence of heterogeneous parts or external boundaries.
