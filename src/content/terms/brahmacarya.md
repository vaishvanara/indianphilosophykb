Brahmacarya is the fourth yama, traditionally translated as continence, chastity, celibacy, or walking in the path of the Divine (Brahman). While often interpreted narrowly in ancient monastic contexts as strict sexual abstinence, its broader and more comprehensive yogic definition involves the wise, mindful management and conservation of one's vital energy (ojas, prāṇa, and creative potential). It means not letting sensory desires and sexual urges scatter or dissipate the mind's focus and vitality. Practicing brahmacarya requires directing vital energy away from compulsive sense-gratification and toward higher spiritual aspirations, self-realization, and creative or meditative endeavors. It fosters self-mastery, inner purity, and emotional stability. Patañjali asserts that the sustained practice of brahmacarya confers vīrya-lābha—the attainment of immense physical and mental vigor, vitality, and spiritual power, enabling the practitioner to transcend ordinary limitations and sustain deep focus in yoga.
