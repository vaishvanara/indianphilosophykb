Meaning: "on account of merger, reabsorption, or dissolution; the ablative form of *apīti* used in Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad (verse 11) to explain the symbol *makāra* ('M')."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 11)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In Sanskrit grammar and lexicography, *apīteḥ* (Sanskrit: अपीतेः) is the ablative singular case form of the feminine abstract noun *apīti* (अपीति), derived from the verbal combination *api-i* ("to go into," "to merge," "to dissolve"). Lexically, *apīti* signifies merger, reabsorption, destruction, dissolution, or entry into a cause. The inflected ablative form translates literally as "from merger," "on account of reabsorption," or "due to dissolution."

In the eleventh verse of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the term appears within the phrase *tṛtīyā mātrā māter apīter vā*, meaning: "the third sound-measure (of *AUM* is *makāra*), on account of measuring or on account of merger/dissolution." Here, *apīteḥ* serves as one of the two explicit grounds of correspondence (*sāmānya*) between *makāra* (the third constituent letter 'M' of the sacred monosyllable *AUM*) and *Prājña* (the Self in the state of deep sleep, *suṣupti*).

In Śaṅkarācārya's commentary (*bhāṣya*), the ground of *apīti* ("merger" or "dissolution") is interpreted through a dual symbolic macrocosm-microcosm framework:

1. **Phonetic resolution:** When reciting *AUM*, the preceding vowels *akāra* ('A') and *ukāra* ('U') dissolve or merge into the closing labial sound *makāra* ('M'), making *makāra* the container or place of dissolution for the sound-symbol.
2. **Cosmic and experiential reabsorption:** Similarly, upon entering dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*), all gross waking experiences (*Viśva*) and subtle dream experiences (*Taijasa*) dissolve into the causal state of *Prājña* (or cosmic *Īśvara*).

Furthermore, the Upaniṣad establishes an exegesis connecting the ground *apīteḥ* with the spiritual fruit (*phala*) gained through meditation: he who contemplates *makāra* as *Prājña* on the ground of *apīti* "measures all this and becomes the place of merger/dissolution (*apītiś ca bhavati*)," realizing the causal substratum into which phenomenal multiplicity is resolved.

## Etymology

Etymologically, *apīteḥ* is a grammatically inflected form parsed according to classical Sanskrit nominal morphology:

1. **Verbal root and prefix:** Derived from the primary verbal root *i* (इ), meaning "to go," "to move," or "to enter," prefixed by the preverb *api-* (अपि-), which in Vedic and classical usage signifies "into," "unto," or "internal to" (*api* + *i* = *apī*, "to merge into" or "to be absorbed").
2. **Nominal suffixation:** Formed by appending the primary feminine action suffix *-ti* (*ktin-pratyaya*) to the combined verbal stem *apī-*, yielding the abstract noun *apīti* (*api* + *i* + *ti* = *apīti*), meaning "the act or state of merging into," "reabsorption," or "dissolution."
3. **Case inflection:** In the ablative singular case (*ṣaṣṭhī* / *pañcamī vibhakti*), the *i*-stem noun *apīti* inflects as *apīteḥ* ("from dissolution" / "on account of reabsorption"), functioning as an ablative of cause (*hetau pañcamī*) parallel to *māteḥ* ("on account of measuring").
4. **Recurrent base form:** In the second clause of *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 11), the base noun recurs in the nominative singular form as *apītiḥ*, which before the voiceless palatal stop *c-* in *ca* undergoes visarga-sandhi transformation into *apītiś*, yielding the combined textual reading *apītiś ca bhavati* ("and becomes the place of merger").
