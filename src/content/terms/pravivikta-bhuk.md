Meaning: "enjoyer or experiencer of subtle objects; the aspect of Consciousness (*Taijasa*) that experiences internal mental impressions during the dream state."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 4)](mandukyopanisat.md) 

---

## Definition

Literally, *pravivikta-bhuk* (प्रविविक्तभुक्) signifies an "enjoyer or experiencer of that which is fine, isolated, or subtle". In its primary philosophical and lexical context within the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (Mantra IV), the term designates the second quarter or aspect (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), known as *Taijasa* ("the luminous"), whose sphere of activity is the dream state (*svapna-sthāna*). 

While waking consciousness (*Viśva*) experiences gross, external material objects, *pravivikta-bhuk* refers to the consciousness that turns inward (*antaḥprajña*) to experience subtle objects (*pravivikta*). These subtle objects consist of internal mental representations, fancies, and impressions (*vāsanās*) derived from previous waking experiences, projected by the mind during sleep when external sense organs are inactive. Figuratively, the term denotes the subjective self-effulgent witness (*sākṣin*) that illumines and consumes these fine mental modifications as its objective field.

---

## Etymology

The Sanskrit term *pravivikta-bhuk* (nominative singular of the stem *pravivikta-bhuj*) is a compound noun and adjective. It is formed by joining the past passive participle *pravivikta* (प्रविविक्त) with the agentive root-noun suffix *-bhuk* (stem *-bhuj*).

The first component, *pravivikta*, is derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *vic* (विच्, "to sift, separate, distinguish, or discern") preceded by the prefixes *pra-* (प्र) and *vi-* (वि). With the past passive participle suffix *-ta*, *pravivikta* means "well-separated," "isolated," "sequestered," or "subtle". The second component, *-bhuk* (stem *-bhuj*), is an agent noun formed from the verbal root *bhuj* (भुज्, "to enjoy, eat, or experience"). Grammatically, the compound serves as a nominal predicate describing *Taijasa* in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*.
