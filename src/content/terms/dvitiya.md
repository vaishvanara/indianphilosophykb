Meaning: literally meaning "second," *dvitīya* in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* designates *Taijasa*, the second quarter (*pāda*) of *Ātman* in dream.

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 4, 10)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

As an ordinal numeral adjective, *dvitīya* (Sanskrit: द्वितीय) literally means "second" or "the second". Lexicographical authorities record its primary sense as "second", while also noting its nominal, adjectival, and grammatical extensions, including "a second time" (adverbial *dvitīyam*), "companion", "friend", "foe", "half" (when combined with *bhāga* or in the neuter *dvitīyam*), the feminine *dvitīyā* denoting a female companion or the second day of a lunar fortnight, and the accusative grammatical case.

In the specific textual setting of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 4), *dvitīya* occurs in the masculine nominative singular form *dvitīyaḥ*, modifying *pādaḥ* ("quarter" or "foot") in the technical phrase *taijaso dvitīyaḥ pādaḥ* ("*Taijasa* is the second quarter"). Within the Upaniṣad's fourfold framework (*catur-pāda*) analyzing the Self (*Ātman*), this second quarter corresponds to *Taijasa*, whose sphere of activity is the dream state (*svapna-sthāna*), who is conscious of internal mental impressions (*antaḥ-prajña*), equipped with seven limbs and nineteen mouths, and experiences subtle objects (*pravivikta-bhuk*).

In the broader philosophical discourse of Advaita Vedānta connected with the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *dvitīya* serves as the key reference point for defining *advaita* ("non-duality" or "without a second"), which establishes the complete absence of any second independent entity (*na dvitīyam*) apart from pure Consciousness. While empirical cognition experiences phenomenal duality (*dvaita*), Vedantic exegesis demonstrates that all second appearances are unreal manifestations (*māyā*), leaving only the non-dual Absolute Reality.

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *dvitīya* (द्वितीय) is an ordinal numeral adjective declined in all three genders (masculine *dvitīyaḥ*, feminine *dvitīyā*, neuter *dvitīyam*). It is derived from the cardinal numeral base *dvi-* (द्वि), meaning "two".

Classical Sanskrit grammarians classify *dvitīya* as an irregular ordinal formation derived by attaching the secondary *Taddhita* suffix *-tīya* (तीया) directly to the cardinal stem *dvi-*. In its contextual occurrence in verse 4 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the word is inflected in the nominative singular masculine form *dvitīyaḥ*, agreeing in gender, number, and case with the noun *pādaḥ*.
