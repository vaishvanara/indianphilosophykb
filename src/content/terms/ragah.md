---
title: "Rāgaḥ (रागः)"
devanagari: "रागः"
iast: "Rāgaḥ"
aliases:
  - "रागः"
  - "Ragah"
---

Rāgaḥ is the third kleśa, defined as the intense attraction, clinging, or thirst toward pleasant experiences, objects, or people. It arises naturally when the mind remembers a past pleasure and craves its repetition, binding happiness to external conditions. While enjoying pleasant things is a normal biological trait, rāga becomes an affliction when it turns into obsessive grasping, addiction, or compulsive dependency. The mind believes, "Only when I have this object, relationship, or status can I be happy," thereby making its inner peace hostage to transient worldly phenomena. Because all material things are impermanent, rāga inevitably leads to frustration, disappointment, and suffering (duḥkha) when the desired object changes, fades, or is lost. Yogic practice counters rāga through the cultivation of non-attachment (vairāgya) and contentment (saṃtoṣa), teaching the seeker to find stability within rather than grasping outward.
