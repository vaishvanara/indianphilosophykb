Meaning: "enjoyer or experiencer of bliss; an epithet of *Prājña* (the Self in deep sleep) in *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, experiencing unconditioned bliss in *suṣupti*."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In ordinary Sanskrit lexicography and grammar, *ānanda-bhuk* (Sanskrit: आनन्दभुक्, nominative singular of the compound stem *ānanda-bhuj*) literally translates to "bliss-enjoyer," "bliss-eater," or "experiencer of bliss." It combines *ānanda* ("bliss," "joy," or "happiness") with the root-noun suffix *-bhuk* / *-bhuj* ("enjoyer," "eater," or "one who experiences").

In its extended philosophical usage within Upanishadic literature, specifically in verse 5 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *ānanda-bhuk* serves as a technical epithet characterizing *Prājña*, the third quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), presiding over the state of dreamless deep sleep (*suṣupti-sthāna*). In this state, where all active dualistic mental projections and sense-perceptions subside into a unified mass of consciousness (*prajñānaghana*), the Self experiences an unconditioned, peaceful reflection of inherent bliss without the intervention of gross or subtle sense-objects.

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *ānanda-bhuk* is a Sanskrit agentive compound (*upapada-tatpuruṣa samāsa*) parsed as follows:

1. **First member:** *Ānanda* (आनन्द), a masculine noun derived from the verbal root *nand* ("to rejoice" or "to delight") prefixed by *ā-*, meaning "bliss," "joy," or "supreme happiness."
2. **Second member:** *-bhuk* (भुक्), the nominative singular form of the nominal root-noun *-bhuj* (भुज्), derived from the verbal root *bhuj* (भुज्), meaning "to eat," "to consume," "to enjoy," or "to experience."
3. **Grammatical function:** Grammatically, *ānanda-bhuk* functions as a masculine nominative singular adjectival noun ("he who enjoys bliss"). In verse 5 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, it modifies the masculine noun *prājñaḥ* ("the knowing Self in deep sleep").

## Ānanda-Bhuk in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 5) and its classical commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā*, Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*, Śrī Sadananda's *Vedāntasāra*, and Śrī Vidyāraṇya Svāmī's *Pañcadaśī*), *ānanda-bhuk* plays an essential role in defining the cognitive and experiential nature of *Prājña* in deep sleep (*suṣupti*).

### Textual locus (verse 5)

Verse 5 defines the third quarter of the Self: "That is the state of deep sleep wherein one asleep neither desires any object nor sees any dream. The third quarter is *Prājña*, whose sphere is deep sleep, in whom all experiences become unified, who is, verily, a mass of consciousness (*prajñānaghana*), who is full of bliss (*ānandamaya*), who experiences bliss (*ānanda-bhuk*), whose doorway is consciousness (*cetomukhaḥ*), and who is the door leading to the knowledge of dreaming and waking."

### Mechanism of enjoyment in deep sleep (suṣupti)

Advaita Vedānta exegesis explains how *Prājña* functions as *ānanda-bhuk*:

1. **Resolution of subject-object duality:** In the waking (*jāgrat*) and dream (*svapna*) states, Consciousness experiences gross and subtle objects through the dualistic, active modifications of the intellect (*antaḥkaraṇa-vṛtti*), resulting in fleeting, object-dependent pleasures (*viṣayānanda*). In deep sleep, the mind and sense organs fold back into their causal source, seed-ignorance (*kāraṇa-śarīra* / *mūla-avidyā*).
2. **Reflected bliss (*ānandābhāsa*):** Devoid of mental agitation (*vikalpa*) and objectification, *Prājña* experiences the tranquil reflection of *Brahman*'s intrinsic bliss through extremely subtle, non-differentiated modifications of nescience (*ajñāna-vṛtti*) illumined by Pure Consciousness (*caitanya*).

### Epistemological proof (*smarana*)

The reality of *Prājña* as *ānanda-bhuk* is established through the universal post-waking recollection: "I slept happily; I knew nothing" (*sukham aham asvāpsam na kiñcid avediṣam*). Because recollection presupposes prior direct experience, this memory confirms that an unconditioned, peaceful bliss was experienced during deep sleep by *Prājña* via subtle awareness.

### Distinction from Supreme Liberation (*Turīya*)

Advaita commentary emphasizes that while *Prājña* as *ānanda-bhuk* enjoys bliss free from mental grief, this state is not the ultimate transcendent Liberation (*mokṣa* or *Turīya*). In deep sleep, bliss remains veiled by causal ignorance (*avidyā*), containing latent impressions (*saṁskāra*) that impel the individual back into dream and waking states upon reawakening. In *Turīya* (the "Fourth"), this seed-ignorance is completely sublated by Self-knowledge (*jñāna*), revealing eternal, unconditioned Non-dual Bliss (*Nirguṇa Brahman*).

### Individual and cosmic parallelism

At the individual level (*vyaṣṭi*), the enjoyer of bliss in deep sleep is *Prājña*; at the cosmic level (*samaṣṭi*), the causal controller associated with the sheath of bliss (*ānandamaya-kośa*) is *Īśvara*. Both share the state of causal unity and bliss-enjoyment during deep sleep and cosmic dissolution.
