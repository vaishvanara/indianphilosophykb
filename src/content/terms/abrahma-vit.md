**Abrahma-Vit** 
Meaning: "person ignorant of Brahman"
From Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad (Verse 10)

**Translation by Swami Sharvananda** 
Sanskrit: अब्रह्मवित् 
Transliteration: abrahma-vit
Meaning: "person ignorant of Brahman"

**Translation by Monier Monier-Williams** 
Sanskrit: अ-ब्रह्म-विद्
Transliteration: A-brahma-vid
Meaning: "not knowing Brahma or the Supreme Spirit"

अब्रह्मवित् (abrahmavit) is a nominative singular form of the Sanskrit compound word अब्रह्मवित् (abrahmavit), which means "one who knows Brahman" or "a knower of Brahman".

In various contexts, अब्रह्मवित् can be translated to:

- A knower of Brahman
- A sage
- A wise one
- A seer
- A spiritual adept

In Hinduism and Buddhism, Brahman refers to the ultimate reality, the unchanging and eternal essence of the universe. A person who knows Brahman is considered to be a wise and enlightened individual who has gained a deep understanding of the nature of reality.

For example, in the Bhagavad Gita (5.18), it is written:

अब्रह्मवित् प्रजानां च

Abrahmavit prajānāṃ ca

Which translates to: "And the knower of Brahman is among the people."
