Meaning: "enjoyer," "eater," or "experiencer" (occurring as a nominal root suffix in Vedantic compounds).

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verses 3, 4, 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Definition

In Sanskrit lexicography, *bhug* (भुग्) is a contextual euphonic variation of the nominal suffix *bhuj* (भुज्), denoting an "eater," "enjoyer," "possessor," or "experiencer". In general usage and Vedantic literature, the term functions as a terminal compound element to designate an agent who partakes of or experiences specific phenomena or objects.

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verses 3–5) and Gauḍapāda's *Kārikā* with Śaṅkarācārya's commentary, *bhug* appears in three technical compound designations that describe the experiencing Self (*Ātman*) across the three states of consciousness (*avasthās*):

1. **Sthūla-bhug** (स्थूलभुग्): "Enjoyer of gross objects." This term qualifies *Vaiśvānara* (or *Viśva*), the Self in the waking state (*jāgrat*), which experiences external physical objects through the outward-directed sensory organs.
2. **Pravivikta-bhug** (प्रविविक्तभुग्): "Enjoyer of subtle objects." This term qualifies *Taijasa*, the Self in the dream state (*svapna*), which experiences subtle internal mental impressions (*vāsanās*) created by the mind.
3. **Ānanda-bhug** (आनन्दभुग्): "Enjoyer of bliss." This term qualifies *Prājña*, the Self in deep dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*), where empirical dualities merge into undifferentiated consciousness, experiencing unmanifest bliss free from mental effort.

## Etymology

Grammatically, *bhug* functions as a primary nominal suffix (*kṛt-pratyaya*) attached to verbal bases to form compound nouns or adjectives. It is derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *bhuj* (भुज्), which belongs primarily to the seventh conjugational class (*rudhādi-gaṇa*, present stem *bhunañj* / *bhuñj* / *bhunaj*) with secondary forms in the 6th and 1st classes, meaning "to enjoy," "to eat," "to consume," or "to experience".

The underlying nominal stem is *bhuj*. Under standard Sanskrit phonological rules (*sandhi*) and nominal declension, a stem-final palatal consonant *-j* (ज) de-palatalizes to the velar stop *-k* (क्) at the end of an uninflected stem (yielding the nominative form *bhuk*). When followed by a soft or voiced sound in compound formation, the unvoiced velar *-k* undergoes regressive voicing assimilation to become the voiced velar stop *-g* (ग्), producing the final form *bhug*.
