Prāṇa (specifically as one of the pañca-prāṇas) is the vital force governing intake, inspiration, and respiration. Located primarily in the chest, heart, and head region, it directs the inward movement of air, food, sensory impressions, and subtle thoughts. Prāṇa is the primary driving energy that sustains life, capturing oxygen from the atmosphere and nourishment from the environment, "appropriating" them into the biological system. Energetically, it feeds the heart and brain, fueling mental clarity, enthusiasm, and sensory perception. When balanced, prāṇa provides vitality and steady, deep breathing; when depleted or disturbed, it results in fatigue, respiratory issues, and anxiety. In yogic practice, through prāṇāyāma (breath control), practitioners consciously direct and expand this vital current, harmonizing the heart-mind complex and awakening higher states of awareness.

# Gopi Krishna (Kundalini - The Evolutionary Energy in Man)

Prana as a medium for the activity of thought and transference of sensations and impulses in living organisms and as a normally imperceptible cosmic substance present in every formation of matter in terms of the classifications made by Hindu cosmologists in earth, water, air, fire and ether, is an established fact, verifiable by the practice of Yoga when undertaken by the right type of man on proper lines. According to these beliefs, Prana is not matter, nor is it mind or intelligence or consciousness, but rather an inseparable part of the cosmic energy or Shakti which resides in all of them and is the driving force behind all cosmic phenomena, as force in matter and vitality in living organisms; in short, it is the medium by which the cosmic intelligence conducts the unimaginably vast activity of this stupendous world, by which it creates, maintains, and destroys the gigantic globular formations burning ceaselessly in space as well as the tiny microbes, both malignant and beneficent, filling every part of the earth. In other words, Shakti, when applied to inorganic matter, is force and when to the organic plane, life, the two being different aspects of the creative cosmic energy operating in both the inorganic and organic planes. For the sake of convenience and to avoid confusion, the term Prana or Prana-Shakti is generally applied to that aspect of the cosmic energy which operates in the organic sphere, as nervous impulse and vitality, while the generic name Shakti is applied to every form of energy, animate and inanimate; in brief, to the creative and active aspect of the Reality.

Prana is merely the life energy by which divinity brings into existence the organic kingdoms and acts on the organic structures, as it creates and acts on the universe by means of physical energy. It is not the reality as sunshine is not the sun, and yet is essentially a part of it, assuming different shapes and appearances, entering into countless types of formations, building persistently the units or bricks to create the complicated organic structures in the same way that physical energy starts with electrons, protons, and atoms to raise the mighty edifice of the universe, all its activity governed by eternal laws as rigid and universal as the laws which rule the physical world.

# Prāṇa in Yoga Sutras & Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa

Both Patanjali’s _Yoga Sutras_ and Swami Pūrṇānanda’s _Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa_ discuss _prāṇa_, but they approach it from fundamentally different angles:

- **Patanjali's _Yoga Sutras_** (Classical Raja Yoga) treats _prāṇa_ as the **vital breath and life force**, focusing primarily on its regulation (_prāṇāyāma_) to still the mind for meditation.
- **The _Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa_** (Tantric Kundalini Yoga) treats _prāṇa_ as an **energetic vehicle** that flows through subtle channels (_nāḍīs_) and carries _Kundalī-Śakti_ upward through the _chakras_.

Here are the key verses in each text that explicitly discuss _prāṇa_.

## 1. Verses in Patanjali's _Yoga Sutras_

In the _Yoga Sutras_, _prāṇa_ appears primarily in Chapter 1 (_Samādhi Pāda_), Chapter 2 (_Sādhana Pāda_), and Chapter 3 (_Vibhūti Pāda_).  

### **A. Regulation of the Vital Breath (Pranayama)**

- **Sutra I.34:**
    > **प्रच्छर्दनविधारणाभ्यां वा प्राणस्य ॥ ३४ ॥**
    > 
    > _pracchardana-vidhāraṇābhyāṁ vā prāṇasya_
    > 
    > **Translation:** "Or [the mind is clarified] by the exhalation (_pracchardana_) and retention (_vidhāraṇa_) of _prāṇa_ (breath)."

- **Sutra II.49 (Definition of Pranayama):**
    > **तस्मिन् सति श्वासप्रश्वासयोर्गतिविच्छेदः प्राणायामः ॥ ४९ ॥**
    > 
    > _tasmin sati śvāsa-praśvāsayor gati-vicchedaḥ prāṇāyāmaḥ_
    > 
    > **Translation:** "That [_āsana_] being accomplished, _prāṇāyāma_ is the cessation or regulation of the flow of inbreath (_śvāsa_) and outbreath (_praśvāsa_)."

- **Sutra II.50 & II.51:**
    
    Patanjali further analyzes _prāṇa_ in terms of external movement (_bāhya_), internal movement (_ābhyantara_), motionless retention (_stambha_), and the fourth state beyond external/internal limits.

### **B. Sub-Pranas (Vayus) and Mastery Over Energy**

In Chapter 3, Patanjali discusses specific manifestations (_vāyus_) of _prāṇa_ within the body and the mastery gained through focus (_saṁyama_):

- **Sutra III.38 / III.39 (Mastery over Udāna):**
    > **उदानजयाज्जलपङ्ककण्टकादिष्वसङ्ग उत्क्रान्तिश्च ॥ ३९ ॥**
    > 
    > _udāna-jayāj jala-paṅka-kaṇṭakādiṣv asaṅga utkrāntiś ca_
    > 
    > **Translation:** "By mastery over _udāna_ (the upward-flowing vāyu/prāṇa), one gains non-attachment/levitation over water, mud, thorns, and the ability to exit the body at death."

- **Sutra III.39 / III.40 (Mastery over Samāna):**
    > **समानजयाज्ज्वलनात् ॥ ४० ॥**
    > 
    > _samāna-jayāj jvalanam_
    > 
    > **Translation:** "By mastery over _samāna_ (the equalizing vāyu/prāṇa in the navel center), glowing radiance or inner fire is kindled."

## 2. Verses in the _Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa_

In the _Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa_ (16th century CE), _prāṇa_ is woven throughout the entire 55-verse text, specifically in relation to the **three main channels (_Iḍā_, _Piṅgalā_, _Suṣumnā_)**, the **heart center (_Anāhata_)**, and the **upward movement of Kundalini**.

### **A. Prana Flowing through the Nadis**

- **Verse 1 (The Energy Channels):**
    
    Describes the central channel (_Suṣumnā_) and its relationship to _Iḍā_ (moon/left) and _Piṅgalā_ (sun/right), through which _prāṇa_ moves.
    
    > _"In the space outside the spine... stand the two Nāḍīs, Śaśī (Iḍā) and Mihira (Piṅgalā)... The Suṣumnā... extends from the middle of the Kanda to the Head."_

- **Verse 3 (The Lower Center and Prana):**
    
    Refers to the lower region near the _Muladhara Chakra_, where the vital air (_Prāṇa-vāyu_) resides alongside the dormant _Kundalini_.


### **B. Prana in the Heart Center (Anahata Chakra)**

- **Verse 22 (The Seat of Prana / Hridaya):**
    
    The _Ṣaṭ-Cakra-Nirūpaṇa_ explicitly locates the primary dwelling place of the _Prāṇa Vāyu_ in the 12-petaled _Anāhata Chakra_ (heart center).

    > _"Here [in the Anāhata] dwells the Vāyu-Bīja ('Yaṁ')... Here also abides the Prāṇa-Vāyu, like a swan (_haṁsa_), protecting the jīva (individual soul)."_


### **C. Prana and Kundalini Piercing the Chakras (Shat-Chakra Bheda)**

- **Verses 50–52 (Merging Prana with Kundalini):**
    
    These culmination verses detail how the practitioner uses the _Prāṇa-vāyu_ to awaken Kundalini and carry her upward through all six chakras to the _Sahasrāra_ (crown center):

    > **Verse 50:** _"The wise Yogi should lead Kuṇḍalinī, along with the Jīva and the Prāṇa-Vāyu, through the central channel (Suṣumnā) into the highest lotus..."_
    > 
    > **Verse 51–52:** Describes how _prāṇa_ and mind (_manas_) are merged into Supreme Consciousness (_Brahman_) at the crown, causing all individual vitality to dissolve into total liberation.
