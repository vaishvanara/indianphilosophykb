**Īśvara**
Meaning: "Lord"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *Īśvara* (as *sarveśvaraḥ*) denotes the Lord of all and Inner Controller presiding over deep sleep (*suṣupti*) as the causal source.

## Definition

In general Sanskrit usage and lexicography, *īśvara* (ईश्वर) signifies "lord," "master," "ruler," "king," or "Supreme God". Derived from the verbal root √*īś* ("to rule," "to possess," "to command"), it literally means "one who is capable" or "the powerful ruler".

In the specific textual setting of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* and its exegesis, *Īśvara* appears within the compound *sarveśvaraḥ* (सर्वेश्वरः, "the Lord of all") in Mantra 6. Contextually, *Īśvara* (or *sarveśvara*) represents the third quarter (*tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ*) of the Self (*Ātman*), identified with *prājña* operating in the sphere of deep sleep (*suṣuptasthāna*). Here, the term does not imply a distant, extra-cosmic personal deity, but rather the causal consciousness (*kāraṇa-rūpa*) in which all physical and mental manifestations are unified into a seed state (*ekībhūta*) and from which the entire universe originates and into which it dissolves.

In Advaita Vedānta exegesis, *Īśvara* is defined as Pure Consciousness (*Brahman*) associated with or conditioned by cosmic ignorance or illusion (*māyā* / *ajñāna*). Unlike individual souls (*jīvas*) who are bound and deluded by ignorance, *Īśvara* controls *māyā* and possesses attributes such as omniscience (*sarvajñatva*), all-controlling power (*sarva-niyantṛtva*), and universal lordship (*aiśvarya*).

## Etymology

Grammatically, *īśvara* (ईश्वर) is a masculine nominal noun/adjective derived from the primary verbal root √*īś* (ईश्), belonging to the second conjugational class (*adādigaṇa*), meaning "to rule," "to possess," "to master," or "to be capable of".

The word formation involves:
- The verbal root √*īś* (ईश्) + the primary nominal suffix *-vara* (*kvarap* or *vara-pratyaya*), used to form agent nouns or adjectives indicating an inherent quality or capability ("one who naturally rules or is powerful").
- When combined with the universal adjective *sarva* (all, whole) in Mantra 6 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, it forms the *Karmadhāraya* compound *sarveśvaraḥ* (सर्वेश्वरः):
  - *sarveṣām īśvaraḥ = sarveśvaraḥ* ("the Lord or Ruler of all").
- In Mantra 6, *sarveśvaraḥ* is inflected in the nominative singular masculine form (*sarveśvaraḥ*), functioning adjectivally in agreement with *eṣaḥ* (referring to *prājña*).

## Īśvara in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the textual framework of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the concept of *Īśvara* is introduced in Mantra 6 through the compound epithet *sarveśvaraḥ*:

> **Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, Mantra 6**:
> *eṣa sarveśvara eṣa sarvajña eṣo'ntaryāmy eṣa yoniḥ sarvasya prabhavāpyayau hi bhūtānām // 6 //*
> ("This is the Lord of all; this is the all-knowing; this is the inner controller; this is the source of all, the origin and dissolution of beings.")

Grammatical Function and Contextual Meaning:
Grammatically, *sarveśvaraḥ* operates as a nominative singular masculine predicate adjective modifying *eṣaḥ*, which refers back to *prājña*, the third quarter (*tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ*) described in Mantra 5. The surrounding text of Mantras 5 and 6 sets up a precise structural hierarchy within the fourfold framework (*catur-pāda*) of *Ātman*:
- Mantra 5 characterizes *prājña* in deep sleep (*suṣuptasthāna*) as unified (*ekībhūtaḥ*), a mass of consciousness (*prajñāna-ghanaḥ*), full of bliss (*ānanda-mayaḥ*), and the doorway to cognition (*ceto-mukhaḥ*).
- Mantra 6 expands *prājña* from an individual sleep state into its macrocosmic and cosmic dimension, defining it through five attributes: *sarveśvaraḥ* (Lord of all), *sarvajñaḥ* (knower of all), *antaryāmī* (inner controller), *yoniḥ sarvasya* (womb/source of all), and *prabhavāpyayau hi bhūtānām* (the origin and dissolution of all beings).

Philosophical Significance and Exegetical Distinctions:
- **Upaniṣad Wording vs. Gauḍapāda's Kārikā**: The Upaniṣad explicitly identifies *prājña* in deep sleep as *sarveśvaraḥ*. Gauḍapāda in his *Kārikā* (I.10) notes that *īśāna* (the Lord) in its ultimate sense is *turīya* (the Fourth), where miseries cease (*duḥkha-kṣaya*) and non-duality reigns. Gauḍapāda clarifies that while *prājña* (as *sarveśvara*) is bound by cause (*kāraṇa-baddha*) due to the non-apprehension of reality (*tattva-agrahaṇa*), *turīya* is free from both cause and effect (*kārya-kāraṇa-baddha*).
- **Śaṅkarācārya's Commentary**: Śaṅkara explains in his *Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya* that *sarveśvaraḥ* means the ruler of the entire physical and super-physical universe. Śaṅkara stresses that this Lordship (*īśvaratva*) is not an external, extra-cosmic creator as dualistic schools hold, but is the inner controller (*antaryāmī*) that directs all beings from within.
- **Later Advaita Synthesis (Vedāntasāra & Pañcadaśī)**: In post-Śaṅkara treatises such as Sadānanda's *Vedāntasāra* (§ 38) and Vidyāraṇya's *Pañcadaśī* (I.24–28, VI.157–158), *prājña* (microcosmic) and *Īśvara* (macrocosmic) are identified as the same Consciousness conditioned by the causal sheath / cosmic ignorance (*ānandamaya-kośa* / *ajñāna*).

## Interpretations and Disputes

Significant differences in interpretation regarding *Īśvara* in the *Māṇḍūkya* context are documented across philosophical traditions and modern scholarship:

1. **Saguna Brahman vs. Nirguna Brahman in Advaita**:
   - In Advaita Vedānta, *Īśvara* is recognized as *Saguṇa Brahman* (Brahman with attributes) or the Absolute conditioned by *māyā* / *ajñāna*. While *Īśvara* is omniscient, omnipotent, and the material/efficient cause of the universe on the empirical plane (*vyavahāra*), Advaitins maintain that from the transcendent standpoint (*paramārtha*), *Īśvara* is a phenomenal appearance (*kalpanā*) that merges into unconditioned *Nirguṇa Brahman* (*turīya*) upon ultimate realization.
2. **Dualistic vs. Non-Dualistic Views of Creation and Rulership**:
   - Śaṅkarācārya explicitly refutes dualistic views (*syād anyeṣāṃ mataṃ*) that treat *Īśvara* as an extra-cosmic agent distinct from the world and individual souls. Śaṅkara asserts that *Īśvara* is both the material and efficient cause (*abhinna-nimittopādāna-kāraṇa*), operating as the non-different Inner Ruler (*antaryāmī*).
3. **The Relationship Between Prājña, Īśvara, and Turīya**:
   - Classical commentators debate the status of *prājña* in Mantra 6. While *prājña* in deep sleep is *ceto-mukhaḥ* and *sarveśvaraḥ*, it carries the seed of potential duality (*bīja-nidrā* / *kāraṇa-baddha*). Some modern interpreters confuse *Īśvara* with *turīya*, but Advaita Vedānta maintains a strict distinction: *Īśvara* is the Lord of the causal state (*suṣupti* / *māyā*), whereas *turīya* is the non-dual Absolute beyond all causal and effectual conditions.
