Meaning: "not consciousness outward" or "not conscious of external objects," negating waking awareness in defining *Turīya* (the Fourth state).

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 7)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

The expression *na bahiṣ prajñaṃ* combines the negative particle *na* ("not") with the compound adjective *bahiṣ-prajñam* ("externally conscious"). In Sanskrit syntactic structure, *bahiṣ-prajña* describes an entity whose cognitive awareness is directed toward external sensory objects.

Literally, the phrase signifies "not having outward-directed consciousness". Idiomatically, it denotes the complete negation of empirical waking awareness (*jāgarita-sthāna*), wherein consciousness appears related to outward physical objects through the sense organs. 

In the textual context of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the phrase functions negatively to characterize the transcendental Self (*Turīya*) by asserting that pure Consciousness is intrinsically unconditioned by external objective perceptions.

---

## Etymology

The expression *na bahiṣ prajñaṃ* comprises three morphological components: the negative particle *na* ("not"), the indeclinable prefix/adverb *bahis* ("outward" or "outside"), and the nominal stem *prajñā* ("consciousness" or "cognition"). Combining *bahis* and *prajñā* forms the possessive exocentric compound (*bahuvrīhi samāsa*) *bahiṣ-prajña*. The established compound expansion (*vigraha-vākya*) for this *Bahuvrīhi* form is *bahiḥ prajñā yasya saḥ* ("he whose consciousness is directed outward"). 

Grammatically, under Sanskrit euphonic combination rules (*sandhi*), the final sibilant/visarga of *bahis* transforms into the retroflex sibilant *ṣ* before the voiceless labial stop *p* when preceded by the vowel *i*. The neuter nominative singular form *bahiṣ-prajñam* agrees grammatically with the implicit subject representing the Fourth quarter (*turīya-pāda*) or *Brahman*. The independent negative particle *na* modifies the compound phrase externally without forming a single compound word, providing explicit clausal negation.

---

## Na bahiṣ prajñaṃ in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

The phrase *na bahiṣ prajñaṃ* occurs in verse 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, where it serves as an essential negative attribute in describing *Turīya*, the non-dual Fourth quarter of the Self. Grammatically, it functions as a nominative neuter predicate describing *Turīya*. 

In the structural progression of the *Upaniṣad*, verse 3 defines the waking state (*jāgarita-sthāna*), embodied as *Vaiśvānara*, as *bahiṣ-prajñaḥ* ("externally conscious") because it perceives gross physical objects through nineteen instruments of experience. Verse 4 defines the dream state (*svapna-sthāna*), embodied as *Taijasa*, as *antaḥprajñaḥ* ("internally conscious") because it cognizes subtle mental impressions. Verse 5 defines deep sleep (*suṣupta-sthāna*), embodied as *prājña*, as *prajñāna-ghanaḥ* ("a mass of unified consciousness").

Verse 7 systematically sublates each of these conditioned states by declaring *Turīya* to be *na antaḥ-prajñam* (not internally conscious), *na bahiṣ-prajñam* (not externally conscious), *na ubhayataḥ-prajñam* (not conscious of both), *na prajñāna-ghanam* (not a mass of consciousness), *na prajñam* (not simple cognition), and *na aprajñam* (not unconscious). By explicitly stating *na bahiṣ-prajñam*, the *Upaniṣad* denies that the Self in its true nature possesses the outward-directed cognition characteristic of the waking state.

In Gauḍapāda's *Kārikā* (*Āgama Prakaraṇa*) and Śaṅkara's commentary (*bhāṣya*), this negation is central to Advaita Vedānta philosophy. Śaṅkara explains that *bahiṣprajñatva* is an illusion caused by ignorance (*avidyā*), which superimposes external objects onto pure Consciousness. Negating outward consciousness removes the limiting adjunct (*upādhi*) of the waking state without reducing *Turīya* to a mere void (*śūnya*). Instead, *Turīya* is realized as the immutable, non-dual substratum (*adhiṣṭhāna*) underlying all three states of experience.
