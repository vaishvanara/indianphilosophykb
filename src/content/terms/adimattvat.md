## Definition

*Ādimattvāt* (Sanskrit: आदिमत्त्वात्; IAST: *ādimattvāt*) is a technical logical term in classical Sanskrit lexical and philosophical literature, meaning "from the fact of having a beginning" or "because of possessing an origin."

In Indian philosophical traditions, particularly Nyāya, Vaiśeṣika, and Vedānta, the term functions primarily as a formal logical reason (*hetu*) or causal ground in syllogistic inference (*anumāna*). It is employed to demonstrate that a given subject or entity is non-eternal (*anitya*), composite, or an effect (*kārya*). 

In classical dialectics, whatever possesses a beginning (*ādimat*) is inherently subject to decay and destruction (*antavat*), making *ādimattvāt* a key premise in arguments establishing the impermanence of created phenomena in contrast to uncaused, beginningless (*anādi*) reality.

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *Ādimattvāt* is the ablative singular case form of the neuter abstract noun *ādimattva* (आदिमत्त्व, "the state or quality of having a beginning"). The term is formed through the following morphological derivation:

1. The nominal base *ādi* (आदि, masculine, meaning "beginning" or "origin"), derived from the Sanskrit prefix *ā-* (towards, taking in hand) combined with the verbal root *dā* (to give, take, or hold) and the nominal suffix *-i*.
2. The possessive nominal suffix *-mat* (*matup*), added to *ādi* to create the adjective *ādimat* (आदिमत्, "possessing a beginning" or "having an origin").
3. The abstract nominal suffix *-tva*, appended to *ādimat* to form the abstract noun *ādimattva*.

The inflectional ablative ending *-āt* (*-tvāt*) expresses cause, source, or logical reason, syntactically functioning as "from the fact of..." or "because of...".
