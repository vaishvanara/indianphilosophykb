Meaning: "of this, his, its, or of him; genitive singular masculine/neuter form of the demonstrative pronoun *idam* ('this') in Sanskrit."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 10)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In lexical and ordinary Sanskrit usage, *asya* (Sanskrit: अस्य) is the genitive singular masculine and neuter form of the proximate demonstrative pronoun *idam* (इदम्, "this"). It translates literally as "of this," "his," "its," or "of him." In Sanskrit grammar, the genitive case (*ṣaṣṭhī vibhakti*) expresses possession, relation, origin, or qualification, connecting the pronominal referent to a noun or adjective in the sentence.

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* and its classical commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā* and Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*), *asya* functions both as a possessive demonstrative pronoun and as a relational pointer within the exposition of *AUM*, the four quarters (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), and the spiritual fruits (*phala*) of Self-knowledge:

1. **Scriptural occurrence (verse 10):** In verse 10 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the term occurs in external vowel sandhi within the statement *nāsyābrahmavit kule bhavati* (parsed as *na* + *asya* + *abrahmavit* + *kule* + *bhavati*). Here, *asya* means "of him" or "his," referring to the spiritual aspirant who knows and meditates on the second sound-measure (*ukāra*) of *AUM* as *Taijasa*: "In his family, no one ignorant of Brahman is born."
2. **Exegetical and commentary usage:** Throughout the commentaries of Gauḍapāda and Śaṅkarācārya, *asya* is frequently used in prose explanations to signify "of this (Self)" (*asya ātmanaḥ*), "of this universe" (*asya jagataḥ*), or "of this sound-measure" (*asya mātrāyāḥ*), denoting the relationship between the transcendent, unconditioned Absolute and its phenomenal appearances.

## Etymology

Etymologically, *asya* is a grammatically inflected pronoun parsed according to classical Sanskrit morphology:

1. **Base and stem:** The primary demonstrative pronominal base is *idam* (इदम्, nominative singular neuter) or *ayam* (अयम्, nominative singular masculine), signifying "this" (indicating proximity to the speaker). In declension, the base switches to the suppletive stem *a-* for oblique cases.
2. **Case ending:** The genitive singular case termination for masculine and neuter pronominal stems in Sanskrit is *-sya* (स्य).
3. **Combination:** Appending the genitive suffix *-sya* to the pronominal stem *a-* produces the inflected form *asya* (*a* + *sya* = *asya*), functioning as a masculine or neuter genitive modifier ("of this").
4. **Sandhi rules:** In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 10), the initial short vowel *a-* of *asya* combines with the preceding negative particle *na* ("not") via *dīrgha-sandhi* (*na* + *asya* = *nāsya*). The final short vowel *-a* of *asya* subsequently merges with the initial *a-* of *abrahmavit* ("knower of non-Brahman"), yielding *nāsyābrahmavit*.
