Meaning: "of beings," "of created entities," "of elemental forces," or "of all existing things" (genitive plural form of *bhūta*).

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 6)](mandukyopanisat.md)
 
---

## Definition

In Sanskrit lexicography and grammar, *bhūtānām* (Sanskrit: भूतानाम्) is the genitive plural inflection of the participial nominal stem *bhūta* (भूत), which denotes "beings," "created entities," "living creatures," or "gross physical elements". 

Depending on textual context, the underlying stem *bhūta* encompasses all manifestation, ranging from individual living entities and divine beings to the five gross physical elements (*pañca-bhūta*: earth, water, fire, air, and space). The genitive plural termination signifies possession, relationship, or origin, translating literally as "of beings," "of created entities," or "belonging to all existing things".

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 6), *bhūtānām* occurs in a pivotal theological passage defining the cosmic nature of *Prājña*, the Self in the state of deep sleep (*suṣupti*), identified macrocosmically as *Īśvara* (the Lord of all), *Sarvajña* (the Omniscient), and *Antaryāmin* (the Inner Controller):

> *eṣa sarveśvara eṣa sarvajña eṣo'ntaryāmy eṣa yoniḥ sarvasya prabhavāpyayau hi bhūtānām*

Within this verse, *bhūtānām* forms part of the compound clause *prabhavāpyayau hi bhūtānām* ("the origin and dissolution of all beings"). Śaṅkarācārya explains in his commentary that the Supreme Lord, as the unmanifested causal source (*Prāṇa*), is the origin from which all living beings and positive entities emanate during creation and into which they return at the time of cosmic dissolution (*pralaya*).

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *bhūtānām* (Sanskrit: भूतानाम्) is the inflected genitive plural form of the nominal/participial stem *bhūta* (भूत) in the masculine or neuter gender.

1. **Verbal root**: The stem originates from the Sanskrit verbal root *bhū* (भू, 1st conjugational class *bhvādi-gaṇa*), meaning "to be," "to become," "to exist," or "to arise".
2. **Participial formation**: The participial stem *bhūta* is formed by appending the primary past passive participial suffix *-ta* (*kta*) directly to the root *bhū* without internal vowel change, producing *bhū-ta*, literally "that which has come into existence" or "a created being".
3. **Declensional inflection**: Under standard Sanskrit nominal declension for stems ending in short *-a*, the genitive plural case termination is formed by lengthening the stem vowel *-a* to *-ā-*, inserting the euphonic nasal augment *-n-*, and adding the genitive case suffix *-ām*, yielding the final inflected form *bhūtā-n-ām*.
