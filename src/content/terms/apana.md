Apāna is the downward and outward-moving vital air, responsible for the elimination, rejection, and expulsion of waste from the body. Located primarily in the lower abdomen, pelvic region, and anus, apāna governs the elimination of feces, urine, menstrual blood, and toxins, as well as the expulsion of a child during birth and the physical act of ejaculation. Physically, it manages the downward grounding force and stability of the lower body. Energetically, apāna is also responsible for grounding the mind and clearing out psychological garbage or emotional toxicity. In yogic physiology (especially Kuṇḍalinī yoga), spiritual evolution often involves harnessing prāṇa (upward) and apāna (downward) and uniting them at the navel center (samāna or maṇipūra chakra) to awaken latent spiritual energy and redirect it up the central channel (suṣumnā).
