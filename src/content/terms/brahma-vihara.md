**Brahma-Vihāra** 

*Brahma-vihāra* (literally translating to "Divine Abidings" or "Sublime Attitudes") refers to a quartet of foundational virtues in Indian contemplative traditions, highly prominent in both Yoga and Buddhist ethics. Often referred to as the *Apramāṇas* (the Immeasurables), these mental states are cultivated to expand the heart infinitely toward all living beings without discrimination. They consist of four core qualities:

1. **Maitrī (Loving-kindness):** The unconditional, benevolent wish for the happiness, well-being, and welfare of all sentient beings, actively dissolving the psychological barrier between "self" and "other."
2. **Karuṇā (Compassion):** A heartfelt, empathetic response to the suffering of others, characterized by a deep desire to alleviate pain and distress. Unlike mere pity, true *karuṇā* moves the practitioner toward active, loving alleviation.
3. **Muditā (Sympathetic or Altruistic Joy):** The practice of genuinely rejoicing in the success, prosperity, good fortune, and happiness of others. It acts as the direct psychological antidote to envy, jealousy, and bitterness.
4. **Upekṣā (Equanimity):** The cultivation of mental stability, calmness, and impartiality amidst the fluctuating, dualistic conditions of life—such as pleasure and pain, praise and blame, or success and failure. It is not cold indifference, but a deeply anchored, wise balance.

Practicing the *Brahma-vihāras* systematically purifies the mind of toxic emotional defilements (*kleśas*) like anger, malice, resentment, and bias. By dwelling in these divine states, a practitioner transforms their inner landscape, fosters boundless emotional resilience, paves the way for deep meditative concentration (*samādhi*), and aligns their daily existence with universal harmony and unconditional love.

---

Meaning: “divine state; 'abiding or dwelling in the Absolute (Brahman)”
Definition: According to Buddhism, these are the characteristics of a perfectly enlightened individual (Bodhisattva) who is in a divine state; compassion (Karuṇā), love (Maitra), equanimity (Upekṣā), and joy (Muditā).
