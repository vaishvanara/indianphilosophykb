**Sarvasya**
Meaning: "of all"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 6)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

[Meaning: "Of all; of everything; of the whole; the genitive relation denoting the source of the entire universe."]

---

## Definition

Lexically, *sarvasya* is the masculine/neuter genitive singular of the pronominal adjective stem *sarva* ("all," "every," "whole," "entire"). As an inflectional genitive form, it expresses the possessive, objective, or partitive relationship "of all" or "of everything."

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *sarvasya* occurs in mantra 6 in the description of *Prājña*, the third quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), corresponding to the condition of deep dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*) macrocosmically identified with the unmanifested causal deity, *Īśvara*:
> *eṣa sarveśvara eṣa sarvajña eṣo 'ntaryāmy eṣa yoniḥ sarvasya prabhavāpyayau hi bhūtānām*  
> ("This is the Lord of all; this is the knower of all; this is the inner controller; this is the source of all; for from this all beings originate and in this they finally disappear.")

Within Advaita Vedānta exegesis, the term operates on two levels:

1. **Ordinary / Lexical function:** It functions syntactically as a dependent genitive qualifying the feminine noun *yoniḥ* ("origin," "womb," "source"), yielding the phrase *yoniḥ sarvasya* ("the source of all").
2. **Extended philosophical meaning:** In his commentary on mantra 6, Śaṅkarācārya explains that *sarvasya* refers to the totality of the phenomenal cosmos—"the entire physical and super-physical universe characterized by diversity." In the causal state of deep sleep (*suṣupti*), the manifold differences experienced during waking and dreaming merge into undifferentiated pure consciousness. *Prājña*/*Īśvara* is designated as the origin of all (*yoniḥ sarvasya*) because the universe of names and forms proceeds from this causal consciousness during manifestation and dissolves back into it upon non-manifestation. Advaita emphasizes that *Prājña* is both the material and efficient cause of all (*sarvasya*), rather than an extra-cosmic creator separate from the world.

## Etymology

* **Part of speech & inflection:** Genitive singular (masculine or neuter) of the pronominal adjective stem *sarva* (nominative singular masculine *sarvaḥ*, feminine *sarvā*, neuter *sarvam*).
* **Grammatical classification:** Belongs to the class of thirty-five pronominal stems designated in Sanskrit grammar as *sarvanāman* (pronoun / pronominal adjective), headed by *sarva* (*sarvādīni sarvanāmāni*, Pāṇini I.1.27).
* **Case affix and inflectional rule:** In the pronominal declension of vowel stems ending in short *-a*, the standard nominal genitive singular affix *-sya* is attached directly to the base (*sarva* + *sya* \\(\rightarrow\\) *sarvasya*), conforming with the general pronominal paradigms where masculine and neuter genitive singular forms are identical.
* **Root status:** In classical Sanskrit grammar and lexicography, *sarva* is treated as an underived pronominal base rather than a secondary verbal formation (*kṛdanta*); its primary root derivation is not established in the classical grammatical sources and remains uncertain.
