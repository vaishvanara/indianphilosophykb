Meaning: "peaceful, calm, tranquil, pacified, ceased; in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the peaceful, immutable nature of the Self (*Turīya*) free from all phenomenal change."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 7)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

Lexically, the primary meaning of *śānta* (Sanskrit: शान्त) is appeased, pacified, tranquilized, quieted, stilled, subsided, or ceased. In its literal sense, it denotes the cessation or abatement of activity, disturbance, agitation, or flame, as in the extinguishing of fire or the subsiding of a tempest. In extended and figurative usage applying to human psychological and spiritual states, *śānta* signifies composure, serenity, freedom from passion, and inward peace of mind. 

In the philosophical context of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 7), *śānta* occurs in the nominative neuter singular form (*śāntam*) as a core predicate of the Fourth (*Turīya*) or the transcendent non-dual Self (*Ātman*). Here, the term transcends ordinary emotional composure and signifies the absolute, unconditioned peace of pure Consciousness. Appearing in immediate conjunction with *prapañcopaśama* ("the cessation of all phenomenal appearance"), *śānta* indicates that *Turīya* is ever peaceful because it is completely devoid of modification, division, or change (*avikriya*).

---

## Etymology

The word *śānta* is a past passive participle (verbal adjective; *kṛdanta*) inflected in verse 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* as a neuter nominative singular substantive/adjective (*śāntam*) agreeing in gender, number, and case with *caturtham* (*Turīya*).

The word is derived from the Sanskrit primary verbal root *śam* (शान् / शम्), belonging to the Fourth class of verbs (the *divādi* class; *parasmaipada*; 3rd person singular present *śāmyati*), signifying "to be calm, quiet, or tranquil; to abate, cease, or be extinguished". The nominal stem *śānta* is formed by appending the primary past passive participial suffix *-ta* (*kta*) directly to the verbal root *śam*. In this standard derivation, the radical short vowel *a* undergoes compensatory lengthening to *ā*, and the root-final labial nasal *m* assimilates to the following dental stop *t* as the dental nasal *n*, producing the established stem *śānta* (शान्त).
