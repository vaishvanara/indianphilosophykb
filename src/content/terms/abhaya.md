Meaning: "fearlessness, freedom from fear, or security; in Indian philosophy, it also denotes liberation (*mokṣa*) or *nirvāṇa*."

---

## Definition

In classical Sanskrit lexicography, *abhaya* functions grammatically as both an adjective, meaning "fearless," "undaunted," "safe," or "secure," and a neuter noun, meaning "fearlessness," "security," "safety," or "freedom from dread and danger". 

As a moral and psychological term, *abhaya* denotes a state of steadfastness and passive courage in which an individual remains unswayed by inner or outer fear-born enemies, confronting danger and adversity with calm composure. It is recognized as a foundational virtue in spiritual practice and a core psychological attribute of the warrior (*kṣatriya*) temperament.

---

## Etymology

The morphological derivation of *abhaya* is well-established across classical Sanskrit linguistic texts. It is a negative compound (a *nañ-tatpuruṣa*) constructed by prefixing the privative particle *a-* (meaning "not," "without," or "un-") to the noun *bhaya* (meaning "fear," "dread," "alarm," or "danger"). 

The nominal base *bhaya* is derived from the primary Sanskrit verbal root *bhī* (√*bhī*, class 3 *bibheti* / class 1 *bhayate*), which carries the core sense "to fear," "to be afraid of," or "to dread". Consequently, the compound structure literally translates to "the absence or negation of fear". Classical lexicographical authorities document this derivation as certain, with no alternative or speculative etymologies recorded in the textual tradition.

## Religious significance

The term *abhaya* carries prominent symbolic and applied meanings across South Asian traditions:

1. **Iconographic Gesture (Abhaya-mudrā)**: In art and religious iconography, the term refers to the symbolic hand pose formed by raising the right hand with the palm facing outward. This pose signifies "do not fear" and acts as the "seal of fearlessness" associated with deities, saints, and enlightened beings.
2. **Social and Ritual Protection (Abhaya-dāna)**: In practical and devotional contexts, compound forms such as *abhaya-dāna* or *abhaya-prada* denote the formal grant, promise, or shelter of security extended to seekers or vulnerable beings.

---

## Philosophy and metaphysics

In philosophical systems, the term transitions from a relative psychological state to an absolute metaphysical designation:

1. **Advaita Vedānta**: *Abhaya* is treated as equivalent to final spiritual liberation (*mokṣa*). Because fear is caused by ignorance (*avidyā*) and the perception of duality, the direct realization of the non-dual Self (*Brahman*) eliminates all sense of a second entity, thereby destroying fear at its root and establishing the soul in a state of unshakeable bliss.
2. **Buddhism**: In early Buddhist canonical texts and commentaries, *abhaya* ("the fearless") functions as an established epithet and synonym for *nirvāṇa* (*nibbāna*), designating the unconditioned realm of peace (*śānti*), ultimate refuge, and liberation from the impermanence and suffering of phenomenal existence.
