Meaning: "negative particle expressing negation (not, no, nor), absence (*abhāva*), prohibition, or comparison (like, as)."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

*Na* (न) is primarily the standard indeclinable particle of negation in Sanskrit, translating ordinary lexical meanings as "not", "no", "nor", or "neither". In general sentence structure, it negates verbs, nominal expressions, or entire propositions. In Vedic literature and archaic poetic usage, *na* also functions as an enclitic particle of comparison, carrying the figurative or extended sense of "like", "as", or "as it were". When two negative elements are used in close proximity, such as *na* alongside another *na* or a negative prefix *a-/*an-*, they form an emphatic affirmative statement.

In Indian philosophy and Sanskrit grammar, *na* (grammatically designated as *nañ*) plays a central role in logic, semantics, and ontology. Philosophical grammarians and logicians distinguish two foundational types of negation:

1. **Nominally bound negation (*paryudāsa*)**: An implicative or relative negation that excludes a specific term while suggesting a related or similar positive entity (e.g., *a-brāhmaṇa*, meaning "other than a Brahmin", such as a *Kṣatriya*).
2. **Verbally bound negation (*prasajya-pratiṣedha*)**: An absolute or simple negation that directly denies an action, predicate, or presence without implying a substitute positive entity (e.g., denying the presence of a pot in a room).

Furthermore, grammarians such as Bhartṛhari, Patañjali, and Kauṇḍa Bhaṭṭa delineate six distinct semantic functions expressed by the negative particle in compound formations: similarity (*sādṛśya*), nonexistence or absence (*abhāva*), difference or mutual absence (*tadanyatva* / *anyonya-bhāva*), smallness (*tadalpatā*), unfitness or pejoration (*aprāśastya*), and contrariety or opposition (*virodha*).

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *na* is classified as an indeclinable particle (*nipāta* or *avyaya*). In Pāṇini’s grammatical system (*Aṣṭādhyāyī*), it is designated by the technical term *nañ*, where the mute indicator letter (*it*) *ñ* triggers specific morphophonemic rules when forming negative compounds. As a primitive indeclinable particle, *na* is not derived from a verbal root in classical Sanskrit grammar, and its root derivation remains unestablished. Comparative philology records its direct cognate relationship with other Indo-European negative particles, including Greek *nē-*, Latin *ne-*, Anglo-Saxon *ne*, and English *no*.
