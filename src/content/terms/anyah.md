Meaning: "other, another, different, distinct, opposed to; an other person or object."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 1)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

The Sanskrit term *anyaḥ* (Sanskrit: अन्यः) is the nominative singular masculine form of the pronominal adjective *anya* (neuter *anyat*, feminine *anyā*). Lexically, it denotes "other," "another," "different," "distinct," or "different from". In ordinary usage, it functions both as an adjective and an independent pronoun designating an entity, person, or quality distinct from the subject or item under discussion. When expressing relational difference ("different from" or "other than"), it regularly governs a noun in the ablative case or occurs as the initial member of a compound.

In classical Indian philosophy, *anya* and its nominal derivative *anyatva* ("otherness," "difference," or "distinctness") serve as central concepts across multiple theoretical frameworks:

1. **Sāṃkhya dualism**: Liberation (*kaivalya*) depends upon discriminative discernment (*vivekajñāna*), wherein the practitioner realizes the absolute "otherness" or distinctness (*anyatva*) of pure conscious spirit (*puruṣa*) from primordial materiality (*prakṛti* or *pradhāna*).
2. **Advaita Vedānta**: The concept of "otherness" is central to discussions of non-difference (*ananyatva*). In Advaitic exegesis of the *Brahmasūtra* (notably II.1.14, *tadananyatvam*), the manifest world of effects is declared to be "non-other" (*ananya*) than its ultimate material cause, *Brahman*. From the absolute standpoint (*paramārtha*), reality is non-dual (*advaita*), entirely destitute of any "other" (*anya*).
3. **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika epistemology**: Difference or otherness (*anyatva*) is analyzed in relation to mutual non-existence (*anyonyābhāva*) and distinguishing qualities (*asādhāraṇadharma*). It also forms the basis of the Nyāya theory of perceptual error (*anyathākhyāti*), which holds that an error occurs when an attribute belonging to one ("other") object is falsely apprehended in another proximate object.

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *anyaḥ* is an adjective and pronoun (*sarvanāman*). In Paninian grammar, the nominal stem *anya* belongs to the pronominal class beginning with *sarva* (*sarvādi-gaṇa*, Pāṇini I.1.27). 

As a pronominal stem, it exhibits distinct pronominal declension patterns rather than purely nominal *-a* stem inflections. Most notably, its nominative and accusative singular neuter form takes the pronominal suffix *-d* (*anyat*), rather than the standard nominal neuter suffix *-am*. Other pronominal inflections include the nominative plural masculine *anye*, the dative singular masculine *anyasmai*, and the ablative singular masculine *anyasmāt*.
