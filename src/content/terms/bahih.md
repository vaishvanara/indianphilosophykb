Meaning: "outside, outward, or external; in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the spatial element in *bahiṣ-prajña*, denoting waking consciousness directed to gross objects."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 3)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In ordinary, lexical, and grammatical Sanskrit usage, *bahiḥ* (Sanskrit: बहिः) is an indeclinable adverb and preposition meaning "out," "outside," "outward," "exterior," or "externally." It functions as the primary antonym of *antar* or *antaḥ* ("in," "inside," "interior").

In the philosophical context of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 3) and its classical commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā* and Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*), *bahiḥ* forms the prefixal component of the technical compound term *bahiṣ-prajñaḥ* (बहिष्प्रज्ञः, "outwardly conscious" or "having consciousness directed outward"). Here, it characterizes *Vaiśvānara* (or *Viśva*), the first quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), which experiences the waking state (*jāgrat-sthāna*).

In this scriptural usage, *bahiḥ* denotes the outward orientation of awareness, wherein consciousness apprehends external gross physical objects (*sthūla-viṣaya*) through the nineteen instruments of cognition and action (the ten sense and motor organs, five vital airs, and four internal mental faculties). This outward direction of consciousness (*bahiṣ-prajña*) in the waking state stands in contrast to dream consciousness (*antaḥ-prajña*, "inwardly conscious"), where cognition is turned inward toward subtle mental impressions (*sūkṣma-viṣaya*), and deep sleep (*prājña*), where all objective distinctions subside into a unified mass of consciousness.

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *bahiḥ* is classified in Sanskrit grammar as an indeclinable adverbial particle (*avyaya*) or preposition. It expresses exteriority, spatial outwardness, or exclusion from an interior domain.

Grammatically, when *bahiḥ* enters into compound formation (*samāsa*) or sandhi juncture with a following noun, it undergoes standard phonetic modifications based on the initial sound of the subsequent term:

1. **Sandhi transformation:** When preceding a voiceless stop such as the labial *p-* in *prajñā* ("consciousness" or "cognition"), the final visarga (*-ḥ*) preceded by *i* transforms into the retroflex sibilant *-ṣ-*, yielding the compound *bahiṣ-prajña* (बहिष्प्रज्ञ, *bahiḥ* + *prajñā* = "outwardly conscious").
2. **Related compounds:** Combined with *mukha* ("face" or "direction"), it forms *bahirmukha* (बहिर्मुख, "outward-turned" or "extroverted"), describing a mind oriented toward external sense objects (*bāhyārtha*), as opposed to *antarmukha* ("inward-turned"). Combined with *aṅga* ("limb" or "component"), it forms *bahiraṅga* ("external limb" or "remote spiritual practice").
