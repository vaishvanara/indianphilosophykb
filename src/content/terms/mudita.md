**Mudita**
Meaning: “joy; happiness”
 
Muditā is the sublime state of sympathetic or altruistic joy—the practice of genuinely rejoicing in the happiness, success, good fortune, and prosperity of others. As the third Brahmavihāra, muditā completes the emotional spectrum by addressing how we relate to the joy of others, just as maitrī addresses general well-being and karuṇā addresses suffering. Naturally, human minds are prone to social comparison, leading to subtle or overt feelings of envy, jealousy, and bitterness (irṣyā) when witnessing another's triumph. Muditā directly shatters this ego-driven competitiveness by transforming potential jealousy into shared celebration. When someone experiences muditā, another person's success multiplies their own inner happiness rather than diminishing it. It fosters a communal spirit of mutual uplifting, eradicates petty rivalry, and fills the heart with an unblemished lightness. Cultivating muditā expands the capacity for limitless joy, ensuring that one's happiness is no longer isolated or fragile, but resonant with the triumphs of the entire universe.
