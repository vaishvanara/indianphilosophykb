Jihvā is the dual-function organ responsible for both taste (rasa) and speech (vāk as an expression). As a jñānendriya, its primary role is tasting, allowing the individual to discern six core flavors: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, pungent, and astringent. It is cosmologically linked to the element of water (apas), which acts as a medium for dissolving flavors. Because what we consume directly impacts physical health and the mental qualities of sattva, rajas, and tamas, the tongue holds a powerful sway over human desires and behavior. In Ayurveda and Yoga, controlling the tongue (jihvā-nigraha) is a crucial milestone in self-discipline. Overcoming palate attachment helps a seeker transition from chasing temporary sensory pleasure to maintaining inner contentment.
