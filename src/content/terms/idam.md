Meaning: "this, this here; the manifest universe or world of name and form ("all this"); present, now, here."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verses 1, 11)](mandukyopanisat.md)
 
---

## Definition

In ordinary Sanskrit usage, *idam* (इदम्) is a demonstrative pronoun meaning "this" or "this here," designated to indicate an object, person, or situation proximate to the speaker. Grammatical tradition distinguishes its spatial application from related demonstratives: *idam* indicates what is near at hand, whereas *etat* denotes what is nearer still, *adas* refers to what is at a distance, and *tat* signifies what is absent. Beyond its nominal pronoun function, *idam* frequently serves as an adverb meaning "here," "hither," "now," "just," or "thus".

When *idam* is joined with universal terms such as *sarvam* ("all"), *viśvam*, or *sakalam*, forming the phrase *idam sarvam* ("all this"), its meaning extends figuratively to encompass the entire manifest world or universe of perception.

In Indian philosophy, particularly within Advaita Vedānta, *idam* ("this") acquires a specialized metaphysical and epistemological meaning as the designation for the objective, phenomenal world of name and form (*nāmarūpa*). It stands in contrast to *anidam* ("not-this") or the inner Self (*pratyagātman* / *viṣayin*). In canonical Upanishadic statements such as *sarvaṁ khalv idaṁ brahma* ("All this `universe` is *Brahman*") and *ahamevedam* ("I am all this"), *idam* denotes the totality of the products of ignorance (*avidyā*), which are ultimately realized as non-different from the non-dual Absolute.

## Etymology

Grammatically, *idam* functions as the nominative and accusative singular neuter form of the demonstrative pronoun stem. Traditional grammarians derive the term via Uṇādisūtra iv, 156 from the primary pronominal root *i-* combined with the affix *d* and suffix *am* (*i-d-ám*).

The declension of *idam* is suppletive, utilizing multiple pronominal bases across its paradigm:

* The nominative singular forms are masculine *ayam* (अयम्), feminine *iyam* (इयम्), and neuter *idam* (इदम्).
* Oblique cases are formed principally from the pronominal base *a-* (such as dative masculine *asmai*, ablative *asmāt*, genitive *asya*, and locative *asmin*) and the base *ima-* (such as nominative plural masculine *ime* and accusative *imam* / *imām*).
* The enclitic base *ena-* is optionally substituted in secondary textual references for specific oblique cases, including accusative singular *enam*, *enām*, *enad*, and instrumental singular *enena*, *enayā*.

Lexicographers note Indo-European cognates in Latin *is*, *ea*, *id*, and *idem*. Numerous pronominal adverbs and derivatives are formed from the same underlying base *i-* / *idam-*, including *ihá* ("here"), *itas* ("hence / from this"), *idānīm* ("now"), and *idaṁ-rūpa* ("having this form").
