Meaning: "wonderful, marvelous, miraculous, secret, or mysterious; as a noun, a wonder or portent."

---

## Definition

In classical Sanskrit lexicography, *adbhuta* (Sanskrit: अद्भुत, IAST: *adbhuta*) functions both as an adjective and a neuter noun. As an adjective, its primary lexical meanings are "wonderful," "marvelous," "miraculous," or "wondrous". Lexicographical tradition also records its extended or figurative meanings as "invisible," "secret," and "mysterious". When used as a neuter noun, *adbhuta* signifies a "wonder" or a "portent".

In classical Indian poetics and aesthetic theory (*rasa* theory), *adbhuta* denotes the aesthetic sentiment or emotional state (*rasa*) of wonder or astonishment. It is categorized as one of the secondary or indirect aesthetic mellows, alongside anger (*raudra*), ghastliness (*bībhatsa*), dread (*bhayānaka*), humor (*hāsya*), chivalry (*vīra*), and compassion (*dayā*).

Classical lexicons document several derivative forms and compound structures based on the headword:

- ***adbhuta-darśana***: adjective meaning "of wondrous aspect".
- ***adbhuta-rūpa***: adjective meaning "of wondrous form".
- ***adbhuta-āvaha***: adjective meaning "exciting wonder".
- ***adbhutatama***: superlative adjective meaning "most marvellous".
- ***adbhutopamā***: feminine noun referring to a rhetorical figure or "simile assuming a miracle".

---

## Etymology

Lexicographical sources analyze the compound structure and derivation of *adbhuta* as originating from the prefix *ati-* ("beyond, transcendent") combined with *bhūta* ("become, risen, existing"), yielding the literal sense of "transcendent" or that which goes beyond ordinary existence. Historical Sanskrit lexicons also document the variant form *adbhūta*.
