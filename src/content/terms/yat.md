Meaning: "1. To strive, endeavor, exert oneself; 2. (relative pronoun/conjunction) which, what, that, because; 3. (participle) going, moving."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 1)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In classical Sanskrit lexicography and grammar, the term *yat* (यत्) functions across three distinct grammatical and semantic categories: as a verbal root, a relative pronoun and conjunction, and a present participle. 

As a verbal root (√*yat*), it primarily denotes striving, exertion, or attempt. Early Vedic literature uses it in the sense of marshaling, placing in order, or competing, while philosophical and grammatical contexts expand this to purposeful volition or spiritual endeavor (*yatna*). 

As a relative pronoun and conjunction, representing the neuter nominative and accusative singular of the stem *ya-* / *yad-*, it translates literally as "which," "what," or "that." Syntactically, it often introduces direct quotations or acts adverbially to mean "because," "since," or "so that." 

Finally, when functioning as an active present participle of the root *i* ("to go"), it signifies "going," "moving," or "approaching," as seen in verbal compounds such as *ā-yat* ("coming" or "future").

---

## Etymology

The grammatical formation of *yat* corresponds directly to its three functional roles. While the ultimate primitive derivation of the verbal root √*yat* remains uncertain, it is traditionally classified as a Class 1 (*bhūvādi*) root. It predominantly employs middle-voice (*ātmanepada*) endings (*yatate*), though active (*parasmaipada*) forms (*yatati*) appear in epic and metrical literature. 

Its primary derivatives include the causative stem *yātaya-* ("to dispose" or "requite"), the past passive participles *yatta* and *yatita*, the agent noun *yati* ("ascetic"), and the abstract noun *yatna* ("effort"). 

In its pronominal and conjunctional role, the stem undergoes standard morphophonological sandhi, altering the final dental consonant to *yac-*, *yaj-*, *yan-*, or *yach-* depending on the subsequent sound. As a present participle, it is produced by attaching the active participial affix *-at* to the root *i*, yielding the masculine *yan*, neuter *yat*, and feminine *yatī*.
