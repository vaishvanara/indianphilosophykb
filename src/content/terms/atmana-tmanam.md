**Ātmanā’tmānaṃ**
Meaning: "by the Self he enters the Self"
From Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 12)

आत्मनाऽत्मानं (ātmanā'tmānaṃ) is a nominative accusative dual form of the Sanskrit compound word आत्मनात्मान (ātmanā'tmāna), which means "the self by the self" or "the soul by the soul".

In various contexts, आत्मनाऽत्मानं can be translated to:

- The self by the self
- The soul by the soul
- The inner self by the inner self
- The true self by the true self
- The essential self by the essential self

This phrase is often used in Hindu and Buddhist philosophy to refer to the idea of the self being aware of itself, or the soul being aware of its own nature.

For example, in the Bhagavad Gita (2.20), it is written:

आत्मनाऽत्मानं पश्यन्नात्मनाऽत्मानं पश्यन्नात्मनाऽत्मानं

Ātmanā'tmānaṃ paśyan nātmanā'tmānaṃ paśyan nātmanā'tmānaṃ

Which translates to: "The self by the self sees the self by the self, and the self by the self sees the self by the self."

In general, आत्मनाऽत्मानं refers to the idea of the self being aware of its own nature, and is often used to describe the process of self-realization or self-awareness.

**Translation by Swami Sharvananda**
Sanskrit: अत्मनाऽत्मानं
Transliteration: Ātmanā’tmānaṃ
Translation: "by his own Self, the Self enters"
Commentary: "i.e., in that superconscious or the fourth state of consciousness, the existence of mind also is denied, the pure consciousness becomes conscious of itself. It is ineffable in ordinary human language. What is said of it is but a hint for those who might have experienced it."

**Commentary by Swami Krishnananda**
"Samviśatyatmanātmānam: One who knows this secret, by deep meditation, enters the Ātman by the Ātman. We do not enter the Ātman by a gate, we enter the Ātman by the Ātman. We do not enter the Ātman; the Ātman enters the Ātman. We do not exist. We evaporate into the Ātman, and the Ātman becomes the Ātman. Sounds merge in Praṇava; it becomes the Ātman. The Ātman alone is. When the Ātman becomes the Ātman through the Ātman, it is called ātmasākshātkāra – realisation of the Ātman. It is also brahmasākshātkāra – realisation of Brahman. From the point of view of the Ātman animating the individual states, we call this achievement ātmasākshātkāra. From the standpoint of this very same Ātman animating the whole cosmos, we call it brahmasākshātkāra. It is Self-realisation and God-realisation at one and the same time. It is Existence, it is Consciousness, it is Power, it is Bliss, it is Perfection, it is Immortality, it is mokṣha, it is Kaivalya."
