[Meaning: "the Self by the Self; the supreme Self projecting or realizing Himself by Himself."

## Definition

Literally translating as "the Self by the Self" or "oneself by oneself," *ātmanā’tmānaṃ* is a Sanskrit reflexive-reciprocal phrase composed of two distinct grammatical forms of the noun *ātman*. Lexically, the phrase expresses an internal action wherein the Self acts as both the subject (agent) and the object of cognition, projection, or realization. In ordinary Sanskrit usage, it functions as a reflexive idiom denoting self-directed action or self-awareness.

In Advaita Vedānta philosophy, the term carries a technical and doctrinal meaning. Figuratively and doctrinally, *ātmanā’tmānaṃ* describes the non-dual, self-luminous Absolute (*Brahman* or *Ātman*) projecting phenomenal appearances within Its own awareness without external aids, as well as the ultimate spiritual realization in which the Self is known by and through the Self alone, transcending the empirical separation between knower and known.

---

## Etymology

The expression *ātmanā’tmānaṃ* (आत्मनाऽत्मानं) is a Sanskrit euphonic combination (sandhi) of two inflected forms of the masculine noun stem *ātman* (आत्मन्):

1. ***ātmanā* (आत्मना)**: The instrumental singular form of *ātman*, meaning "by the Self," "with the Self," or "through oneself."
2. ***ātmānam* (आत्मानम्)**: The accusative singular form of *ātman*, meaning "the Self" or "oneself."

Through standard rules of Sanskrit sandhi, the terminal vowel *-ā* of *ātmanā* combines with the initial *ā-* of *ātmānam* to form *ātmanātmānam*. In written *Devanāgarī*, the *avagraha* sign (ऽ) represents the elided vowel in compound sandhi (*ātmanā’tmānaṃ*), while the final dental nasal *-m* is converted to anusvāra (*-ṃ*) before subsequent consonants or at sentence boundaries.

Grammatically, *ātmanā’tmānaṃ* functions as an instrumental-accusative reflexive noun phrase. The underlying root noun *ātman* is derived either from the Sanskrit verb root *at* ("to breathe") or from the verb root *āp* ("to pervade, reach up to"), denoting the inner Self that is the eternal substratum of all experience and identical with the Absolute.

## Ātmanā’tmānaṃ in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the textual tradition of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, specifically within Gauḍapāda's *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā* and Śaṅkarācārya's commentary, *ātmanā’tmānaṃ* serves as a crucial technical formula explaining non-dual creation and self-knowledge.

In the second chapter of the *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā* (*Vaitathya Prakaraṇa*, verse 12), Gauḍapāda employs the phrase (*kalpayaty ātmanā’tmānam ātmā devaḥ svamāyayā*) to elucidate how the self-luminous Supreme Self (*ātmā devaḥ*) imagines or projects Himself (*ātmānam*) by Himself (*ātmanā*) through His own illusory power (*svamāyā*). Within this framework, all cosmic manifestations, such as the vital breaths (*prāṇa*), the three empirical states of consciousness (waking, dream, and deep sleep), and the manifold universe, are conceptual constructions (*vikalpa*) superimposed upon the non-dual *Ātman*. The phrase emphasizes that because there is no second entity outside of non-dual Reality, the Absolute is both the substratum (*adhiṣṭhāna*) and the apparent agent of cosmic projection, without undergoing any actual origination or real modification (*ajātivāda*).

Doctrinally, *ātmanā’tmānaṃ* also characterizes the direct, non-relational realization of the Fourth state (*Turīya*) expounded in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*. While empirical cognition relies on a triadic division (*tripuṭī*) of knower, known, and knowledge, the realization of *Ātman* as non-different from *Brahman* (*ayam ātmā brahma*) occurs when the Self illumines and recognizes Himself by Himself (*ātmanā’tmānaṃ*), completely devoid of external instruments, mental modifications, or subject-object duality.
