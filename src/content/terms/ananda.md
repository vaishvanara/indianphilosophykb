Meaning: "bliss, absolute joy, or spiritual happiness; the essential non-dual nature of *Brahman* and the intrinsic, unconditioned joy of the liberated Self (*Ātman*)."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In Advaita Vedānta, *ānanda* (Sanskrit: आनन्द) denotes pure, unconditioned, absolute bliss. It is not an empirical emotion, transitory mental state, or psychological pleasure derived from sense-object contact (*viṣayānanda*). Instead, *ānanda* represents the very essential nature (*svarūpa*) of Ultimate Reality (*Brahman*) and the true Self (*Ātman*). 

While ordinary worldly happiness (*sukha*) is temporary, conditional, and dependent on external objects, *ānanda* in Advaita Vedānta is eternal, self-luminous (*svayaṁjyoti*), partless (*niṣkala*), and non-different from Pure Existence (*Sat*) and Pure Consciousness (*Cit*). Empirical pleasures are understood as finite, filtered reflections (*pratibimba*) of this supreme, indivisible Bliss through a tranquil mind.

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *ānanda* is a Sanskrit masculine noun derived from the verbal root *nand* (नन्द्), meaning "to rejoice," "to delight," or "to be pleased". It is prefixed by the intensive and directional prefix *ā-* (आ-), signifying completeness, intensity, or fullness. Thus, *ānanda* literally translates to "full joy," "supreme delight," or "unbounded bliss."

Grammatically, *ānanda* functions both as an abstract noun and as a primary component in compound expressions denoting the Absolute. In classical Advaita literature, it forms the third element of the foundational compound *saccidānanda* (*sat* + *cit* + *ānanda*: Existence-Consciousness-Bliss). Advaita hermeneutics establishes that *ānanda* is not a quality or attribute (*guṇa*) possessed by *Brahman* as a underlying substance (*guṇīn*), but is structurally identical with *Brahman* itself (*ānanda-svarūpa*).

## Ānanda in Advaita Vedānta

In the metaphysics and soteriology of Advaita Vedānta, *ānanda* plays a central role in defining the nature of Reality and the character of spiritual liberation (*mokṣa*).

### Essential definition (svarūpa-lakṣaṇa)

Advaita Vedānta distinguishes between two types of definitions: secondary/accidental qualifications (*taṭastha-lakṣaṇa*, such as viewing *Brahman* as the cause of the universe) and essential nature (*svarūpa-lakṣaṇa*). 

*Ānanda*, along with *Sat* (Existence) and *Cit* (Consciousness), constitutes the *svarūpa-lakṣaṇa* of *Brahman*. Because *Brahman* is partless (*niravayava*) and attributeless (*nirguṇa*), these three terms do not denote three distinct properties or parts of Reality; rather, Existence is Consciousness, and Consciousness is Bliss.

### The five sheaths (pañcakośa) and the ānandamaya-kośa

In its analysis of human embodiment based on the *Taittirīya Upaniṣad*, Advaita Vedānta examines the five sheaths (*pañcakośa*) that envelop the Self:

1. *Annamaya-kośa* (food/physical sheath)
2. *Prāṇamaya-kośa* (vital air sheath)
3. *Manomaya-kośa* (mental sheath)
4. *Vijñānamaya-kośa* (intellectual sheath)
5. *Ānandamaya-kośa* (bliss-sheath).

The *ānandamaya-kośa* is the subtle, causal sheath composed of nescience (*avidyā* / *tamas*) catching a reflection of Pure Consciousness (*cidābhāsa*). It manifests during moments of desire-fulfillment as temporary feelings of pleasure (*priya*), joy (*moda*), and delight (*pramoda*), and is fully active in deep sleep (*suṣupti*). 

Śaṅkarācārya and later commentators emphasize that the *ānandamaya-kośa* is a modified, finite sheath (*vikāra*) and is not itself the Supreme *Brahman*. The transcendent *Brahman* is the unconditioned substratum (*puchha* / *adhiṣṭhāna*) beyond all five sheaths (*pañcakośātīta*).

### Three categories of bliss in post-Śaṅkara Advaita

In post-Śaṅkara manuals such as Śrī Vidyāraṇya Svāmī's *Pañcadaśī* (Chapters 11–15), *ānanda* is classified into three experiential modalities:

1. ***Brahmānanda* (bliss of *Brahman*):** The primary, self-revealing, infinite bliss of non-dual Reality.
2. ***Vāsanānanda* (bliss of latent impressions):** The subtle, quiet happiness experienced in a tranquil mind when mental modifications subside, arising from the latent impressions of *Brahmānanda* (such as upon awakening from deep sleep).
3. ***Viṣayānanda* (bliss of objects):** Empirical pleasure experienced when a desired external object is attained. Upon gaining the desired object, the restless mind momentarily turns inward (*antarmukhī*), resulting in a peaceful mental state (*sāttvika-vṛtti*) that briefly reflects a tiny fraction (*leśa*) of *Brahman*'s inherent bliss.

### Bliss in deep sleep (suṣupti) and liberation (mokṣa)

In dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*), sense organs and dualistic mental modes subside. The *jīva* experiences an unconditioned reflection of bliss, as evidenced by the universal waking reflection: "I slept happily, I knew nothing" (*sukham aham asvāpsam*). 

In liberation (*mokṣa* or *jīvanmukti*), this bliss is realized permanently through Self-knowledge (*jñāna*), removing fundamental ignorance (*avidyā*) and ending all suffering (*duḥkha-nivṛtti*).

## Ānanda in Viśiṣṭādvaita Vedānta

In contrast to Advaita Vedānta, the Viśiṣṭādvaita school (founded by Rāmānuja) interprets *ānanda* as an essential, real attribute (*guṇa*) belonging to God (*Īśvara*/*Brahman*) as a personal Supreme Lord endowed with infinite auspicious qualities (*sakala-kalyāṇa-guṇātmaka*). 

While Advaita holds that *ānanda* is identical with the partless, attributeless Absolute without subject-object distinction, Viśiṣṭādvaita asserts that individual souls (*jīva*) remain distinct modes (*prakāra*) eternally enjoying the bliss of God in an inseparable relationship (*apṛthak-siddhi*).

## Primary texts and commentaries

- ***Taittirīya Upaniṣad* (2.1–8, 3.6):** Defines *Brahman* as "Truth, Knowledge, Infinite" (*satyaṁ jñānam anantam brahma*, 2.1). Chapter 2, Section 8 (*Ānandavallī*) sets forth a calculus of bliss (*ānandasya mīmāṁsā*), ascending from human bliss to celestial realms, establishing that supreme Bliss is non-dual, immeasurable, and experienced by one free from desires. Chapter 3, Section 6 records Bhrigu's realization that "Bliss is *Brahman*" (*ānando brahmeti vyajānāt*).
- ***Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* (3.9.28.7, 4.3.32):** Declares "Knowledge and Bliss is *Brahman*" (*vijñānam ānandaṁ brahma*) and affirms that all creaturely pleasures are mere fractions of this supreme Bliss.
- ***Chāndogya Upaniṣad* (7.23.1):** Proclaims "That which is Infinite is Bliss" (*yo vai bhūmā tat sukham*), asserting that no finite thing contains real happiness.
- ***Brahma-sūtra* (1.1.12–19, *Ānandamayādhikaraṇa*):** Badarāyaṇa analyzes whether *ānandamaya* refers to the individual soul or *Brahman*. Śaṅkarācārya's *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* demonstrates that while *-maya* can denote modification, scripture uses *ānanda* repeatedly to designate the Supreme Self (*Para-Brahman*) due to its abundance (*prācurya*) and causal role. Śaṅkara further clarifies that the *ānandamaya-kośa* itself is a sheath, whereas the ultimate ground is *Nirguṇa Brahman*.
- ***Upadeśasāhasrī* and *Ātma Bodha* (Śaṅkarācārya):** Explicate the Self as eternal, non-dual, self-effulgent bliss (*nityānanda-svarūpa*).
- ***Pañcadaśī* (Śrī Vidyāraṇya Svāmī):** Chapters 11–15 systematically examine the nature of *Brahmānanda*, *Ātmānanda*, *Advaitānanda*, *Vidyānanda*, and *Viṣayānanda*.
- ***Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* (attrib. Śaṅkarācārya):** Details the nature of the *ānandamaya-kośa* (vv. 207–209) and describes the direct intuitive absorption in non-dual bliss (*akhaṇḍānandarasānubhūti*, v. 378).

## Interpretations and disputes

### The Ānandamayādhikaraṇa hermeneutical debate

A major debate within Vedantic exegesis concerns *Brahma-sūtra* 1.1.12–19. The *Pūrvapakṣa* (opponent view) argues that the suffix *-maya* in *ānandamaya* indicates a modification (*vikāra*), placing it in the same category of created sheaths as food (*annamaya*) and vital air (*prāṇamaya*). Badarāyaṇa's *Sūtras* (1.1.12–13) answer that *-maya* here denotes abundance (*prācurya*) or fullness. 

In his *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya*, Śaṅkarācārya presents a nuanced position: while acknowledging the *sūtras'* argument that *ānanda* denotes *Brahman*, Śaṅkara maintains that in *Taittirīya Upaniṣad* II.5, *ānandamaya* refers to the fifth conditioned sheath (with joy as its head and delight as its wings), whereas the true, unconditioned Absolute is designated as the underlying support: "*Brahman* is the tail, the support" (*brahma puccham pratiṣṭhā*).

### Nature of bliss in liberation (mokṣa)

Rival philosophical schools (such as Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika) contend that liberation is merely a state of neutral absence of pain (*duḥkhābhāva*) devoid of consciousness or positive joy. Advaita Vedānta refutes this, demonstrating through scriptural testimony (*śruti*) and direct intuition (*anubhūti*) that *mokṣa* is positive, infinite, self-luminous bliss (*svarūpānanda*). This bliss is not an empirical feeling produced by mind-object interaction, but the unconditioned reality that remains when ignorance and limiting adjuncts are dissolved.

### Modern historical-critical scholarship

Modern scholars of Advaita Vedānta (such as Paul Hacker, Sengaku Mayeda, and Karl H. Potter) note that while the unified compound *saccidānanda* became ubiquitous in post-Śaṅkara text manuals (e.g., *Vedāntasāra*, *Tattvabodha*), the specific tripartite compound *saccidānanda* does not occur in Śaṅkarācārya's authenticated commentary works. In his authentic bhasyas, Śaṅkara treats *sat*, *cit*, and *ānanda* individually or follows the Upanishadic phrasing *satyaṁ jñānam anantam brahma*.
