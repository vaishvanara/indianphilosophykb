**Paśyati Na**
Meaning: "does not see"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *paśyati na* ("does not see") denotes the complete absence of dream vision and mental projection during deep sleep (*suṣupti*).

---

## Definition

The Sanskrit expression *paśyati na* (पश्यति न) is a negative verbal phrase composed of the third-person singular present indicative verb *paśyati* ("sees," "beholds," "perceives") and the negative particle *na* ("not"). Lexically, it signifies "does not see," "beholds not," or "does not perceive."

Literally, *paśyati* refers to visual perception through the physical organ of sight (*cakṣus*) or to mental cognition and discernment. The particle *na* negates the act of seeing or perceiving. 

In the contextual framework of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (Mantra 5: *yatra supto na kañcana kāmaṃ kāmayate na kañcana svapnaṃ paśyati tat suṣuptam*), the phrase occurs within the definition of the third quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), designated as *Prajñā* or the state of deep sleep (*suṣupta-sthāna*). In this verse, *na kañcana svapnaṃ paśyati* ("nor does he see any dream") describes the total cessation of objective visual imagery, mental desires (*kāma*), and dream manifestations (*svapna*).

Philosophically, Advaita Vedānta contrasts this state with the two preceding states of experience:
1. **Waking State (*Viśva* / *jāgarita-sthāna*)**: Characterized by the perception of gross external objects through nineteen sensory and mental avenues.
2. **Dream State (*Taijasa* / *svapna-sthāna*)**: Characterized by the internal perception of subtle mental impressions (*vāsanās*).
3. **Deep-Sleep State (*Prajñā* / *suṣupta-sthāna*)**: Defined by *na kañcana svapnaṃ paśyati*, wherein all dualistic perception dissolves into an undifferentiated mass of consciousness (*prajñānaghana*) and bliss (*ānandamaya*).

In his commentary (*Bhāṣya*), Śaṅkarācārya explains that while waking and dream involve the mis-apprehension of reality (*anyathā-grahaṇa*), deep sleep is characterized by the absence of dualistic perceptions. In Gauḍapāda's *Kārikā* (I.13–15), deep sleep (*nidrā*) is distinguished from the unconditioned fourth state (*Turīya*): whereas deep sleep involves non-apprehension of truth (*tattvā-grahaṇa*), *Turīya* is eternally all-seeing (*sarva-dṛk*) and free from both non-apprehension and dream mis-apprehension.

## Etymology

The expression *paśyati na* (पश्यति न) is a negative verbal phrase consisting of two distinct grammatical components:

1. ***paśyati* (पश्यति)**:
   - **Part of Speech**: Finite verb in the 3rd person singular present active indicative (*laṭ-lakāra*, *prathama-puruṣa*, *ekavacana*, *Parasmaipada*).
   - **Root**: Derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *dṛś* (दृश्, class 1 Parasmaipada), meaning "to see," "to look," "to perceive," or "to behold."
   - **Derivation & Stem Substitution**: According to classical Sanskrit grammatical rules (Pāṇini 7.3.78), the root *dṛś* substitutes the present verbal stem *paśya-* (पश्य-) before four-fold conjugational tenses (*sārvadhātuka*). Adding the primary active third-person singular termination *-ti* (ति) to the stem *paśya-* produces the inflected verb *paśyati* ("he/she/it sees").

2. ***na* (न)**:
   - **Part of Speech**: Particle / Indeclinable (*avyaya*).
   - **Function**: Serves as a standard negative particle meaning "not" or "no," directly modifying the finite verb *paśyati*.

In the text of *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* Mantra 5, the negative particle *na* appears in disjunctive coordination (*na kañcana kāmaṃ kāmayate na kañcana svapnaṃ paśyati*), negating both the verbal action of desiring (*kāmayate*) and that of dream-seeing (*paśyati*).
