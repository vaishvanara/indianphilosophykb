Cakṣuḥ is the sense organ of vision, responsible for the perception of light, color, shape, and form (rūpa). Philosophically, it is tied to the element of fire or light (tejas), which illuminates objects and makes visual cognition possible. Vision is often regarded as the most expansive and immediate of the human senses, bridging the observer with distant objects across space instantaneously. In spiritual traditions, Cakṣuḥ transcends physical sight to symbolize inner discernment, clarity, and higher knowledge—often referred to metaphorically as the "eye of wisdom" (jñāna-cakṣus). Yogic paths emphasize guarding the eyes from distracting or harmful visual inputs, utilizing sight instead to contemplate sacred forms, nature, and the inner light of consciousness.
