Maitrī
Meaning: “friendliness; friendship; love”

Maitrī (often referred to as mettā in Pali) is the virtue of universal loving-kindness, friendliness, and active benevolence. It is the foundational Brahmavihāra (sublime abiding) and involves a deep, unconditioned wish for the ultimate happiness, welfare, and well-being of all living beings, without any expectation of personal reward or reciprocation. Cultivating maitrī requires breaking down artificial psychological barriers that categorize people into "friends," "strangers," or "enemies," viewing all conscious entities as equally deserving of peace and safety. Psychologically, maitrī serves as the primary antidote to anger, hatred, ill-will, and aggression. In meditative practice, it typically begins with generating love toward oneself, then systematically radiating it outward to loved ones, neutral persons, difficult individuals, and eventually across the entire cosmos without limitation. By abiding in maitrī, the mind becomes serene, joyful, and deeply resilient, fostering harmonious relationships and creating an inner sanctuary of warmth that shields the practitioner from the turbulence of malice and resentment.
