Meaning: "beyond empirical transaction, unrelatable, unbusinesslike; incapable of being acted upon or brought into practical interaction."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 7, 12)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

Lexically, *avyavahāryam* (Sanskrit: अव्यवहार्यम्; base/masculine form: *avyavahārya*) means "beyond empirical transaction," "unrelatable," "beyond practical interaction," "incapable of being dealt with," or "beyond conventional usage." In standard Sanskrit, *vyavahāra* denotes empirical activity, commercial transaction, social or legal procedure, worldly dealing, or relational interaction; consequently, the negated form *avyavahārya* describes anything that lies entirely outside or beyond the domain of such empirical transactions.

In Indian philosophy, particularly within Advaita Vedānta, *avyavahāryam* functions as a key technical term defining the transcendent status of Ultimate Reality (*Brahman* or *Ātman* in the state of *Turīya*). Figuratively and doctrinally, the term indicates that Reality cannot be brought into any subject-object relationship, cognitive transaction, or practical activity (*vyavahāra*). Because empirical transactions require a triad (*tripuṭī*) of knower, known, and means of knowledge, the non-dual Absolute, being partless and free from all limiting adjuncts (*upādhi*), transcends every form of empirical relation and action.

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *avyavahāryam* is an adjective inflected in the nominative/accusative singular neuter form. It is formed as a negative compound derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *hṛ* (हृ; "to take, carry, fetch, or conduct"):

1. ***a-* (अ-)**: The negative prefix meaning "not," "un-," or "devoid of."
2. ***vi-* (वि-) + *ava-* (अव-)**: Combined prefixes functioning together to signify "distinct downward or outward conduct, customary behavior, or commercial transaction."
3. ***hṛ* (हृ) + gerundive / potential passive participle suffix *-ya* (-य)**: The verb root "to carry/conduct" forming *vyavahārya*, meaning "that which can be transacted, dealt with, or related to."

From these components, *vyavahāra* signifies "empirical activity, practical usage, or relational dealing." Prefixed with *a-*, *avyavahārya* literally denotes "that which cannot be made an object of empirical activity, transaction, or relational dealing." In lexicographical compilations of Indian philosophical terminology, it is concisely defined as "unrelatable" or "beyond empirical transaction."

## Avyavahāryam in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

Within the textual tradition of the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad (Verse 7), Gauḍapāda's *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā*, and Śaṅkarācārya's commentary (*Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya*), *avyavahāryam* serves as an essential apophatic epithet characterizing *Turīya*, meaning the "Fourth" state of unconditioned Consciousness that constitutes the true nature of the Self (*Ātman*).

Verse 7 defines *Turīya* by systematically negating the characteristics of the three empirical states of consciousness: waking (*jāgrat*), dreaming (*svapna*), and deep sleep (*suṣupti*). The text asserts that *Turīya* is "unseen (*adṛṣṭam*), beyond empirical transaction (*avyavahāryam*), ungraspable (*agrāhyam*), uninferable (*alakṣaṇam*), unthinkable (*acintyam*), and indescribable (*avyapadeśyam*)." 

In his commentary, Śaṅkarācārya explains that *avyavahāryam* means *vyavahāra-atītam*, meaning "that which is beyond the reach of all empirical transactions or worldly dealings." Śaṅkara observes that empirical activity (*vyavahāra*) depends on the operation of speech, mind, and the sense organs interacting with external or internal objects within the realm of duality. Because *Turīya* is characterized by the total cessation of phenomenal appearance (*prapañcopaśama*) and is essentially peaceful (*śānta*), auspicious (*śiva*), and non-dual (*advaita*), It cannot become an instrument, object, or arena for any transactional activity.

Doctrinally, *avyavahāryam* underscores the fundamental Advaita distinction between two standpoints: the empirical standpoint (*vyāvahārika*) and the absolute standpoint (*pāramārthika*). All cognitive procedures (*pramāṇa*), ritual actions (*karma*), and social usages operate exclusively within the domain of *vyavahāra*, which is grounded in fundamental ignorance (*avidyā*) and the superimposition (*adhyāsa*) of egoism. By designating the Self as *avyavahāryam*, the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad teaches that liberation (*mokṣa*) is not an action or transaction to be brought about through empirical means, but the direct realization of the Self as the eternal, non-relational Witness (*sākṣin*) that illumines all empirical experience while remaining entirely untouched by it.
