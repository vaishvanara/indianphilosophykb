Meaning: "the unmoving substrate, locus, or ground underlying all action, illusion, and world-appearance"

---

## Definition

In Classical Indian Philosophy, *adhiṣṭhāna* (Sanskrit: अधिष्ठान; IAST: adhiṣṭhāna) denotes a "standing-place," "locus," "basis," or "substratum". 

Within the *Bhagavad Gītā*, the term functions in an empirical and psychological sense to specify the physical body as the foundational seat of action, as well as the sense-mind complex as the locus of desire. 

In Advaita Vedānta, *adhiṣṭhāna* assumes a central metaphysical and epistemological role, signifying the immutable, non-dual Reality (*Brahman* or *Ātman*) that serves as the absolute, non-transforming locus upon which the illusory world-appearance (*prapañca*) and individual embodied existence (*jīvatva*) are falsely superimposed (*adhyāropa*) through primal ignorance (*avidyā*).

---

## Etymology

Morphologically, *adhiṣṭhāna* is a neuter noun derived from the Sanskrit verbal root *sthā* (स्था, "to stand," "to abide," "to exist") combined with the directive prefix *adhi-* (अधि-, "above," "over," "upon," or "in"), suffixed with the nominalizing suffix *-ana* (अन, indicating locus or instrument). Grammatically, it signifies "that upon which something stands or abides," hence a location, base, seat, or underlying ground. 

Established linguistic analysis shows its development from physical seats or dwellings in Vedic usage to its specialized philosophical usage in classical systems. Traditional Advaitic exegesis further distinguishes *adhiṣṭhāna* from generic containment (*ādhāra*): while *ādhāra* denotes any physical support or container, *adhiṣṭhāna* specifically signifies an unalterable ground whose non-cognition permits an illusory appearance to be falsely projected upon it.

---

## Adhiṣṭhāna in Advaita Vedānta

In Advaita Vedānta, *adhiṣṭhāna* is an essential pillar of the theory of error (*anirvacanīya-khyāti*) and the doctrine of apparent transformation (*vivarta-vāda*). According to Śaṅkarācārya and subsequent post-Śaṅkara commentators, an illusion (*adhyāsa*) cannot occur in a vacuum; every false superimposition requires a real, unchangeable substrate (*adhiṣṭhāna*).

In the standard illustrative paradigm of the rope and the snake (*rajju-sarpa-nyāya*), the rope acts as the *adhiṣṭhāna*. In dim light, non-apprehension of the true nature of the rope permits the mental projection of an illusory snake. The snake has no independent reality (*sattā*) apart from the rope; when right knowledge dawns, the illusory snake is sublated (*bādha*), leaving only the underlying rope intact. Metaphysically, pure Consciousness (*Brahman*) is the ultimate *adhiṣṭhāna* of the entire cosmos. The world of multiplicity, names, and forms (*nāma-rūpa*) is superimposed upon *Brahman* through *māyā* or *avidyā*.

Advaitic treatises emphasize several key features of *adhiṣṭhāna*:

1. **Unattached immutability (*asaṅgatva*)**: The *adhiṣṭhāna* remains entirely untouched by the attributes, defects, or changes of the superimposed object. Just as the desert ground is not wetted by the waters of a mirage, *Brahman* as *adhiṣṭhāna* is never contaminated by the transmigratory suffering (*saṃsāra*) of the individual.
2. **Existence-giving locus**: Superimposed phenomena derive their apparent reality and manifestation solely from the self-effulgent existence (*sat*) of the *adhiṣṭhāna*.
3. **Sublation through discrimination**: Realization of the *adhiṣṭhāna* automatically sublates the superimposition, revealing that the world was never created in reality (*ajātivāda*).

## Adhiṣṭhāna in the Bhagavad Gītā

In the *Bhagavad Gītā*, *adhiṣṭhāna* is employed primarily in a structural and psychological context rather than as an explicit synonym for Nirguṇa Brahman:

### The locus of action (BG 18.13–14)
 
 Lord *Kṛṣṇa* enumerates five factors (*pañca kāraṇāni*) declared in the Sāṅkhya system as necessary for the accomplishment of all human actions (*sarva-karma-siddhi*):

   - *Adhiṣṭhāna* (the physical body or seat of action)
   - *Kartā* (the agent or ego-sense)
   - *Karaṇa* (the various sense organs and instruments)
   - *Vividhāś ca pṛthag ceṣṭāḥ* (the distinct vital functions and energies)
   - *Daiva* (providence or the presiding divine factors)

In his commentary (*bhāṣya*) on BG 18.14, Śaṅkarācārya explains *adhiṣṭhāna* as the physical body (*śarīra*), designated as the "seat" because it serves as the physical abode where desire, hatred, pleasure, pain, and effort manifest.
   
### The seat of desire (BG 3.40)

The text identifies the senses (*indriyas*), the mind (*manas*), and the intellect (*buddhi*) as the *adhiṣṭhāna* (abode or seat) of desire (*kāma*), through which desire veils true knowledge and deludes the embodied soul.

## Primary texts and commentaries

- ***Bhagavad Gītā* (3.40; 18.13–14)**: Establishes *adhiṣṭhāna* as the physical body in the taxonomy of action and as the sense-mind complex in the taxonomy of desire.
- **Śaṅkarācārya’s *Adhyāsa-bhāṣya* (*Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya* Intro)**: Defines superimposition (*adhyāsa*) as the apparent presentation of a previously experienced object in a locus where it is not (*atasmin tad-buddhiḥ*), establishing that pure Consciousness is the fundamental *adhiṣṭhāna* of all empirical experience (*vyavahāra*).
- **Śaṅkarācārya’s *Brahmasūtra-bhāṣya* (1.4.3)**: Clarifies that the unmanifested causal potentiality (*avyakta or māyā*) relies upon the Supreme Lord as its locus or substratum (*tad-adhīnatvāt*), preventing it from being an independent entity like the Sāṅkhya *Pradhāna*.
- **Sadānanda’s *Vedāntasāra* (§142–147)**: Details how unassociated Consciousness (*anupahita-caitanya*) serves as the substratum (*adhiṣṭhāna*) of both cosmic ignorance (*Īśvara or māyā*) and individual ignorance (*jīva or avidyā*), and is indicated through implied meaning (*lakṣaṇā*) in the *Mahāvākya* "*Tat Tvam Asi*".
- **Vidyāraṇya’s *Pañcadaśī* (1.33–35; 6.224–226)**: Defines *Kūṭastha* (the immutable Self) as the *adhiṣṭhāna* upon which the gross and subtle bodies, together with reflected consciousness (*cidābhāsa*), are projected like silver onto mother-of-pearl.
- **Śrī Śaṅkarācārya (attributed), *Vivekacūḍāmaṇi* (vv. 231, 236, 407)**: Asserts that a superimposed object (*āropita*) has no existence separate from its substratum (*adhiṣṭhāna*); when error is dispelled, the universe is realized as *Ātman* alone.

## Interpretations and disputes

1. **Adhiṣṭhāna vs. ādhāra**: Post-Śaṅkara scholastic texts (such as the *Vicāra Sāgara*, discussed in Ramana Maharshi's *Talks*) distinguish *ādhāra* from *adhiṣṭhāna*. An *ādhāra* is a simple physical container or support (e.g., a table supporting a book), whereas an *adhiṣṭhāna* is an illusory ground that appears as something other than what it truly is due to ignorance.
2. **Agency vs. non-agency in action**: In *Bhagavad Gītā* 18.14, dualistic and non-Advaitic commentators interpret *adhiṣṭhāna* (the body) as a real constituent of real actions performed by a real individual soul. In Śaṅkara's Advaita commentary, while the body serves as the empirical *adhiṣṭhāna* for action within the realm of ignorance (*vyavahāra*), the true Self (*Ātman*) is entirely non-agent (*akarta*) and actionless (*niṣkriya*), remaining the transcendent witness (*sākṣin*).
3. **Advaita vs. Viśiṣṭādvaita**: While Advaita Vedānta regards *Brahman* as the sole non-dual *adhiṣṭhāna* upon which an unreal world (*mithyā*) is superimposed (*adhyāsa*), Rāmānuja’s Viśiṣṭādvaita rejects the doctrine of illusory superimposition (*anirvacanīya-khyāti*). Viśiṣṭādvaita holds that God is the real *adhiṣṭhāna* (supporter and inner ruler) of real individual souls (*cit*) and a real material world (*acit*), which constitute His body (*śarīra*).
