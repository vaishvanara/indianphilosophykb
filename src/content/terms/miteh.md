**Miteḥ**
Meaning: "from its being a measure"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 11)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

On account of measuring or comprehending; in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad (Mantra 11), state of ablative cause linking makāra ('M') and Prājña to measurement.

---

## Definition

*Miteḥ* (मितेः) is the ablative singular grammatical form of the Sanskrit feminine noun *miti* (मिति), meaning "on account of measuring," "from measurement," "due to cognition," or "by reason of dimension." 

Lexically, the base noun *miti* denotes the act, standard, or process of measuring, estimating, or bounding an entity, as well as correct knowledge or cognition (*pramiti*). 

Contextually within the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (Mantra 11), *miteḥ* functions as an ablative of reason (*hetau pañcamī*), establishing the first of two grounds of comparison (*sāmānya*) between the letter *M* (*makāra*, the third *mātrā* of *AUM*) and *Prājña* (consciousness in deep sleep, *suṣuptasthāna*):

> *suṣuptasthāno prājño makāro tṛtīyā mātrā miter apīter vā minoti ha vā idaṁ sarvam apītiśca bhavati ya evaṁ veda* (Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, 11)

In this passage, *miteḥ* ("on account of measuring") explains why *makāra* corresponds to *Prājña*. Śaṅkarācārya notes in his *Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya* that just as a standard measure (such as a *prastha*) measures grain, *Prājña* measures or bounds the waking (*Viśva*) and dream (*Taijasa*) states during their evolution (*sṛṣṭi*) and involution (*laya*). Similarly, when reciting *AUM*, the sound *M* bounds and measures the preceding vocalic sounds *A* (*akāra*) and *U* (*ukāra*).

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *miteḥ* (मितेः) is an inflected nominal form—specifically the ablative/genitive singular (*pañcamī-ekavacana* / *ṣaṣṭhī-ekavacana*) of the feminine noun stem *miti* (मिति).

1. **Root**: *Mā* (मा, Class 2/3), meaning "to measure," "to mete out," "to mark off," or "to limit."
2. **Nominal Suffixation**: The base noun *miti* is formed by attaching the primary nominal suffix *-ti* (Pāṇinian *ktin*), which denotes an action or abstract quality, to the root *mā-*, with weak-grade vowel shortening of *mā-* to *mi-* (*mā* + *ti* = *miti*, "measuring" or "measurement").
3. **Case Inflection**: In classical Sanskrit grammar (*vyākaraṇa*), the declension of feminine stem nouns ending in short *-i* (*i-kāra-strī-liṅga*) takes the ablative/genitive singular termination *-eḥ* (from Pāṇinian *ṅasi* / *ṅas*), yielding *miteḥ* ("from / of / on account of measurement").

Lexicographical works (such as Macdonell's *Sanskrit-English Dictionary*) record two homonymous roots for *miti*: primary *miti* derived from √*mā* ("measure, weight, value, correct knowledge") and a secondary *miti* derived from √1 *mi* ("setting up, erection"). In the Upaniṣadic context, *miteḥ* strictly derives from √*mā* ("to measure").
