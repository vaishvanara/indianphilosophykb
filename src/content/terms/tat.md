Meaning: "demonstrative pronoun meaning 'that', 'it', or 'this'; used adverbially as 'there' or 'therefore', and in philosophy to denote *Brahman* or Absolute Reality."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 1)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Definition

In standard Sanskrit lexicography and grammar, *tat* (तत्), also occurring as *tad* (तद्), is primarily the nominative and accusative singular neuter form of the third-person demonstrative pronoun *ta*. Literally, it translates to "that", "it", or "this", pointing to an entity, object, or concept that is distant, previously mentioned, or contextualized. In classical usage, it functions both as a demonstrative adjective or noun and as a correlative pronoun paired with relative pronouns such as *yat* or *yaḥ*. It can also be attenuated in certain syntactical contexts to function similarly to a definite article ("the"). 

Adverbially, *tat* serves as an indeclinable particle meaning "there", "thither", "then", "at that time", "in that case", "thereupon", "on that account", "therefore", "consequently", or "thus". In Vedic and classical prose, it frequently acts as a clause-connecting particle of transition ("now" or "with regard to that").

In extended philosophical usage across Indian philosophical traditions, *Tat* designates the Supreme Spirit, the Absolute Reality, or *Brahman*. It signifies the unmanifest, undifferentiated, and ultimate principle of existence beyond finite conceptualization. In Vedantic discourse, *Tat* appears prominently in the sacred Upanishadic formula (*mahāvākya*) *"Tat Tvam Asi"* ("That Thou Art"), which asserts the essential non-difference and identity between the ultimate Absolute (*Tat*) and the individual self (*Tvam*). Additionally, in scriptural contexts such as the *Bhagavad Gītā* (notably in the triple designation *"Om Tat Sat"*), *Tat* represents acts of sacrifice, charity, and penance performed for the Absolute without seeking personal reward. In Sāṃkhya philosophical texts, *tat* is employed referentially in analytical contexts, such as pointing to ritualistic results or established principles (*tattva*).

## Etymology

Grammatically, *tat* (or *tad*) functions as a demonstrative pronoun of the third person. Morphologically, *tad* serves as the underlying pronominal stem or crude base utilized in nominal composition, whereas *ta* represents the base from which inflected gendered forms are constructed, such as *sa* or *saḥ* in the masculine nominative singular, *sā* in the feminine nominative singular, and *tad*/*tat* in the neuter nominative and accusative singular. 

Regarding verbal derivation, standard Sanskrit grammatical sources treat *tad*/*ta* as a primitive pronominal base rather than deriving it from a verbal root (*dhātu*). While ancient etymological tradition (such as the *Uṇādisūtra*) attempted to assign verbal roots to all nominal stems, mainstream grammarians recognize that primary pronominal stems do not possess an established derivation from verbal roots, leaving any verbal root derivation uncertain.

In Sanskrit word formation, *tat* regularly combines as the prior member (*pūrvapada*) in nominal compounds, surfacing as *tat-* before unvoiced consonants (such as in *tat-puruṣa* or *tat-tva*) and as *tad-* before voiced sounds (such as in *tad-anantara* or *tad-ātman*). It also forms derivative adverbs and adjectives through pronominal affixes, including *tatas* (ablative/adverbial suffix *-tas*), *tatra* (locative suffix *-tra*), *tadā* (temporal suffix *-dā*), *tāvat* (quantitative suffix *-vat*), and *tādṛśa* (qualitative suffix *-dṛś*).
