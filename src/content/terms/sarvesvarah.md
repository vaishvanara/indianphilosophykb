**Sarveśvaraḥ**
Meaning: "Lord of All"
From the [Mandukya Upanishad](mandukyopanisat.md) (Verse 6)

सर्वेश्वरः (Sarveśvaraḥ) is a Sanskrit compound word that consists of two parts: सर्व (Sarva) and इश्वर (Iśvara).

सर्व (Sarva) means "all" or "everything", and is often used to describe something that is universal or all-encompassing.

इश्वर (Iśvara) means "lord" or "master", and is often used to describe a being or entity that is considered to be supreme or all-powerful.

So, when combined, सर्वेश्वरः (Sarveśvaraḥ) can be translated to "the lord of all" or "the supreme being".

In Hinduism and other Eastern spiritual traditions, सर्वेश्वरः is often used to describe the ultimate reality or the supreme being, which is considered to be the source of all existence and the ultimate goal of spiritual pursuit.

In a more literal sense, सर्वेश्वरः can be translated to "the lord of all" or "the master of everything", and is often used to describe a being or entity that is considered to be all-powerful and all-knowing.
