Meaning: "this, this here (referring to what is near the speaker, present, or recently mentioned); adverbially: thus, so, here, now, therefore."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verses 1 & 2)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

*Etat* (Sanskrit: एतत्) is the nominative and accusative singular neuter form, as well as the base in composition, of the demonstrative pronoun *etad* (एतद्). In classical and Vedic Sanskrit, it functions as the proximate demonstrative pronoun signifying "this" or "this here", specifically pointing to an object, person, or situation immediately present, near the speaker, or currently in hand (e.g., *eṣa bāṇaḥ*, "this arrow here in my hand"; *eṣa kālaḥ*, "here/now is the time"). 

Grammatical and philosophical traditions distinguish *etat* from other demonstrative pronouns based on spatial and contextual proximity: *etat* indicates what is nearest to the speaker (*sannikṛṣṭa*), whereas *idam* denotes what is near, *adas* refers to what is distant, and *tat* signifies what is absent.

Beyond direct visual or spatial presence, *etat* functions contextually to refer to what has just been stated, preceding occurrences, or recently mentioned topics. When used in contrast with *idam* within a text, *etat* refers back to the preceding rule or statement, while *idam* introduces what follows. However, when linked with a relative pronoun (*yad*), *etat* can also refer forward to an upcoming relative clause. 

Additionally, *etat* is employed emphatically alongside personal pronouns (e.g., *eṣo'ham*, "I, this very person here"). As the grammatical subject of a sentence, *etat* typically agrees in gender and number with the predicate noun, though the neuter singular form *etat* may also remain as an uninflected general subject. In an extended or indeclinable adverbial sense, *etat* means "in this manner", "thus", "so", "here", "at this time", "now", or "accordingly/therefore".

## Etymology

In Sanskrit grammatical taxonomy, *etad* is classified as a nominal stem (*prātipadika*) belonging to the *sarvādi-gaṇa* (the class of pronominal bases headed by *sarva*). Morphologically, the base is formed by compounding the pronominal element *e-* (a pronominal base related to *i*) with the demonstrative stem *tad* / *ta*. It inflects across three genders: the nominative singular masculine is *eṣaḥ* (which drops its final *visarga* before most consonants), the feminine nominative singular is *eṣā*, and the neuter nominative/accusative singular is *etat* or *etad*. In syntactic operations involving subsequent mention (*anvādeśa*), Paninian grammar prescribes that the stem *eta-* optionally undergoes substitution by *ena-* in specific oblique cases (such as the accusative, instrumental singular, and genitive/locative dual).

In traditional Indian linguistic philosophy, following Pāṇini and Gārgya, pronominal bases like *etad* are recognized as primitive, non-derivable nominal stems (*prātipadika*) rather than primary verbal formations (*kṛd-anta*) derived from verbal roots (*dhātu*). From the perspective of comparative Indo-European philology, the pronominal base is cognate with Avestan *aeta*, Old Persian *aita*, Armenian *aid*, and Oscan *eiso*, exhibiting parallel demonstrative structures to Latin *iste*, *ista*, *istud*.
