Meaning: "unspeakable, indescribable, non-verbalizable; that which cannot be designated, specified, or expressed through words."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad(Verse 7)](mandukyopanisat.md)
 
---

## Definition

Lexically, *avyapadeśyam* (Sanskrit: अव्यपदेश्यम्; masculine/base form: *avyapadeśya*) means "unspeakable," "indescribable," "inexpressible," "indesignable," or "non-verbalizable." In ordinary Sanskrit usage, it describes any object, attribute, or state that cannot be pointed out, specified, named, or denoted through language and verbal expressions.

In Indian philosophy, particularly within Advaita Vedānta, *avyapadeśyam* carries a profound technical and doctrinal signification. Extended to metaphysical discourse, it designates the transcendent, unconditioned nature of Ultimate Reality (*Brahman* or *Ātman* in the state of *Turīya*). Because human speech (*vāk*) functions solely through empirical categories, such as genus (*jāti*), quality (*guṇa*), action (*kriyā*), and relation (*sambandha*), the attributeless Absolute transcends all verbalization. Consequently, *avyapadeśyam* defines Reality as inherently beyond positive linguistic description, denoting that It can be indicated only apophatically through negation (*neti neti*, "not this, not this").

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *avyapadeśyam* is an adjective inflected in the nominative/accusative singular neuter form. It is a compound term constructed from the Sanskrit verbal root *diś* (दिश्; "to point out, show, exhibit, or designate"):

1. ***a-* (अ-)**: The negative prefix denoting "not," "un-," or "devoid of."
2. ***vi-* (वि-) + *apa-* (अप-)**: Combined prefixes functioning as intensifiers denoting distinct specification, indication, or pointing away/out.
3. ***diś* (दिश्) + gerundive suffix *-ya* (-य)**: The verb root "to designate" combined with the potential passive participle suffix meaning "capable of being" or "fit to be."

From this root structure derives *apadeśa* ("designation, specification, or statement"), yielding *vyapadeśa* ("distinct denotation or verbal description"). With the negative prefix *a-*, *avyapadeśya* literally signifies "that which is incapable of being distinctly designated, named, or expressed in speech."

## Avyapadeśyam in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

Within the textual tradition of the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad (Verse 7) and Gauḍapāda's *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā* with Śaṅkarācārya's commentary, *avyapadeśyam* functions as a fundamental technical epithet characterizing *Turīya*, meaning the "Fourth" state of pure Consciousness that is the non-dual reality of the Self (*Ātman*).

In Verse 7, the Upaniṣad defines *Turīya* through a rigorous series of negations, setting it apart from the three empirical states of waking (*jāgrat*), dreaming (*svapna*), and deep sleep (*suṣupti*). The text asserts that *Turīya* is "unseen (*adṛṣṭam*), beyond empirical transaction (*avyavahāryam*), ungraspable (*agrāhyam*), uninferable (*alakṣaṇam*), unthinkable (*acintyam*), and indescribable (*avyapadeśyam*)." 

In his *Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya*, Śaṅkarācārya clarifies that *avyapadeśyam* means "that which cannot be denoted by words" (*śabdaiḥ anabhidheyam*). Śaṅkara explains that words can only designate entities that possess specific attributes or limiting adjuncts (*upādhi*). Because *Turīya* is partless (*niṣkala*), birthless (*aja*), and completely free from the manifold universe (*prapañcopaśama*), it has no conventional attributes through which speech can apprehend or articulate it.

Doctrinally, *avyapadeśyam* highlights the epistemological principle of Advaita Vedānta that Ultimate Reality is not an object of cognition or verbalization (*vāk-manas-agocara*). While lower knowledge (*aparā vidyā*) deals with the empirical universe of names and forms (*nāma-rūpa*), higher knowledge (*parā vidyā*) leads to the direct recognition of the Self by removing illusory superimpositions (*adhyāropa-apavāda*). Thus, *avyapadeśyam* emphasizes that *Turīya* is not a conceptual entity to be described, but the self-luminous Subject that illumines all speech and thought.
