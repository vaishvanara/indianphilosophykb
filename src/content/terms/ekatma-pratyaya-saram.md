**Ekātma-Pratyaya-Sāraṃ**
Meaning: "the Essence of the consciousness of Selfhood and Oneness"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 7)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* denotes the non-dual Self whose sole essence is the unwavering conviction or cognition of the one Self.

## Definition

The Sanskrit compound *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* (एकात्मप्रत्ययसारं) is a complex philosophical term composed of four constituent elements: *eka* (one, unique), *ātman* (Self, innermost Being), *pratyaya* (cognition, conviction, notion, or proof), and *sāra* (essence, substance, or core). In general philosophical lexicography, the expression is defined as "the essence as oneness with the Self" or having for its sole essence the conviction of the one Self.

Within its specific textual setting in the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (Mantra 7), *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* functions as a primary positive epithet of *turīya* (the Fourth quarter of the Self). While empirical experience is fragmented across the three relative states of waking (*jāgarita*), dream (*svapna*), and deep sleep (*suṣupti*), *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* specifies that the unconditioned Absolute (*turīya*) is not an empirical object, but is established through the continuous, unbroken awareness of the identity of the single Self (*ekātman*) that witnesses all states. Contextually, the term distinguishes the non-dual Reality (*advaita*) from mere non-existence or voidness, grounding it in self-luminous consciousness.

## Etymology

Grammatically, *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* (एकात्मप्रत्ययसारं) is an adjectival compound (*samāsa*) belonging to the class of *Bahuvrīhi* (possessive or exocentric compounds). In Mantra 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, it is inflected in the nominative/accusative singular neuter form, modifying the neuter nominal concept *caturtham* (the Fourth, *turīya*) or *Ātman*.

The compound is formed by combining four constituent stems:
1. *eka* (एक): A cardinal numeral adjective stem meaning "one", "sole", or "single".
2. *ātman* (आत्मन्): A masculine nominal stem meaning "Self", "soul", or "innermost essence".
3. *pratyaya* (प्रत्यय): A masculine nominal stem derived from the verbal prefix *prati-* (towards) and the root √*i* (to go) with the primary suffix *-aya*, denoting "cognition", "conviction", "notion", "trust", or "valid proof".
4. *sāra* (सार): A nominal stem derived from the root √*sṛ* (to flow), meaning "essence", "substance", "core", "quintessence", or "sole foundation/proof".

Classical exegetical grammarians resolve the compound through a multi-tiered structural expansion (*vigraha-vākya*):
- First, *eka* and *ātman* combine as a *Karmadhāraya* compound: *ekaś cāsau ātmā ca = ekātmā* ("the one Self").
- Second, *ekātman* combines with *pratyaya* as a Genitive *Tatpuruṣa* compound: *ekātmanaḥ pratyayaḥ = ekātma-pratyayaḥ* ("the cognition or conviction of the one Self").
- Third, *ekātma-pratyaya* combines with *sāra* to form the ultimate *Bahuvrīhi* compound: *ekātma-pratyayaḥ sāraḥ pramāṇaṃ yasya tat = ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* ("that entity whose sole essence or valid proof is the conviction of the one Self").

Śaṅkarācārya formulates this grammatical resolution in his commentary on Mantra 7 by glossing the expression as *ekātma-pratyaya-samavedyam* ("that which is to be known solely through the cognition of the one Self") or as *ekātma-pratyayaḥ sāraḥ pramāṇaṃ yasyātmano dhyāne tat*.

## ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

The compound *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* occurs in Mantra 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, a principal Upaniṣad attached to the *Atharva Veda*.

Grammatically, *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* functions as an adjectival modifier in the nominative singular neuter, aligned with a series of parallel epithets defining the Fourth quarter (*caturthaḥ pādaḥ* or *turīya*): *prapañcopaśamaṃ* (the cessation of phenomena), *śāntaṃ* (peaceful), *śivam* (blissful/auspicious), and *advaitaṃ* (non-dual).

The surrounding text of Mantra 7 articulates a famous apophatic formulation (*neti neti*) that systematically negates the cognitive conditions of the three preceding quarters (*pādas*):
- *nāntaḥ-prajñam* ("not conscious of internal objects") negates *taijasa*, the dream state (*svapna-sthāna*).
- *na bahiṣ-prajñam* ("not conscious of external objects") negates *vaiśvānara*, the waking state (*jāgarita-sthāna*).
- *nobhayataḥ-prajñam* ("not conscious of both internal and external objects") negates any intermediate state between dream and waking.
- *na prajñāna-ghanam* ("not a mass of undifferentiated consciousness") negates *prājña*, the deep sleep state (*suṣupti*).
- *na prajñaṃ nāprajñam* ("neither simple cognition nor non-cognition") negates simultaneous omniscience and insentiency.
- *adṛṣṭam avyavahāryam agrāhyam alakṣanam acintyam avyapadeśyam* ("unseen, unrelatable, ungraspable, inferenceless, unthinkable, indescribable") negates all empirical means of knowledge (*pramāṇas*).

Amidst these extensive negations, *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* serves as a crucial positive marker. It clarifies that *turīya* is not an empty void (*śūnya*), but is positively known through the intuitive conviction of the one Self.

Textual and Exegetical Distinctions:
- **Upaniṣad Wording vs. Gauḍapāda's Kārikā**: The Upaniṣad text introduces *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* as part of its core definition of *turīya*. Gauḍapāda, in his *Kārikā* (I.10–I.18, I.29; III.11–III.35), elaborates on the philosophical implications of this phrase, explaining that while *prājña* in deep sleep is bound by cause (*kāraṇa-baddha*, non-apprehension of reality), *turīya* transcends both cause (non-apprehension) and effect (misapprehension). Gauḍapāda notes that when the illusion of duality is dispelled through the conviction of the one Self (*ekātma-pratyaya*), the phenomenal world (*prapañca*) naturally ceases.
- **Śaṅkara's Commentary**: Śaṅkarācārya emphasizes that *ekātma-pratyaya* represents the unbroken thread of Self-awareness ("I am") that persists unchanged across waking, dream, and sleep states. By analyzing *sāra* as "sole proof" (*pramāṇa*), Śaṅkara demonstrates that *turīya* requires no external empirical evidence; rather, self-luminosity (*svayaṃ-prakāśatva*) and self-conviction (*ekātma-pratyaya*) constitute its definitive proof.

## Primary Texts and Commentaries

The primary textual locator for *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* is Mantra 7 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*:

> **Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, Mantra 7**:
> *nāntaḥ-prajñaṃ na bahiṣ-prajñaṃ nobhayataḥ-prajñaṃ na prajñāna-ghanaṃ na prajñaṃ nāprajñam / adṛṣṭam avyavahāryam agrāhyam alakṣanam acintyam avyapadeśyam ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ prapañcopaśamaṃ śāntaṃ śivam advaitaṃ caturthaṃ manyante sa ātmā sa vijñeyaḥ // 7 //*

Key commentaries and treatises clarifying the expression include:
- **Śaṅkarācārya's *Māṇḍūkyopaniṣad-bhāṣya*** (on Mantra 7): Glosses *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* as *ekātma-pratyaya-samavedyam* and *ekātma-pratyayaḥ sāraḥ pramāṇaṃ yasya*, affirming that the non-dual Self is known through the conviction of one's own inner Self.
- **Ānandagiri's *Ṭīkā*** on Śaṅkara's commentary: Explains that *ekātma-pratyaya* serves as the effective means (*pramāṇa*) for eliminating superimposition (*adhyāsa*) and realizing the non-relational character of *turīya*.
- **Sadānanda's *Vedāntasāra*** (§ 196): Cites classical texts affirming the unattached, non-dual, self-luminous nature of the Self (*ekaṃ kevalaṃ*) that corresponds to the state described as *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ*.
- **Gauḍapāda's *Māṇḍūkya-kārikā*** (I.7, I.10, I.12, I.29): Systematizes the non-dual doctrine of *turīya*, establishing that the cessation of duality occurs upon realizing the oneness of the Self.

## Interpretations and Disputes

Differences in translation, grammatical parsing, and philosophical interpretation of *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* are documented across classical commentaries and modern scholarship:

1. **Lexicographical and Translational Variations**:
   - Arthur Anthony Macdonell parses the constituent stems (*eka*, *ātman*, *pratyaya*, *sāra*) in standard dictionary terms, translating compound structures with *sāra* as "whose sole nature or essence is...".
   - John Grimes (*A Concise Dictionary of Indian Philosophy*) defines *Ekātma-pratyayasāra* as "the essence as oneness with the Self".
   - Swami Nikhilananda renders the phrase as "that which has for its sole proof the conviction of the one Self" or "whose sole essence is the consciousness of the one Self".

2. **Dual Meaning of *Sāra* (Essence vs. Proof)**:
   - *Sāra as Essence (Svarūpa)*: One classical interpretive line takes *sāra* to mean essential nature (*svarūpa*). Under this rendering, *turīya* is defined as that state whose intrinsic nature is the undivided cognition of the single Self.
   - *Sāra as Proof (Pramāṇa)*: Śaṅkarācārya provides an alternative gloss taking *sāra* as valid means of knowledge or proof (*pramāṇa*). Because *turīya* is non-empirical (*adṛṣṭam*, *avyavahāryam*), it cannot be established by sensory perception or logical inference; its sole proof (*sāra*) is the direct, intuitive conviction (*pratyaya*) of the one Self.

3. **Refutation of Nihilistic (Śūnyavāda) Readings**:
   - Because Mantra 7 contains a long series of negative qualifiers (*adṛṣṭam*, *agrāhyam*, *acintyam*), certain opponent schools argued that the Upaniṣad describes absolute non-existence or voidness (*śūnya*).
   - Advaita Vedānta commentators explicitly cite *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* to refute this view. Śaṅkara and Gauḍapāda demonstrate that negation applies only to superimposed empirical attributes (*upādhis*); *ekātma-pratyaya-sāraṃ* positively asserts the underlying, self-luminous Subject (*Pratyagātman*) as the undeniable foundation of experience.
