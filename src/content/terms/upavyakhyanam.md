Meaning: "explanation; clear explanation; detailed exposition."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 1)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In the verse 1 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the Sanskrit term *upavyākhyānaṃ* (appearing in euphonic combination as *tasyopavyākhyānam*) denotes a "clear explanation," "exposition," or "thorough commentary". In his authoritative commentary on this verse, Śaṅkarācārya explicitly defines *upavyākhyānam* as a lucid explanation (*spṣṭīkaraṇam* / *prastutam*), observing that the *Upaniṣad* opens with an exposition of the imperishable syllable *AUM**(*Om*) because *AUM* serves as the direct spiritual means to the knowledge of *Brahman* due to its supreme proximity to the Absolute. 

Beyond its general lexical sense of an explanatory discourse or commentary, the term functions in the context of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* as a specific designation for the systematic correlation of sound and reality. Figuratively and doctrinally, this *upavyākhyānaṃ* extends the literal meaning of commentary into a meditative analysis: it maps the three phonetic constituents of *AUM*—*A*, *U*, and *M*, along with the soundless measure (*amātra*)—to the three manifested states of consciousness (waking / *Viśva*, dream / *Taijasa*, and deep sleep / *Prājña*) and the ultimate unconditioned Reality (transcendental / *Turīya*).

## Etymology

Grammatically, *upavyākhyānaṃ* is a neuter noun in the nominative singular. Its morphological structure is derived from a compound prefixing of two verbal prefixes attached to a primary Sanskrit root, followed by a primary nominal suffix:

- **Prefixes:** The primary prefix *upa-* functions as a prepositional prefix denoting "nearness," "towards," "proximity," or "secondary/subordinate". Śaṅkarācārya highlights this sense of nearness by noting that *AUM* is in the closest proximity (*sannikṛṣṭa*) to *Brahman*. The secondary prefix *vi-* denotes "apart," "distinctly," or "in detail".
- **Root:** The core verbal root is *khyā* (Class II P.), which means "to declare," "to make known," "to recite," or "to speak". Combined with *vi-* and *ā-*, the verbal stem *vyākhyā* means "to explain, discuss, or elucidate".
- **Nominal suffix:** The primary nominal suffix *-ana* (Kṛt suffix / *lyuṭ*) is added to the verbal stem to form neuter abstract nouns of action or instrument, yielding the sense of an act of explaining or an exposition.

The combined compound formation *upavyākhyāna* literally signifies a "proximate or detailed exposition". While the component prefixes, root, and nominal suffix are fully established in standard Sanskrit grammar and lexicography, the full compound *upavyākhyānaṃ* as a single headword is understood through established rules of prefix combination rather than an isolated verbal root.
