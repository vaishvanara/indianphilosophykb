**Karuṇā** 
Meaning: “compassion”
Definition: A type of meditation in Buddhism: One should sympathize with the sorrows of one's friends and foes alike. An inherent principle embraced and protected by all Buddhas and Bodhisattvas.

Karuṇā is the sublime virtue of compassion, defined as a heartfelt, empathetic response to the pain, grief, and suffering of others. Derived from roots implying action, true karuṇā is far more than passive pity or sentimental sorrow; it is an active movement of the heart that seeks to alleviate distress. When a person embodies karuṇā, the suffering of another is felt as a resonant vibration within one's own awareness, dissolving the illusion of absolute separation between individuals. It serves as the direct psychological antidote to cruelty, callousness, and hardness of heart. In spiritual traditions like Buddhism and Yoga, karuṇā drives aspirants and bodhisattvas to dedicate their energy to the upliftment and liberation of all sentient beings. Meditating on compassion expands emotional capacity, softens rigid ego boundaries, and transmutes personal grief into a universal healing force, fostering profound grace and a deep sense of shared human vulnerability.
