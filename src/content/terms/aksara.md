Meaning: "imperishable, indestructible, or immutable; a letter, phoneme, or syllable (notably *AUM*); Supreme Reality (*Brahman*) transcending change."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 1, Verse 8)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In ordinary, phonological, and lexical usage, *akṣara* (Sanskrit: अक्षर) denotes a letter of the alphabet, a vocalic sound, or a syllable. In Vedic and classical Sanskrit grammar, it designates an elemental, indivisible unit of spoken language, most prominently the sacred monosyllable *AUM* (*OM*).

In its extended philosophical sense across the Upanishads and Vedantic literature, *akṣara* refers to the "Imperishable" or "Indestructible"—the immutable, uncaused Supreme Reality (*Brahman*) that transcends the world of flux (*kṣara*). The term thus encompasses both a linguistic-symbolic dimension (the phonetic representation of the Absolute through *AUM*) and a metaphysical dimension (the transcendent, partless, and attributeless Ultimate Reality). 

Secondarily, in specific cosmological contexts, *akṣara* is also used to designate the unmanifest causal state of matter (*avyakta* or *māyā*).

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *akṣara* is a Sanskrit masculine and neuter noun or adjective formed from the negative prefix *a-* ("not," "without") and the verbal root *kṣi* or *kṣar* ("to perish," "to decay," "to stream away," "to flow"). Literally signifying "that which does not decay" or "indestructible," the term designates that which is permanent and immune to physical or temporal dissolution.

Grammatically, when applied to speech, *akṣara* denotes a syllable or phoneme because a vocalic sound is viewed as an unalterable sound-unit. When applied metaphysically, it functions as a noun designating the Immutable Absolute (*Brahman*) or as an adjective describing the eternal, changeless nature of Pure Consciousness.

## Akṣara in Advaita Vedānta

In Advaita Vedānta, *akṣara* plays a fundamental role in both scriptural exegesis (*mīmāṁsā*) and contemplative practice (*upāsanā* / *jñāna*), operating simultaneously as the primary verbal symbol of the Absolute and as the Absolute itself.

### Symbolic and meditative dimension (*Praṇava*)

As the sacred syllable *AUM* (*OM* or *Praṇava*), *akṣara* serves as the supreme sound-support (*ālambana*) for spiritual contemplation. In texts like the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *Kaṭha Upaniṣad* (1.2.15–17), and *Chāndogya Upaniṣad* (1.1.1), *AUM* as *akṣara* is identified with all time (past, present, future) and that which transcends time. Advaita Vedānta explains that meditating on *akṣara* enables the aspirant to progress from gross manifestations to the soundless, non-dual Reality (*Turīya* / *Amātrā*).

### Metaphysical dimension and exegesis

In the exegesis of major Upanishadic texts, Śaṅkarācārya and the Advaita tradition establish *akṣara* as a technical designation for *Nirguṇa Brahman* (attributeless Absolute):

1. ***Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad* (3.8.7–11):** In response to Gārgī, Yājñavalkya declares *Akṣara* as the ultimate principle in which unmanifest space (*ākāśa*) is woven like warp and woof. In his *Brahmasūtrabhāṣya* (1.3.10–12, *Akṣarādhikaraṇa*), Śaṅkara demonstrates that *Akṣara* here cannot mean a mere linguistic syllable or unmanifest matter (*pradhāna*), because it is described as the ruler that holds the sun, moon, heaven, and earth in their positions and as the ultimate witness—unseen but seeing, unheard but hearing.
2. ***Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad* (1.1.5–6, 2.1.2):** Higher knowledge (*parā vidyā*) is defined as that by which the Imperishable (*Akṣara*) is comprehended. This *Akṣara* is described apophatically: imperceivable, ungraspable, without family or caste, without eyes or ears, eternal, all-pervading, and extremely subtle. Where the text speaks of *Puruṣa* as "higher than the high Imperishable" (*akṣarāt parataḥ paraḥ*), Advaita commentary clarifies that the lower *akṣara* refers to the unmanifest seed-state of name and form (*avyakta* / *māyā*), whereas the higher *Akṣara* / *Puruṣa* is the transcendent Self.
3. ***Bhagavad Gītā* (8.3, 15.16–17):** The text explicitly equates *akṣara* with Supreme *Brahman* (*akṣaraṁ brahma paramam*). In Chapter 15, a distinction is drawn between the perishable (*kṣara*, the entire realm of changing effects) and the imperishable (*akṣara*, the changeless, *kūṭastha*), both of which are transcended by the Supreme Self (*Uttama Puruṣa* / *Paramātman*).

### Synthesis of negative attributes (*akṣarādhy-upasaṁhāra*)

In *Brahmasūtra* (3.3.33), Advaita Vedānta establishes that all negative descriptions of *akṣara* across different Upanishads, such as "neither gross nor subtle" (*asthūlam anaṇu*), must be combined in meditations on the Absolute, as the non-dual object (*Brahman*) behind all such apophatic teachings is identical.
