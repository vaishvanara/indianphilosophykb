Tvak is the sense organ of touch, governing tactile perception, texture, temperature, pressure, and pain. It is aligned with the subtle element of touch (sparśa) and corresponds to the gross element of air (vāyu), which governs movement and contact throughout the cosmos. As the body's primary protective boundary, Tvak provides immediate feedback about the external environment, mediating the physical interaction between the self and the material world. It plays a foundational role in human experiences of comfort, warmth, and physical intimacy. In yogic psychology, because the skin feels constant contact with external stimuli, it can easily tether the mind to sensory indulgence (māyā). Mastering this sense through detachment is critical for achieving sensory withdrawal (pratyāhāra) and emotional equilibrium.
