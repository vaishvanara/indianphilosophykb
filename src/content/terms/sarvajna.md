**Sarvajña**
Meaning: "all-knowing"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 6)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

Meaning: "All-knowing; omniscient; the knower of all beings and states."

---

## Definition

Lexically, *sarvajña* functions as an adjective meaning "all-knowing" or "omniscient," designating an individual or entity endowed with universal knowledge. In classical Sanskrit literature, the word is used both of deities and of mortals, such as scholars, ministers, and philosophers who possess comprehensive erudition.

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the term occurs in mantra 6 (*eṣa sarveśvara eṣa sarvajña eṣo 'ntaryāmy eṣa yoniḥ sarvasya...*) as an attribute of the third quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), termed *Prājña*. In the non-dualistic (*Advaita*) framework of the text, *Prājña* represents consciousness associated with deep dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*), which is macrocosmically identical with *Īśvara*, the unmanifested causal source and sovereign ruler of the universe. 

Philosophically, Advaita exegesis distinguishes the literal meaning from its metaphysical application:

1. **Literal meaning:** An entity that knows all things in their totality.
2. **Metaphysical meaning in *Māṇḍūkya* 6:** In his commentary on mantra 6, Śaṅkarācārya defines *sarvajña* as the "knower of all beings in their different conditions". Rather than positing an extra-cosmic creator who retains discrete intellectual items of empirical information, the commentary explains that *Ātman* is the unified witness (*sākṣin*) of the three temporal divisions (past, present, future) and the three experiential states (waking, dreaming, and deep sleep). Because awareness of these states depends on this foundational conscious principle, *Prājña*/*Īśvara* is designated as the universal knower.
3. **Extended epistemic meaning in the *Kārikā*:** In Gauḍapāda's *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā* (IV.85) and Śaṅkara's commentary thereon, the qualification of omniscience (*sarvajñatā*) is extended to the sage (*brāhmaṇa*) who realizes the non-dual Self. Because *Ātman* constitutes all that truly exists, whoever realizes the non-dual reality knows all, leaving no separate object to be known.

## Etymology

* **Part of speech:** Adjective (frequently used substantively in the masculine as an epithet or proper name).
* **Compound classification:** An *upapada-tatpuruṣa* (an accusatively dependent determinative compound with an integrated verbal member).
* **Constituent members:**
  * *Pūrvapada* (first member / *upapada*): The pronominal adjective base *sarva* (all, whole, entire).
  * *Uttarapada* (second member): The bound agentive root-noun *-jña* (knowing, knower).
* **Root and affix derivation:** Derived from the primary Sanskrit verbal root *jñā* (Class 9 *jānā́-* / *-nī-*, "to know, perceive, ascertain, recognize"). Under classical grammatical rules governing roots ending in long *-ā* compounded with an antecedent governing nominal element (*upapada*), the primary suffix (*kṛt pratyaya*) *-ka* is affixed to the root, eliding the radical vowel *-ā* and leaving the agentive terminal *-jña*.
* **Grammatical resolution (*vigraha*):** *sarvaṃ jānāti iti sarvajñaḥ* ("one who knows all").
* **Derivative abstracts:** Lexical sources establish the derived abstract feminine noun *sarvajñatā* and the neuter nouns *sarvajñatva* and *sārvajñya*, all signifying "omniscience".
