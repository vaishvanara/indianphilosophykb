Meaning: "all-knowing; omniscient; the knower of all beings and states."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 6)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

Lexically, *sarvajña* (Sanskrit: सर्वज्ञ) functions as an adjective meaning "all-knowing" or "omniscient," designating an individual or entity endowed with universal knowledge. In classical Sanskrit literature, the word is used both of deities and of mortals, such as scholars, ministers, and philosophers who possess comprehensive erudition.

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the term occurs in verse 6 (*eṣa sarveśvara eṣa sarvajña eṣo 'ntaryāmy eṣa yoniḥ sarvasya...*) as an attribute of the third quarter (*pāda*) of the Self (*Ātman*), termed *Prājña*. In the non-dualistic (*Advaita*) framework of the text, *Prājña* represents consciousness associated with deep dreamless sleep (*suṣupti*), which is macrocosmically identical with *Īśvara*, the unmanifested causal source and sovereign ruler of the universe. 

Philosophically, Advaita exegesis distinguishes the literal meaning from its metaphysical application:

1. **Literal meaning:** An entity that knows all things in their totality.
2. **Metaphysical meaning in *Māṇḍūkya* 6:** In his commentary on verse 6, Śaṅkarācārya defines *sarvajña* as the "knower of all beings in their different conditions". Rather than positing an extra-cosmic creator who retains discrete intellectual items of empirical information, the commentary explains that *Ātman* is the unified witness (*sākṣin*) of the three temporal divisions (past, present, future) and the three experiential states (waking, dreaming, and deep sleep). Because awareness of these states depends on this foundational conscious principle, *Prājña*/*Īśvara* is designated as the universal knower.
3. **Extended epistemic meaning in the *Kārikā*:** In Gauḍapāda's *Māṇḍūkya Kārikā* (IV.85) and Śaṅkara's commentary thereon, the qualification of omniscience (*sarvajñatā*) is extended to the sage (*brāhmaṇa*) who realizes the non-dual Self. Because *Ātman* constitutes all that truly exists, whoever realizes the non-dual reality knows all, leaving no separate object to be known.

## Etymology

Grammatically, *sarvajña* functions as an adjective that is frequently employed substantively in the masculine as an epithet or proper name. It is classified as an *upapada-tatpuruṣa*—an accusatively dependent determinative compound with an integrated verbal member. Its constituent parts comprise the *pūrvapada* (first member or *upapada*), which is the pronominal adjective base *sarva* ("all, whole, entire"), and the *uttarapada* (second member), the bound agentive root-noun *-jña* ("knowing, knower"). 

Derivationally, the terminal element originates from the primary Sanskrit verbal root *jñā* (Class 9 *jānā́-* / *-nī-*, "to know, perceive, ascertain, recognize"). In accordance with classical grammatical rules governing roots ending in long *-ā* compounded with an antecedent governing nominal element (*upapada*), the primary suffix (*kṛt pratyaya*) *-ka* is affixed to the root; this elides the radical vowel *-ā*, yielding the agentive ending *-jña*. The compound’s formal grammatical resolution (*vigraha*) is thus formulated as *sarvaṃ jānāti iti sarvajñaḥ* ("one who knows all"). In addition, lexical sources record several derived abstract nominal forms from this stem, including the feminine *sarvajñatā* and the neuters *sarvajñatva* and *sārvajñya*, all of which denote "omniscience."
