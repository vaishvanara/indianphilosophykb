Meaning: "being, present, or that which exists now; in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad, the present moment within the threefold division of time (trikāla)."

See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 1)](mandukyopanisat.md)

---

## Definition

In ordinary Sanskrit lexicography and grammar, *bhavat* (Sanskrit: भवत्, neuter form: भवत्, in sandhi *bhavad*) is the present active participle of the verbal root *bhū* (भू, "to be," "to become," or "to exist"). Lexically, it signifies "being," "existing," "present," "that which is becoming," or "the present time."

In its extended philosophical usage, specifically within Upaniṣadic temporal cosmology, *bhavat* serves as a technical designation for the present aspect of time (*vartamāna-kāla*). It is positioned between *bhūtam* ("that which has been" or the past) and *bhaviṣyat* ("that which will be" or the future), forming the triad of empirical time (*trikāla*).

---

## Etymology

Etymologically, *bhavat* is a participle formed according to classical Sanskrit morphology:

1. **Verbal root:** Derived from the primary verbal root *bhū* (भू), meaning "to be," "to exist," or "to come into being."
2. **Grammatical affix:** Formed by adding the present active participial suffix *-at* (*śatṛ-pratyaya*) to the modified verbal stem *bhava-* (*bhū* + *a* + *at* = *bhavat*).
3. **Part of speech and declension:** Functions grammatically as a present participle, adjectival modifier, or neuter nominalized noun. In the nominative and accusative singular neuter, the base form is *bhavat* (भवत्).
4. **Phonetic sandhi:** In verse 1 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, before the voiced dental consonant *bh-* in *bhaviṣyat*, the final voiceless dental stop *-t* undergoes consonantal sandhi (*jaśtva*), converting to its corresponding voiced stop *-d-*, yielding the combined textual reading *bhūtaṁ bhavad bhaviṣyat*.

## Bhavat in the Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (verse 1) and its classical commentary tradition (*Gauḍapāda Kārikā* and Śaṅkarācārya's *Bhāṣya*), *bhavat* plays an essential role in the exposition of *AUM* (*OM*) as the comprehensive sound-symbol of all manifest and unmanifest reality.

### Temporal triad (trikāla) and sound-identification

Verse 1 states: *bhūtaṁ bhavad bhaviṣyad iti sarvam oṁkāra eva* ("All that is past, present, and future is indeed AUM"). Within this formula:

- *Bhūtam* represents the past.
- *Bhavat* represents the present.
- *Bhaviṣyat* represents the future.

Advaita Vedānta commentary explains that time (*kāla*) is a conceptual construct that structures phenomenal experience. By identifying *bhavat* (the present) alongside the past and future with *AUM*, the Upaniṣad asserts that all temporal experience and everything conditioned by time is nothing other than *AUM*.

### Phenomenal manifestation and transcendence

1. **Correlation with quarters (*pāda*):** In the overall scheme of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the three temporal dimensions (*bhūtaṁ bhavad bhaviṣyat*) correspond to the three sound-measures (*mātrā*) of *AUM* (*akāra*, *ukāra*, *makāra*) and their corresponding quarters (*pāda*) of Consciousness: the waking state (*jāgrat* / *Viśva*), dream state (*svapna* / *Taijasa*), and deep sleep state (*suṣupti* / *Prājña*).
2. **Transcendence (*trikālātīta*):** Immediately following the statement containing *bhavat*, verse 1 adds: *yac cānyat trikālātītaṁ tad dhy oṁkāra eva* ("And whatever else there is beyond the threefold division of time—that also is truly AUM"). Thus, while *bhavat* belongs to the empirical realm of time (*trikāla* / *vyavahāra*), both the temporal triad (*bhūtaṁ bhavad bhaviṣyat*) and the transcendent non-dual Reality (*Turīya* / *Amātrā*, which transcends time) are shown to be unified in *AUM*.
