**Ekī-Bhūtaḥ**
Meaning: "unified experience"
See: [Mandukya Upanishad (Verse 5)](mandukyopanisat.md)

## Meaning

In the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, *ekī-bhūtaḥ* means "become one" or "unified," denoting the state of deep sleep where manifold experiences merge into non-differentiation.

---

## Definition

Lexically, *ekī-bhūta* (एकीभूत) is the past passive participle of the derivative verbal stem *ekī-bhū* (to become one, to unite, to coalesce, or to merge into unity). In general Sanskrit lexicography, it signifies "become one," "amalgamated," "blended," or "integrated into a single whole". In broader Indian philosophical literature, its abstract nominal form *ekī-bhāva* denotes oneness, complete integration, or union with the Absolute.

In the specific textual setting of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad* (Mantra 5), *ekī-bhūtaḥ* occurs in the nominative singular masculine form as a technical epithet of *prājña*—the third quarter (*tṛtīyaḥ pādaḥ*) of the Self (*Ātman*) presiding over the deep sleep state (*suṣuptasthāna*).

Literally translating as "having become one" or "unified," the term denotes a profound state of non-differentiation. Figuratively and exegetically, *ekī-bhūtaḥ* describes the condition wherein the manifold dualistic cognitions, sensory perceptions, and mental projections of the waking (*jāgarita*) and dream (*svapna*) states are temporarily re-absorbed and merged into a single, uniform mass of undifferentiated consciousness (*prajñāna-ghana*). Classical Vedāntic commentators liken this state to the way distinct objects during the day become imperceptible and unified under the darkness of night, remaining in seed form until awakening.

---

## Etymology

Grammatically, *ekī-bhūtaḥ* (एकीभूतः) is a participial adjective formed by adding the past passive participial suffix *-ta* (*Kta-pratyaya*) to the derivative verbal stem *ekī-bhū*.

The expression is derived from two primary elements:
1. *eka* (एक): A cardinal numeral stem meaning "one," "single," or "sole".
2. √*bhū* (भू): A primary verbal root belonging to the first conjugational class (*bhvāditva*), meaning "to be," "to become," or "to arise".

The formation of the stem *ekī-bhū* involves the grammatical operation of the *Cvi* suffix (*Cvi-pratyaya*), governed by Pāṇinian rules of derivation for expressions indicating something becoming what it previously was not (*abhūta-tad-bhāva*). Under *Cvi* derivation, the final short vowel *-a* of the nominal stem *eka* is lengthened to *-ī-* before combining with forms of the root √*bhū*, yielding the derivative stem *ekī-bhū* ("to become one" or "to undergo unification").

In its specific contextual occurrence in Mantra 5 of the *Māṇḍūkya Upaniṣad*, the word is inflected in the nominative singular masculine form *ekī-bhūtaḥ*, agreeing in gender, number, and case with the masculine noun *prājñaḥ*.
